Telephone Messages
Objectives
take and leave messages
state and confirm important details

I

n today's busy world, it can be difficult to reach people
at your convenience. Therefore, you rely on messages to
communicate important information. Knowing specific
phrases and observing basic message etiquette can ensure
that your message is received as well as understood.
Be brief and get to the point. Try to limit your message to 15 seconds. If necessary, prepare yourself
ahead of making the call so that you know exactly
what to say.
Speak clearly and slowly, especially when giving important details, such as phone and account numbers,

Phrases
Giving Details over the Phone
Telephone messages contain important information that
must be clearly stated so that the person on the receiving
end knows exactly whom to contact and how to get in
touch.

Numbers
North American phone numbers contain ten digits: for example, 604-659-3773. The first three are the area code,
with the last seven belonging to an individual residence or
office. When making an international call, you need to include the particular country code.
Companies and organizations have what are called
1-800, or toll-free numbers, that you can call without having to pay long-distance charges.
When giving someone a phone number, say the numbers
individually (6-0-4-3-5-6-8-2-1-4) or the last four in pairs
(6-0-4-3-5-6-82-14).
For repeated numbers, like 33, one can say double-three
(or thirty-three); for 888, triple-eight.

--- Page Break ---

The number 0 is read as zero or the letter O. With phone
numbers, it doesn't matter which one you use, but if you're
stating a postal code consisting of numbers and letters, it is
necessary to differentiate between the two. For example,
V0R, is read as V-zero-R, and 1O5 as 1-oh-5.
When giving an international number, you should state
the country and city codes and phone number in a manner
that is easy to write down or record as well as to understand. For example, 49 911/55 29 16: forty-nine, nine-oneone, fifty-five, twenty-nine, sixteen.
Account numbers are usually given number by number.
In the case of a long account number, you should speak
slowly or, if you are taking down someone's account number, repeat each number as the caller gives it.

Addresses
When giving someone an address over the phone, you can
break the numbers into groups of ones and tens, rather
than saying them as a complete number.
Examples:
683 Hollywood Crescent: six, eighty-three Hollywood
Crescent (rather than six-hundred-and-eighty-three)
1142 Rose Street: eleven, forty-two Rose Street
426 964 Heywood Avenue: suite / apartment / unit
number four, twenty-six, nine, sixty-four Heywood
Avenue

Spelling
Because similar sounding or rhyming letters, such as F and
S, or D and T, can be misunderstood over the phone, you
can use the International Telecommunications Union
Standard Phonetic Alphabet when you spell out a name or
word.
Example:
Harper: H for Hotel, A for Alpha, R for Romeo, P for
Papa, E for Echo, R for Romeo

Leaving a Message
A telephone message should include:
your name and company or department
the time and date of your call
your reason for calling
your phone number and other contact information,
such as an e-mail address, and your location (e.g., a
hotel room number) if you're traveling

Asking Someone to Take a Message

Stating Importance
It's urgent / of utmost importance.
It's an emergency situation.
I need to get this information to him right away.
We're on a tight schedule.
It can't wait.

Taking a Message
When you take a message for a colleague or friend over the
phone, you need to let the caller know why the person to
whom they wish to speak is unavailable. You also need to
ask:
why the person they wish to speak to is unavailable
what the call's about
what specific information the caller wants to pass on

--- Page Break ---

When the caller gives you a number or spells a name, you
should repeat the information to assure the caller that you
have taken it down correctly.

Stating Unavailability
I'm sorry, but John's not available / free at the
moment.
I'm afraid Frieda won't be in until tomorrow
morning.
Marin's tied up / out of town at the moment.

Asking to Take a Message
Would you like me to give him a message?
If you'll give me your name and number, I'll have
someone call you back / return your call.
Can / shall I take a message?
Is there something I can pass on to her?

Giving Assurance
When you take a message, it's important to let the caller
know that his message will be passed on. Note the use of
the future with "will."
I'll give him your message.

--- Page Break ---

I'll pass that on to her.
I'll see to it right away.
I'll let him know as soon as he gets back.
I'll have her call you back / return your call right away /
as soon as possible.
I'll see what I can do.
I'll do my best.
Will do.

Declining to Leave a Message
If you're calling about a personal matter or it may be difficult for someone to reach you, you can decline to leave a
message.
Thanks, but I'll call / check back later.
I'd prefer to try again another time.
I'll try later.

Offering Additional Help
Do you need anything else / further help?
Is there anything else I can help you with today?

--- Page Break ---

Ensuring Communication
When words fail you in a face-to-face conversation, gestures come to the rescue. Not so during telephone calls,
where you need to let the person on the other end know
that you're listening and that you understand him or her. In
this case, a few little words can make a big difference.
Yes.
Right.
Okay.
I see / understand.
Go on.
(

For more phrases, see Discourse Markers, page 30.)

Asking for Clarification
Would / do you mind repeating that / going over that
once more?
Could you walk me through that again?
Could you repeat that, please?
Can you confirm / double-check / go over my
directions?
I'm sorry, I didn't catch your name.
I'm sorry, what was your name again?

Confirming Details
I'll repeat your e-mail address to verify it.
I'll read that back to you.
Let me see if I've got that right.

Asking for Additional Contact Information
Does Eriko have your cell phone number / e-mail
address?
Can we reach you at another number?
How can James get ahold of you in case there's a
change in schedule?
Is there anyone else we can call if there's a problem?

Returning a Call
When you've received a message, the next step is to call
back as soon as possible.
I'm calling in response to the message you left for
me this morning.

Hello, I'm following up on your message of July
10th at 9:05.
This is Silvia calling back / returning your call.
Hello, this is Jack. You called?
Hi, I got your message.

Answering Machines, Voice Mail,
and Text Messages
A busy schedule doesn't always allow you to leave or take
messages in person, in which case technology comes in
handy. When you're recording a message on a private answering machine or voice mail, it's not a good idea to let
strangers know that you're away from home. The following
are examples only.

Private Recordings
You've reached 248-5792. Please leave a message
after the tone / beep.
Hello, this is Carly. Sorry, but I can't make it to the
phone right now. Please leave a message and I'll get
back to you as soon as possible.
