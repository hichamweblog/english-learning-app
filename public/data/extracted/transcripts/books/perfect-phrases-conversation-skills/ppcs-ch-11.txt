When Thao and Duong Tran immigrated to America, they
dreamed of starting up their own Vietnamese restaurant.
Running a restaurant, however, will require them to conduct much of their business over the telephone, and Thao
feels somewhat intimidated by the prospect of dealing
with people she cannot see. Will she know what to say?
And what if the person she's dealing with doesn't understand her? Or she doesn't understand them? How will she
get the services or supplies she needs?

ow that the telephone has developed from rotary
dial to cellular and smart phones, with almost as
many mobile phone subscribers as there are people
on Earth, the telephone has become indispensable to personal as well as business communication.
In terms of content, telephone conversations do not differ greatly from face-to-face conversations. There are,
however, specific phrases used in telephone calls, particularly in formal business situations. What you say and how
you say it are important not only in making a good impression, but in getting your message across.

Phrases
Making a Call
In English-speaking cultures, it is customary for people to
identify themselves on the telephone with their first and
last names. Very rarely do people refer to themselves as
Mr., Mrs., Ms., and so on. (
See Introducing Yourself,
page 9.)
In a formal business situation, you should state both
your name and the name of your company, organization, or
department. If you are unknown to the person whom you
are calling, it's a good idea to give your last name as well.

Identifying Yourself as the Caller
Good morning / good afternoon, this is Mary O'Reilly
from Summit Publishing.
Hello, my name is Steven Nugent.
Hi, it's Kim.
If you're calling for someone else, you can say:
Hello, I'm calling on behalf of Adam at Brightman
Securities.

I just wanted to touch base / reach out.

Checking to See Who's Answering
In case you're not sure if you've reached the right person,
particularly if the person on the other end hasn't identified
him- or herself, you will need to verify. Also, when you receive a call, there may be times when you don't understand
or catch the caller's name.
To / with whom am I speaking again?
Am I speaking to Customer Service?
Is this Jane / the Ferguson residence?

Asking for an English Speaker
I'd like to talk to an English speaker.
Is there someone there who speaks English?
Do you speak English?

Receiving a Call
When answering the phone in a more formal or business
situation, you should give information in the following
order:
company, department or office name, and greeting

speaker's name
offer to help
Stating the company phone number is another, but
rather uncommon, way to answer the phone. In an informal
situation, such as at home, you can answer simply with
"Hello." Some people may answer with "Smith residence,"
for example, although this is outdated.
And remember, it's always a good idea to put a smile in
your voice that will make you sound friendly and helpful to
the caller.

Checking Details
When speaking on the phone, you may misunderstand or
miss information for a variety of reasons: background
noise, a bad connection, a person's accent, or your own
nervousness.

Connecting a Call
At work or at home, there are occasions when you will
reach someone—a secretary, receptionist, or relative—other
than the person with whom you want to speak. If it's a secretary or receptionist, that person should automatically
connect you or "put you through," or "put you on hold" until the person is available. Depending on the nature of the
call and your success, or lack of success, in getting what you
want, you can ask to be connected with someone else.

Saying "Please"

Expressing Thanks

Response
Remember that "Please" is not a response to "thank you."
When someone thanks you for something, it is customary
to reply with:
You're welcome! (more common in North America
than in Great Britain)
My pleasure / The pleasure was all mine!
Don't mention it!
Not at all!
No problem!
(

See Appreciation and Gratitude, page 103.)

Calling a Wrong Number
Because people move and forget to change their contact information, or they press the wrong digits when dialing, they
can end up reaching you by mistake. Or for the same reasons, you might reach the wrong person.
I'm sorry, I must have the wrong number.
I seem to have the wrong number.

--- Page Break ---

Receiving a Call to the Wrong Number
You've got / must have the wrong number.
There's no one here by that name.

Unwanted Calls
Unless you have an unlisted number (a number that doesn't
appear in a telephone book), there will be times when you
receive annoying calls from telemarketers, poll takers, solicitors for donations, and scam artists.
I'm sorry, but I'm not interested.
Please remove my number from your records.
Don't call this number again.

Making Small Talk
When people are busy, you don't want to waste time with
irrelevant or meaningless chitchat. But sometimes, starting
a conversation by asking how the person is and making a
comment about the weather, a recent meeting or business
trip, a sports event, or work can open up the lines of communication. And it is always thoughtful to show interest in
what the other person's saying. Some useful phrases are:
Asking About Someone's Well-Being, page 22.
Rejoinders, page 47.
Showing Interest, page 8.

Dialogue: Touching Base
A: Good morning, Mountain Trekking. Jody speaking.
How may I direct your call?
B: Hello, this is Tom Smith. I'd like to speak to Marcia
Grant.
A: Oh, I'm sorry, Marcia's on another line. May I ask
what you're calling about?
B: Well, I just wanted to touch base.
A: I see her line's still busy. Can I put you on hold?
B: Well, all right, if it's not too long.
A: It shouldn't be more than a minute.
(a minute later)
Marcia, I have Tom Smith on the line.
C: Go ahead, Jody. Put him through. Hi, Tom?
B: Hello, Marcia. How's it going?
C: Oh, things are pretty crazy as usual around here, but I
can't complain. What's up with you?
B: Well, I've been on holiday for a couple of weeks.
C: Really? Where?
B: I was doing some skiing up at Whistler.
C: Whistler? Lucky you! I bet you had a great time.
B: I did, but now it's back to work.
C: So what can I do for you?
B: Actually, if you've got some time, I was just calling to
touch base. It's been a while.

C: You aren't kidding. Time just seems to fly. Listen,
