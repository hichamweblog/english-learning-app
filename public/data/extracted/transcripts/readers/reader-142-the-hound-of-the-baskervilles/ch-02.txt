Holmes nodded and Mortimer opened the newspaper and began to

Sir Charles was only at Baskerville Hall for a short time but his generosity
won the affection and respect of all. He made his fortune in South Africa
before returning to England and wanted to use some of his wealth to repair
Baskerville Hall. He wanted the whole area to profit from his good luck.
The circumstances of his death are unusual, but we must forget the local
superstitions. He apparently died of natural causes. The evidence of his
servants, Mr Barrymore and his wife, shows that Sir Charles was in bad
health, and probably suffered from a heart problem. On the day of his
death he ordered Barrymore to prepare his luggage. He intended to leave
for London the next day. That night he went for his usual walk down the
Hall's famous avenue of trees. He never returned. At midnight Barrymore
went to look for him. Halfway down the avenue there is agate that opens
onto the moor. It seems that he stood here for several minutes but his body
was in fact found at the end of the avenue. There were no signs of violence
but his face had a terrible expression on it, possibly the result of a heart
attack, the verdict confirmed by the post-mortem examination. We
understand that Sir Charles's only living yelative is Mr Henry Baskerville,
his nephew. He is presently living in America and we hope that if he comes
to the Hall he will continue hisylate uncle’s good work in the community.
