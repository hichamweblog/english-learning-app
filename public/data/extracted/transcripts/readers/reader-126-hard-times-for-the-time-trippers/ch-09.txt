Unions

fter many years of working in terrible, dangerous conditions
A for little money, miners began to organise their protest,

forming unions. These unions were initially at a local level,
but soon the British Federation of Miners was created. They asked
for better working conditions, higher pay and better living
conditions. They organised accident funds and pension funds, too.
These also covered miners who could not work because of illness,
until they qualified for a state pension. Most of their requests were
accepted — the government realized that the miners’ contribution
to the economy of Britain was very important.
The National Union of Miners became the strongest union in the
twentieth century. But in the 1980s they lost a desperate battle to
keep the mines open and save miners’ jobs, and so generations of
courage, tradition and industry came to an end.
