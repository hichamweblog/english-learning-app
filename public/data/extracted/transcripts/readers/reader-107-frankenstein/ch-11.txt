creature across deserts and mountains beyond the limits of the
known world. In the novel we have a strong sense that our “safe” world
of cities and houses is surrounded by-unknown, unexplored territory.

For Europeans the early 19th century was still the age of explora-
tion. People had a general idea of what the world looked like but
enormous areas of the globe were still unexplored, including
Australia and the Pacific Ocean, the interior of Africa, large parts of
North and South America and the polar regions. Nobody knew
what strange things were still waiting to be discovered.

cientific expeditions travelled to all parts of the world
during this period. Their purpose was to expand geographical
knowledge and to study the plants and animals they found.

James Cook, a captain in the British Navy, carried out expedi-
tions into the Pacific Ocean during the 1760s and 1770s. He visited
New Zealand and many islands in the South Pacific then unknown
to Europeans. He was searching for a continent that geographers
believed lay south of Australia.

During his second expedition, from 1772 to 1775, he went
farther south than any
European had ever vil
gone. He sailed into
freezing Antarctic
waters, past gigantic
icebergs hidden by fog.
Thick ice prevented
him from coming close
enough to Antarctica
to see the continent.
