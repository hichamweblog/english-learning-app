‘At home we started with an innocent life. Walking home
from village dances across pale wet fields, looking at birds on
the moonlit lake, playing a tune across the water in the carly
morning with no other sound in the clear cold air.

Butit was a alse life. False because it wasn’t right to let people
live a life like that if they weren’t going to be allowed to stay in
it, if they were already marked to go someplace else. It didn’t
prepare us for New York or London, Boston or Manchester.

There was bitterness and jealousy and hunger at home ~
that’s true, I can’t say it isn't. But is it fair to be punished with
a slow death from a bleeding wound? I look at people's faces
when they hear the names of tunes from home, ‘The Boys of
Ballisodare’ and ‘The Plains of Boyle’, and I know they are
dying inside.

Seamus owned a bar. Like the cop, he lived in a good house
