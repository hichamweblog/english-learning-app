At breakfast the next morning Hughie’s servant told him a
visitor was waiting for him. ‘Monsieur Gustave Naudin, sir,’ the
servant said, ‘representing Baron Hausberg.’

‘He probably wants me to apologise! for what I did
yesterday,’ Hughie thought.

The servant opened the door for the visitor. He was an old
gentleman with gold glasses and grey hair. He had a French
accent. ‘Do | have the honour of speaking to Monsieur Erskine?’
he asked. ‘I have come from Baron Hausberg.’

Hughie interrupted the man. ‘Sir, please offer the Baron my
sincerest apologies for what happened yesterday.’

‘The Baron,’ said the old gentleman with a smile, ‘has told me
to give you this envelope.’

On the envelope was written, ‘A wedding present to Hugh
Erskine and Laura Merton, from an old beggar,’ and inside was a
cheque? for ten thousand pounds.

When they got married Alan Trevor was the best man, 3 and
the Baron made a speech at the wedding.

‘Millionaire models are rare,’ remarked Alan, ‘but model
