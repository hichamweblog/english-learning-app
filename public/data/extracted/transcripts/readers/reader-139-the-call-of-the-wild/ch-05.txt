Perrault looks at his wristwatch. He isn’t happy that they are
Wasting so much time with Buck’s game. They have to get on the
trail. The two men decide to give-in — Buck can be the leader. They
take Sol-leks from the lead position and call to Buck. Buck laughs as
dogs laugh, with his mouth open, but he doesn’t come. Francois takes
off Sol-leks’ harness and puts him back in his old position, in front of
the sled. The dog team is harnessed to the sled, ready to start on the
trail. The lead position is empty. Frangois calls Buck again. Again,
Buck laughs and doesn’t come.

Perrault watches the scene for,a.while. Then he says, “Throw
down your stick.”

Frangois throws it down. Buck watches him, then trots into the
lead position, wagging his tail. They put on his harness and the dogs
begin to pull the sled. They are on the trail again.

The Dogsled Tear
Has New Owners

uck is an excellent leader. The dogsled team makes good

time now. The temperature is fifty degrees below zero and

they don’t stop very often — they make long runs each day
to keep the dogs moving in the freezing cold. Each day for two weeks,
the team travels forty miles a day.

Perrault and Frangois stop for three days to rest in Skagway to
drink with their friends there. The dogs are very popular. Many peo-
ple stop to look at them. Francois decides to sell the dogsled team to a
Scottish man who offers him a lot of money. When he takes the
team to the man the next day he is very unhappy. He throws his
arms around Buck and,cries. Buck never sees Frangois or Perrault
again.

N ow Buck must work with new dogs. They go back down the

trail to Dawson. Buck works very hard — he is in the mail
service on the busiest route. The dogs carry a very heavy load.
They carry many letters from all over the world to the men who are
looking for gold in the Northland.
