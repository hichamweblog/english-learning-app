Possible answer:

Dracula brings the ancient world of
Gothic novels into the modern world,
and in an interesting way creates a
direct comparison between the dark
powers of a Gothic vampire and the
powers of the modern scientific
world. Indeed many powers of the
Count have a direct counterpart in
Van Helsing, who is a strange, almost
menacing figure himself, and his

142

science. The most obvious scientific
arm is that of blood transfusion: blood
is taken from one person and given to
another. This is almost exactly what a
vampire does, only, of course, the
vampire’s action is considered evil.
Then there is the telegram which
allows the ‘good guys’ of the book to
communicate over long distances
rapidly; this is much like the Count’s
telepathic powers. Here though, the
Count has an advantage because he
cannot only communicate but he can
also control over long distances with
the power of his mind. Then the
modern world offered Jonathan and
his friends the Winchester rifles,
which made it easier for them to fight
the Count’s Slaves. They also had
steam launches which moved rapidly
whatever the weather was like, much
like what the Count was able to do
with ships.

Finally the Count’s enemies organised
their information for the fight, and the
typewriter certainly made this much
easier for them.

Still, according to Van Helsing, the
key element they had in their fight
was love, and as we now know, the
power science has given man without
love is as deadly as any evil vampire.

a. First-person narrative.

articles and business
correspondence.

c. Certainly (as with the
epistolary novels, i.e., novels
composed of letters), these kind
of documents give the narrative
a feeling of immediacy and
realism that the standard third-

person narrative lacks. We also
have the chance to see the most
intimate thoughts of the

their more public sides in the

collection of documents that
the characters themselves

the Count.

realistic and made the ‘ancient’
menace of the Count seem more
of our own world and time.
