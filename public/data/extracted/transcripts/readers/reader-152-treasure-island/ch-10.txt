Where?

W e can imagine that Treasure Island was somewhere in the

Caribbean Sea between North and South America. Here
there are thousands of islands, some small and uninhabited, others
large and densely populated (like Cuba or Jamaica). The climate is
sub-tropical and attractive for tourists but there are also tremendous
hurricanes smashing into the islands, destroying towns and killing
people.

T he Caribbean Sea was

famous for pirates, espe-
cially in the years between 1550 and
1750. Spain, England, France and
Holland all had colonies in this area.
These countries were often at war
and attacked each others’ ships. It was easy for pirates of all nationa-
lities to take advantage of this situation. Some pirates only attacked
foreign ships, others attacked all ships.

panish galleons carrying gold and sil-
ver back to Spain were favourite tar-
gets. Pirate money was usually the Spanish gold
dollar, called the doubloon. Each doubloon was
worth eight gold escudos and so they were also
called “pieces of eight”.
_ There were no banks to keep money
safe so some pirates buried chests
full of treasure in secret places.
They planned to return one day
to Tecover the valuables put many

Who? .

e have a romantic

led brutal lives. They attacked
ships, stole gold and other valu-
ables and often murdered sailors
and passengers.

Some pirates were simply cri-
minals, greedy for money, others
were sailors who had deserted
their ships because of the hard life
and the severe discipline. There
were also mutineers, escaped priso-
ners and even escaped slaves.
They were of different
nationalities and diffe-

rent races.

hey did not usually

live very long. Many
pirates died of disease in
the tropical climate or of
wounds received in battle.
Some were murdered by
their own comrades. If they
were captured, they faced
execution by hanging.
