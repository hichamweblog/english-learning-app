‘Life would have been comfortable if Mr Kurtz had not arrived’

Look at this example of the third conditional:
If + had + past participle, would have + past participle
If Mr Kurtz had not arrived, life would have been comfortable.

The third conditional tells us about a situation in the past that is not
real or that did not happen. In this case, the real situation is that:

Mr Kurtz arrived and life is not comfortable.

‘Get him hanged’
Look at this:
get + object + past participle
The manager got him hanged.
This means that the manager did not hang him, but that the manager
made the plans for someone else to hang him.
Instead of ‘get’ (which is often used in spoken English) we often use ‘have’.

have + object + past participle
The manager had him hanged.
