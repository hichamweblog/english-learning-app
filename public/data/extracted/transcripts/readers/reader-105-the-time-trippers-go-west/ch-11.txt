The Native Americans

n the 1600s, after the European colonisation of America,

the Native Americans abandoned their agricultural life and

became nomadic hunters. This change was possible thanks
to the presence of wild horses and their ability as riders. Now, more
than ever, their lives depended on the calendar of Nature, following the
migrations of the buffalo. They lived in harmony with Nature, with no
concept of private property. The Indians of the plains believed that
Wakan Tanka was the creator of the world, and that when they died
they went to the Happy Hunting Ground. The stories and legends of
the tribe were recorded in oral traditions, often by the Shaman, their
spiritual leader and “Medicine Man”. Political decisions were made by
a tribal council of men, but equality was important and women were
respected; they had a precise role, the preparation of food and clothes,
while the men hunted and prepared weapons. Children were also very
important and were given a name at the age of eight or nine, when they
had their first “vision”, usually of an animal. Their education came from
their uncle, their mother’s brother. Generally, a family consisted of three
or four children, and tribes were divided into bands with family connections.
It was a harmonious society. Until the arrival of the white man, there
was no word for law in the Indian vocabulary.
