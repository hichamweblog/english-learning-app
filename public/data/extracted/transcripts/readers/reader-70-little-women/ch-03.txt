r. March, the father of the main characters in Little

Women is a very religious man. He was once wealthy,
but because of his religious beliefs, he loses most of his money
by helping friends. This is why the little women grow up in
poverty. Their father’s religion leads him to leave the family
and serve as a chaplain in the Union Army dur-
ing the Civil War. Mr. March is a Puritan, a
type of Christian who is very strict in lead-

ing a moral life.

Puritans strongly believe that
each individual should con-
tinually fight off any tempta-
tion of sin. According to the
Puritans, life is a test, In the
novel Little Women this idea
is a constant theme. Each of
the little women has some flaw: Meg is greedy; Jo has a short
temper; Beth is painfully shy; and Amy is selfish. The girls
overcome their flaws through difficult lessons learned by living
a simple life. These Puritan ideals are commonly found in
many other works in American literature. Many American sto-
ries focus on characters who must make choices in life.
Lessons are learned the hard way and the main characters
become better people morally after learning these lessons.
