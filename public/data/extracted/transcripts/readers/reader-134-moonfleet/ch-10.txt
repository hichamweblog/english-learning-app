We use although, though and even though at the beginning of a clause
of concession. These three expressions mean the same thing, but even
though is slightly stronger than though. In the example above, John
means that it might be expected for Elzevir to be weaker than the
younger guard, but the opposite is true. You can put a clause of
concession at the beginning, middle, or end of a sentence.

For example:

Although | love you, | can't marry you.

I can’t marry you, even though I love you.

I can’t marry you, though | love you.
