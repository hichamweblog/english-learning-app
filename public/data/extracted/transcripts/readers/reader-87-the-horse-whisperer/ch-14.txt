ABOUT NICHOLAS EVANS
Nicholas Evans was born in 1950 in Bromsgrove, in the
Midlands of England. He was educated in Britain, but
before going to university he served in Africa with the
charity Voluntary Service Overseas.
Evans returned to England to take up a place at Oxford
University. He then studied journalism and worked as a
reporter and screenwriter for newspapers and television
at home and abroad. Evans' early life is reflected in The
Horse Whisperer in the character of Annie, who makes a
successful career out of journalism.
In the early 1990s Evans' career as a journalist and
screenwriter was suffering a depressing low. He began to
write The Horse Whisperer, and its success ended his
financial worries. Evans lives in London with his wife,
Jennifer, and their three children.

ABOUT THE FILM

BACKGROUND AND THEMES
Nicholas Evans got the idea of writing The Horse
Whisperer when he heard about a gypsy man who had the
ability to calm wild horses just by talking to them. He
spent six weeks in America interviewing animal healers
and watching them work before returning to London to
write his book.
'It's not a horsey story,' says Evans. 'It's about people.
Relationships. The horsey elements are important, of
course. What I wanted, though, was an epic romance.'
Relationships are a fundamental theme throughout the
book. We see a family under stress, where tragic
circumstances enhance the flaws and tensions which are
already there. Mother and daughter are not at all close at
the beginning of the book, as Annie works long hours at
her job. The one thing that links them from the start,
however, is Grace's horse Pilgrim. After the accident,
despite all professional advice, Annie stubbornly refuses
to let Pilgrim die, because she can see that he is the tiny
thread that connects her with Grace.
Mother, daughter and horse are inextricably intertwined
with each other. Tom Booker sees that they are '...all
joined in suffering. If he could help the horse, perhaps he
could help them all.' Annie and Grace undergo a spiritual
journey of self discovery. Their lives are forced to change
by a dramatic event which makes them look at
themselves, their thoughts and their feelings. As the pain
is released things get worse before they get better, but
when they come through the pain, they are stronger than
before.

An important theme underlying the novel is that of trust.
Trust is fundamental in bringing about the healing process
within Pilgrim, Grace and Annie. Pilgrim slowly learns to
trust, first Tom, then Grace; Grace learns to trust, first
Tom, then her mother; and Annie learns to trust, first Tom,
then herself. In each case Tom offers what he always
offers, the gift of trust.
Being at ease in one's surroundings is a theme that runs
through the book. Tom Booker is totally at home in the
beautiful Montana countryside, whereas his ex-wife
Rachel never felt at home in the countryside. Annie has no
sense of belonging - she doesn't feel at home in England,
nor does she feel at home in America. 'In each country
they thought that she came from the other one.' Once
more this theme is linked to relationships. Annie feels that
she has had no home since her father died. When she falls
in love with Tom Booker, however, Annie begins to
discover herself and feel at home with herself.

Chapters 4-6
Divide the class into small groups. Ask students to re-read
the paragraphs about the dreams that Annie (pp 19-20)
and Grace (pp 34-5) have. The groups then talk about the
meaning of the dreams and what they say about Annie
and Grace. Each group tells the whole class what they
think.

Chapters 7-9
Divide the class into small groups. Each group talks and
agrees on what they think are the three most important
things that happen in this section of the book. Compare
what each group thinks, and try to reach a whole-class
decision.

Chapters 10-12
Divide the class into small groups. Tell them to write a
different story for Chapter 12. Help them with ideas:
Imagine that Tom didn't die, imagine that Annie stayed in
Montana, imagine that Grace set fire to the hut, etc. Ask
groups to read out their ideas. As a class, decide on the
best one.
