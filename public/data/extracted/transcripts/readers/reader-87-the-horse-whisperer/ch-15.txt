ACTIVITIES AFTER READING THE BOOK

Communicative activities
The following teacher-led activities cover the same
sections of text as the exercises at the back of the reader,
and supplement those exercises. For supplementary
exercises covering shorter sections of the book, see the
photocopiable Student's Activities pages of this Factsheet.
These are primarily for use with class readers but, with the
exception of discussion and pair/group work questions,
can also be used by students working alone in a selfaccess centre.

ACTIVITIES BEFORE READING THE BOOK
Photocopy all the illustrations in the book and cut off the
captions. Divide the class into small groups and give each
group a picture. Students decide what is happening in
their picture and write a short caption plus any words that
people may be saying. Ask groups to read out their
captions.

ACTIVITIES AFTER READING A SECTION
Chapters 1-3
Make separate cards with the names of the main
characters on some, and what they do or what they look
like, or what their characters are on other cards. Make
sure you have a name card for every descriptive card.
Photocopy them as necessary. Working in small groups,
students deal the cards round the group. They take it in
turns to read a card and ask someone for the name to
match a description. If that person has that name he/she
must hand it over. The winner is the person with the most
cards when they have all been matched.

Photocopy all the illustrations with their captions. Put
students into groups and give a set of illustrations to each
group. Ask the groups to write new captions for each
illustration.

Glossary
It will be useful for your students to know the following new words.
They are practised in the 'Before You Read' sections of exercises at
the back of the book. (Definitions are based on those in the Longman
Active Study Dictionary.)
Chapters 1-3
clinic (n) a place where people go for help and medicine
horn (n) the thing in a vehicle which you push to make a noise
needle (n) a very thin metal thing that is pushed into your skin to put
medicine into you
ranch (n) a big farm where cattle (see below), horses or sheep are
kept
rope (n) this is long and strong and is used for tying things
stable (n) a building where a horse is kept
trailer (n) a vehicle which is pulled behind a car; animals or things are
put in it
truck (n) a big lorry
trust (v) to believe that somebody or something will not hurt you
Chapters 4-6
arena (n) an area of ground with a wooden wall round it
brand (n) this is burned into an animal's skin to show who owns it
cattle (n) large animals that farmers keep for their meat and milk
Chapters 7-9
loop (n) a shape like a circle
trick (n) a clever game which makes people surprised and amused
Chapters 10-12
edge (n) the part of something that is furthest from the centre
stallion (n) an adult male horse
