‘When | get to the article on Murphy more tears fill my eyes.’

In this sentence there are two clauses. ‘When | get to the article on
Murphy...’ is called a time clause. A time clause is a series of words with
a subject and a verb, but it doesn’t make complete sense on its own.
The clause ‘more tears fill my eyes’ is called the main clause because it
makes complete sense on its own. Time clauses begin with when, while,
until, before, after, as soon as. Note that in English if we talk about the
future, the verb in the time clause is in the present tense:

When | go to England | want to go to Yorkshire.
NOT When-+t+wit-ge to England | want to go to Yorkshire.
