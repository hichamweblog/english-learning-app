VIEW OF THE NATION

TV magazine in Britain has recently carried out a survey of
A viewing habits of the nation, and the results are
sometimes predictable, sometimes surprising and, it must
be said, a little worrying.
The findings are interesting but full of contradictions. For example,
in 40% of the homes that took part in the survey, the television is
on for more than six hours a day but only 12% of us admit to
watching it for that length of time. Why? Well, we call people who
watch TV for more than six hours a day “heavy viewers"; this is
quite a negative comment and it suggests we do not approve of
those who spend so much time in front of the TV every day.
Television is a necessity without which 54% would feel lonely.
This is an even more worrying statistic when we see that 48% of
the viewers of this 54% do not live alone. What has happened to
the art of conversation? Maybe it isn’t dead yet. The survey asked
people what they regularly did while watching TV and more than half
said that they talked to family and friends or made a phone call.
Others admitted that they did the housework, read, cooked or had a
snack. Doesn't anyone just watch the programme? Only viewers
over the age of 55 concentrate on programmes; younger viewers
watch it with just one eye so that they can complete other tasks
at the same time.

TiVo COMES TO TOWN

hen you're watching TV, do you sometimes want
LVAVE: record a programme that has already started?

Or maybe you want to watch the replay of the goal
your favourite team has just scored? Have you ever wanted to
record a programme but find that you don't have a blank videotape?
Well, TiVo is the answer to your problems. It's the latest gadget in
the TV revolution. What is it? It’s a personal video recorder (PVR),
a box that you put on top of your television set, and it means that
you can watch what you want when you want.
How do you use TiVo, the PVR? This kind of recorder doesn't use
tape; it converts television pictures into a digital format and saves
them, just as a computer does, onto a hard drive big enough to
store forty hours of programmes with DVD-style clarity.
You no longer have to set details of a recording; you simply point
your remote control at the TV screen. You will see a programme
guide and all you have to do is highlight the title of the programme
you want to record. Nothing could be easier. You can record the
programmes of an entire evening, which you can watch when you
have time.
But TiVo does much more than this. It permits you to skip the
advertising; if you have to do something urgent, you can pause
a live programme for
up to thirty minutes
and then return to it
without missing a
moment. If you don't
want to miss a
particular series, you
can use the “Season
Pass", which
automatically records
every episode for you.
TiVo is intelligent
enough to learn your
viewing habits. It
remembers which
shows you enjoy and

automatically records programmes that it thinks will interest you.
You can even rewind live programmes if you missed something
important.

So, is TiVo too good to be true? Possibly. You have to buy the box
and pay a monthly subscription fee. TiVo owners have found some
drawbacks. Six out of ten people with TiVo now watch more TV
and neglect other things to use their time to watch all the TV
programmes TiVo has recorded for them. Seven out of ten record
programmes every day.

TiVo's enthusiasm for recording means that you have more
programmes to watch. If you are an avid TV fan and television is
your main source of entertainment, TiVo may be the gadget for
you. It enables you to take control of your TV... but don't let it take
control of your life!
