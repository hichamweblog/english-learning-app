THE WorLD oF JANE EYRE:
UCTORIAN ENGLAND

Women
¢ he life of a middle-class woman was very restricted. Girls grew

up to become wives and mothers but they did not usually
have a job. There were almost no women doctors or lawyers
or engineers. The only profession really open to women was teaching.

If they were not well-connected to rich
families, it was common for educated midd-
le-class girls — such as Jane Eyre and Miss
Temple — to become teachers or gover-
nesses.

Middle-class families usually had servants.
Many women from poorer families lived
and worked in the houses of the rich (as
Bessie worked for Mrs Reed).

coe — 100 -

Most ordinary working-class women had very hard lives. In the
country they worked in the fields next to the men. In the cities they
worked in the factories. England was experiencing the Industrial
Revolution at that time. Women, and even young children, were
employed in the new cotton mills and down in the coalmines.

Marriage

arriage was the most important event in a woman's life.

It established her position in society. Parents worked hard

to arrange a “good” marriage for their daughters.
Marriage usually lasted for
the whole of a person's life.
Victorian society did not
approve of divorce or
separation and they were
not common. It was also a
scandal in Victorian society
if a man and a woman lived
together without getting
married. This helps to explain
why Jane ran away from
Thornfield Hall after she
found out about Rochester's
marriage to Bertha.

| | | Schools were often owned by individual people or by the
Meee church (such as St John’s village school). Parents often

had to pay to send their children to school. There were also “charity
schools” for orphans or children from poor families (like Lowood
School in the story). Generous people contributed money to set up
institutions like these.

Boarding schools, in which children lived as well as studied, were
common. As an alternative, many rich families employed a private
teacher (a “governess”) to live in their homes and teach only their
children.

School discipline was very strict
and children were regularly beaten.
However attending school was not
obligatory. A lot of children from
poor families, especially girls, had
no education at all and could not

read or write.

Health

Ilness and early death were very common, especially among
( the poor. Most people today live for 75-80 years. But in

Victorian England the average life was only about 40 years.
There were many reasons for this, including insufficient food and bad
sanitary conditions, especially in the growing industrial cities. There
were not many hospitals and doctors. Medical treatment was expensive
and it was not as advanced as it is today. Doctors often did not know
the real cause of a disease or how to cure it. Helen Burns probably died
of tuberculosis, caused by poor food and living conditions. This disease
is very infectious and killed millions of people in the 19th century.
Nobody knew how to treat people who were mentally ill (such as
Mrs Rochester in the story). The science of psychology was only
beginning in this period and there was no real understanding of the
causes of insanity. It was a terrible disgrace for a family to have someone
who was “mad”. It was common to lock up such people in institutions,
where they were often forgotten.
