Common Health & Exercise  
Myths Debunked 
There is a lot of misinformation out there about 
health and exercise. Someone at the gym says 
something, and then that “fact” gets passed around 
through friends and acquaintances until it ends up 
online.  
 
Many people would like to eat better or get in 
shape, but end up getting bad advice that doesn't 
work – and then they become discouraged and give 
up. Let’s put an end to a few common myths about 
health and exercise right here and now. 
 
Myth #1 – “Treadmills are easier on your 
knees than asphalt” 
 
One of the biggest reasons people don't run is that 
they're afraid it will damage their knees. While it's 
true that over-stressing your knees by running too 
much is bad for you, using a treadmill, as opposed 
to running outside, doesn't actually change 
anything. When you run, your body weight comes 
down on your knee at an accelerated pace. So 
whether you're coming down on asphalt or rubber 
doesn't make much difference.

Myth #2 –  
“Swimming is great for weight loss” 
 
Swimming is good for a lot of things, mainly 
increasing lung capacity and toning muscles. It is 
not, however, the best formula for weight loss. The 
buoyancy of the human body means that 
exercising in water doesn't work the body as much 
as exercising outside of water. For the same reason, 
a lot of fitness classes for elderly people are done 
in swimming pools.  
 
That isn't to say that swimming is a bad workout, it 
just needs to be done alongside other more fat-
burning exercises like cardio and strength 
training when trying to lose weight.

Myth #4 – “Sugar is healthier than High 
Fructose Corn Syrup” 
 
High fructose corn syrup or HFCS is often vilified 
as one of the causes of poor health in the United 
States, and natural sugar is often identified as a 
“healthier” alternative.  
 
Unfortunately this is not true. Sugar and HFCS both 
cause a spike in blood sugar levels which, when 
consumed regularly, leads to obesity and Type 2 
diabetes. The true risk factor is not HFCS 
specifically, but one’s total intake of sugars of any 
kind.
