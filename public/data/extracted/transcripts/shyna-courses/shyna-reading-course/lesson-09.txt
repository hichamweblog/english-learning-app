Cyberbullying on the Rise 
If you open a newspaper or do a Google search, 
you’ll find a wealth of current articles about 
cyberbullying and its consequences. Although 
some try to excuse it as normal teenage antics, 
cyberbullying can cause psychological trauma 
and even lead to suicide. It is indisputably on 
the rise, and one reason is because it’s so easy. 
A few words on a keyboard, then a post to a 
social media site, and the torment begins… 
and escalates. 
The Evolution of Bullying 
In the past, bullying actually used to take a bit 
of work. A bully would corner his or her victim 
in the schoolyard, administer a beating either 
alone or with the help of a few like-minded 
friends. Or the bully would start a rumor, 
nurture it, and wait for it to spread. Bullying 
took more effort, and time. 
That’s not the case any longer. Now, videos go 
viral in no time. In cyberspace, anyone can 
make hateful or humiliating posts and have 
them seen by countless people in the blink of 
an eye.

Types of Cyberbullying 
Cyberbullying typically involves slander, 
harassment, and sometimes stalking. It’s 
obviously not physical – no one can punch you 
online. It does, however, often derive from, or 
lead to, physical bullying; and you might be 
surprised at how inventive cyberbullies can be 
when it comes to causing harm. 
In his article on Cyberbullying Tactics 2015, 
Michael Nuccitelli, Psy.D., identifies 42 different 
types of cyberbullying. It’s not possible to talk 
about all of them within the scope of this article, 
but here are a few examples. 
1. Blogobullying 
Can you imagine someone creating and 
devoting a blog to the specific purpose of 
targeting one child? It happens, and it’s 
one of the most vicious forms of 
cyberbullying. Cyberbullies know how to 
draw traffic to defamatory blogs. And of 
course we know that the Internet is forever 
– this type of bullying can dog a child for 
years, and could even hurt him or her 
when applying for jobs or college 
admission.

This goes beyond the occasional rude or

barrage, sometimes involving hundreds,

3. Digital Piracy Inclusion 
With this form of attack, the victim is 
encouraged to pirate copyrighted content. 
The cyberbully then reports the victim to 
the authorities. This can be very 
damaging in terms of legal ramifications. 
4. Social Exclusion 
Children all want to be included in a peer 
group, and being left out can be agonizing. 
With social exclusion, the bully encourages 
others to “unfriend” the victim on social 
media sites. It’s a very old tactic, with a 
modern twist. 
5. Impersonation 
This is a kind of identity theft. Sometimes 
called “imping,” it involves impersonating 
the victim and making offensive comments 
under his/her name on social media sites.

How can Cyberbullying be Stopped? 
It is not enough to tell kids and teens to simply 
“get over it” or develop a thicker skin – 
cyberbullying goes beyond innocent teasing. 
Nowadays, laws are being made to deal 
specifically with the problem of cyberbullying – 
but legislation is far from a surefire solution to 
the problem.  
Instead, it is imperative for parents and teachers 
to be proactive and try to nip cyberbullying in 
the bud. Pay attention to what your child is doing 
and experiencing online, and educate him or her 
on the virtues of the golden rule.
