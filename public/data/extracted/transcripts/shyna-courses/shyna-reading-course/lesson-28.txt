6 Songs that Changed 
the World 
They say music is the universal language.  It 
speaks to us. It moves us. It gets our feet 
tapping, it makes us dance, and it makes us cry.  
Occasionally a song comes along that's so 
powerful that it changes the very world we live 
in. Maybe it inspires social change or helps us 
relate to the plight of our fellow man. One song 
with a striking message can bring home a 
point even better than a speech or rally.   
Keep reading to discover six songs that have 
packed a punch in the past century. 
“I Wanna Hold Your Hand” by 
The Beatles 
This is the song that kick-started "Beatlemania" 
in the United States. After it aired, there was a 
landslide of purchases of the hit single. The 
upbeat song was a godsend to a nation that was 
still reeling from the assassination of President 
John F. Kennedy one month prior.  
“Johnny B. Goode” by Chuck 
Berry 
Many consider Chuck Berry to be the father of 
modern rock n' roll – and this is his crown 
jewel. Music today just wouldn't be the same 
without this one.  
Magazines like “The Rolling Stone” have called it 
the best guitar track ever – and NASA even sent

 “A Change is Gonna Come” by 
Sam Cooke 
This song, released in 1964, tells a story of a man 
who's convinced that things will change soon – 
for the better. It was inspired by Cooke's own 
experiences with discrimination and 
segregation – particularly an incident where he 
and his wife were turned away from a “whites-
only” motel in the southern U.S. 
The sad yet prophetic tune resonated with 
audiences in the '60s and became the anthem for 
the American Civil Rights Movement.  
“Imagine” by John Lennon 
Lennon's most successful solo effort asks us to 
picture a world without religion, borders, or 
violence. Both the utopic vision and the 
soothing melody struck a chord with listeners, 
and it's been considered one of the greatest 
songs of all time ever since.  
In an interview, Lennon admitted that his anti-
religious, anti-capitalistic, anti-nationalistic 
message was contentious – but said that 
because it was sugar-coated, it went over well 
with the masses. 
The song became especially poignant after 
Lennon was assassinated in 1980.

“Strange Fruit” by Billie Holiday 
Long before Sam Cooke sang “A Change is Gonna 
Come”, Billie Holiday gave her own take on 
racism in America. In 1939, decades after 
emancipation, blacks were still the victims of 
hate crimes – a fact that most Americans 
preferred to sweep under the carpet. 
The lyrics allude to the lynching of African-
Americans – a practice that was common at the 
time. The song hauntingly describes the 
“strange fruit swinging in the Southern breeze”. 
This was one of the first times a black singer had 
dared to sing publicly about the racial issues of 
the period, and “Time” magazine named it their 
“song of the century” in 1999.
