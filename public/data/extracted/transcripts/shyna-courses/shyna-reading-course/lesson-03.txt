Amazing Celebrations  
Around the World 
Cultures around the globe share a love of 
celebration. The reasons and methods for 
festivals are as distinct as the people 
themselves, and each one contributes to 
the rich heritage of human existence. Here 
are a few celebrations that stand out. 
 
Carnival in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil 
 
This festival, which dates back to 1723, 
has become a world-renowned 
celebration. In its early days, rowdy 
celebrants took to the streets and soaked 
each other with buckets of water and 
limes – a tradition that often led to brawls. 
In the 19th century, the festival evolved to 
include parades with masks and music.  
 
Nowadays, over two million people flood 
the streets of Rio de Janeiro to participate 
in the singing and dancing, with costumes, 
ornately-decorated floats and general 
revelry.   
 
Samba schools from all over Brazil put on 
magnificent performances, each one 
relating a different story or theme. The 
beat of the music fills the air and you can 
feast your eyes on the vibrant colors and 
sights.

The Chinese New Year 
 
China commemorates the dawning of the New 
Year on a different schedule than the Western 
world, and its people don’t hold back in 
celebrating it. The Chinese New Year is the 
largest and longest festival in the country, 
with festivities typically lasting two full weeks. 
It is also celebrated in countries with sizable 
populations of Chinese immigrants. 
 
The celebration, which was traditionally a 
time to honor deities and ancestors, is 
replete with fireworks, lanterns and music. 
Families perform a thorough house cleaning 
to get rid of any bad luck and make way for a 
happy and prosperous year.  
 
South Korea’s Boryeong  
Mud Festival 
 
This rather odd tradition is a recent invention 
– it began in 1996 when a local cosmetics 
company created a line of mud-based beauty 
products. While most companies would invest 
in marketing and commercials, this one 
instead started the mud festival as a real-life 
example of the products.  
 
Attendees can participate in mud slides, mud 
wrestling, and even mud skiing. The event 
spread by word of mouth and became an 
international phenomenon – now millions of 
people come to the city in order to slather 
themselves in the mineral-rich mud.

Harbin Ice and Snow Festival - 
Harbin, China 
 
Each year in Harbin, China there is a truly magical 
celebration that blends art and ice. Craftsmen 
from around the country go to Harbin to create 
some of the most exquisite snow and ice 
sculptures found anywhere in the world. The 
exhibitions often include lasers and lights to add 
some special effects. Weather-permitting, the 
artistic creations last for an entire month.  
 
St. Petersburg, Russia: White Nights 
Festival 
 
The White Nights Festival is considered one of the 
finest international arts festivals. It takes place 
over the summer, the “season of the midnight sun” 
in Russia – hence the name. Created in 1993 to 
serve as “a musical gift to the city,” it features 
outstanding performers in genres ranging from 
opera to popular music to classical ballet.  
 
The culmination of the festival, which is called 
the Scarlet Sails festival, is a massive event 
attended by over a million people. Many of them 
are students and schoolchildren, as the event 
coincides with the end of the school year.
