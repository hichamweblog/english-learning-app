The Dark Side of  
International Adoption 
Each year, approximately 40,000 children join 
new families via international adoption. The 
procedures and requirements vary from country 
to country – some have well-established systems 
to facilitate the process, while others expressly 
forbid adopting across borders.  
International adoption is often a lengthy and 
costly endeavor, and the amount of paperwork 
may seem onerous. However, it is all done with 
the end goal of providing a loving and stable 
home for the child. 
Adopting a child from another country is usually 
a positive experience for both the family and 
child – but it is not without its problems. 
Trafficking and Abuse  
The demand for healthy, young children from the 
developing world is enormous – especially as 
birth rates in developed countries decline. 
Critics of international adoption believe that this 
demand is fueling nefarious practices in poorer 
countries. With such a large “marketplace,” 
unscrupulous profit-seekers can make a lot of 
money from desperate Western families who

Roadblocks to Happiness  
Adopted children may experience a range of 
emotional issues. Recurring feelings of rejection 
and abandonment are quite common. If the 
child’s birth parents are deceased, he or she may 
experience even greater feelings of loss.

On top of the trauma of being in an unfamiliar 
environment, some adopted children have also 
experienced emotional and physical abuse. The 
combination of these factors can result in the

are unprepared to cope with their child’s

Conclusion  
International adoption has provided many people 
with a nurturing home and opportunities for a 
good life. On the flip side, some adoptees are 
still dealing with the trauma of being stolen from 
their families and sold by human traffickers.  
UNICEF estimates that there are 18 million 
children without parents in the world, and many 
people argue that the exacting regulations are 
restricting these children’s chances of finding a 
loving family. Do the risks of abuse outweigh the 
greater good of international adoptions?
