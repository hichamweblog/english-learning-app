Excite Says ‘No’ to Buying Google 
 
Google has become such a staple of our 
internet experience that it is now a word in the 
dictionary. But back when Google was just a 
fledgling company in Silicon Valley, it was 
available for sale. Excite.com (now known as 
Ask.com) had the opportunity to buy Google 
for the bargain price of $750,000 – and they 
turned it down. Today, Google is worth nearly 
$365 billion, and the leaders of Excite.com are 
probably still kicking themselves to this day. 
 
Kodak Falls Behind the Times 
 
At one point, Kodak was a giant in the film 
industry – but now it is just a small-time 
player. Once a pioneer in technology, Kodak 
completely failed to capitalize on the surge of 
digital photography.

Blockbuster Could Have Owned Netflix 
 
Another tale of a business decision that 
spelled doom for a company is that of 
Blockbuster, which once had nearly 8,000 
video-rental stores in the U.S. In the early 
2000s, a company called Netflix – which was 
providing DVD rentals by mail – approached 
Blockbuster multiple times, hoping to be 
acquired.  
 
At the time, Netflix was in the red and 
Blockbuster decided to pass on the purchase. 
When Netflix later launched on-demand 
streaming over the internet, Blockbuster 
scrambled to play catch-up – but it was too 
late. Blockbuster went under in 2013, and 
Netflix is now worth nearly $20 billion.

Who doesn’t love a bargain? 
 
Sometimes an attempt to innovate is actually 
a misstep – this was a painful lesson learned 
by American retailer JC Penny in 2012. The 
new president of the company decided to 
overhaul the pricing strategy and do away 
with sales and coupons. He implemented 
“everyday low prices” instead of artificially-
inflated ones that could be discounted.  
 
But it turns out that shoppers like to feel like 
they’re getting a bargain – after the pricing 
change, sales plunged 25% in a year. 
Although JC Penny quickly backpedaled and 
restored the previous pricing, the loss of 
clientele continues to dog the company to 
this day.
