History’s Heroic Dogs 
There’s a reason why dogs are considered “man’s 
best friend.” Every owner has had a dog that he knew 
would save his life if tragedy struck. Throughout 
history, there are countless amazing stories of dogs 
saving their owners from intruders, disasters, and 
wild animals.  
Although maybe not the ideal replacement for a 
bodyguard or a good life insurance policy, a good 
dog will do everything it can to keep you and your 
family safe.  Here are a few stories of heroic dogs 
whose deeds have gone down in history. 
Saint Guinefort 
There is one heroic dog that is actually considered a 
saint by the local community. According to legend, 
Guinefort was a greyhound owned by a French 
knight in the 1200s. One day, the knight left his 
young child alone with Guinefort for the day. When 
he returned he couldn’t find his child, but saw 
Guinefort with blood across its face.  
Fearing that his dog had just slaughtered and 
possibly eaten his young boy, the knight killed 
Guinefort in a rage. Moments later, the knight found 
his child as well as the dead snake that Guinefort had 
killed in order to protect the boy.

Barry the St. Bernard 
The St. Bernard is now associated with hundreds of 
stories of saving people’s lives, especially children, 
and that’s mostly because of Barry, the heroic St. 
Bernard from Switzerland. Barry lived near a 
monastery in Switzerland from 1800-1812. Over 
that time, he is credited with rescuing at least 40 
people who had gotten lost or trapped in the snow.  
One of his most famous stories involves him climbing 
a mountain to rescue a small child when the snow 
was falling too heavily, and the monks were not able 
to reach the boy. The child wrapped his arms around 
Barry, and Barry carried him all the way down the 
mountain. Barry is such a beloved piece of Swiss 
history that after his death he was stuffed, and he 
now has a place in the Swiss Natural History 
Museum.
