It’s the Best Time  
to Be Alive 
The news is chock-full of disasters – 
shootings, terrorism, rape – and it seems like 
every time you tune in, things are getting 
worse. The constant stream of bad news 
often makes it seem like the future is bleak – 
or even that the self-destruction of society is 
imminent. 
But is this really the case? 
The media fixates on the most horrifying 
news because people eat it up. Of course, this 
results in big paydays for the networks and 
advertisers. But when we step back and 
consider the broader historical context, the 
truth is that today is the best time to be alive.  
If you’re not convinced, read on… 
We Live Longer 
Nowadays, the average life expectancy 
worldwide is just over 70 years. That's more 
than double what it was a hundred years ago. 
In those days, people were lucky to make it to 
their early 30s. Most of us would already be 
past our prime! 
Part of the reason for the boost in life 
expectancy is a drastic reduction in infant 
mortality rates. In the early 1900s, 1 in 5 
babies would die before their first birthday. 
These days, it's only 1 in 25. Still not perfect, 
but keep in mind that's the average for the 
entire world, which includes even the poorest 
and most war-torn areas.

There’s Less Poverty 
Since 1990, the percentage of people around the 
world who live in extreme poverty has dropped 
by over 25%. If you go all the way back to 1900, 
the decrease approaches a whopping 60%.  
Per capita income has grown fivefold in the past 
century – from $2,000 to $10,000. Although 
there is still work to be done, we have made 
substantial progress towards eliminating 
extreme poverty. 
Improvements in Education 
Knowing how to read and write is something we 
might take for granted nowadays – but a 
century ago, more than half of the Earth's 
population was illiterate. These days, literacy 
levels are at a historic high of 84% and 
climbing. 
Education is now more accessible than ever, 
especially as the internet puts unlimited 
amounts of information at our fingertips. The 
ability to learn online is very empowering, and 
if governments prioritize connectivity for all 
their citizens, it will go a long way towards 
leveling the playing field economically. 
In addition, the internet helps us interact with 
people from different cultures and viewpoints, 
so we can gain a global perspective instead of 
remaining secluded in our local communities. 
Violence is Decreasing 
No, we haven’t reached the pinnacle of world 
peace just yet. But we’re the closest we’ve been 
in the past 50 years.

Although the headlines and social media might 
give the impression that the world is a powder 
keg… the statistics don’t lie. The number of 
wars (including civil wars and other armed 
conflicts) is going down, and the number of 
casualties is at its lowest point in decades. 
In addition, the homicide rate has been 
declining worldwide, and violence against 
women and children - including physical and 
sexual abuse, has also fallen since 1990. 
Although the reduction is unevenly distributed 
across geographic regions, there are grounds 
for optimism.  
Doomsday Is Not Upon Us 
On average, the world is much safer and more 
prosperous than it was even one or two 
generations ago. So why this sense of 
impending doom? 
One reason is that, thanks to the world’s 
interconnectedness, we are far more cognizant 
of disasters nowadays – regardless of where 
they are happening. Another factor is that the 
human brain is hard-wired by evolution to 
retain information about danger instinctually. 
But when we look at the facts, we realize that 
life is better than it's ever been.  
There is just one worrisome metric, and it has 
to do with the environment. Carbon dioxide 
levels in the atmosphere and global 
temperature have been creeping steadily 
upwards over the past century. This could 
cause major disruption to the global climate 
unless we reduce our carbon output and 
transition to clean energy.
