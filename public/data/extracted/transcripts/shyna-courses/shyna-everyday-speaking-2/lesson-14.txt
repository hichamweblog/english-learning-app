Lesson 14: Previous Jobs 
 
William and Samantha are co-workers at an accounting firm. Listen to them share 
memories about their previous jobs, and try to answer the questions below: 
 
Listening Comprehension 
1. Samantha is feeling pressure from... 
a. clients 
b. co-workers 
c. the boss 
2. William says that ten years ago the workload was... 
a. about the same 
b. heavier 
c. lighter 
3. William sometimes does extra work... 
a. at home 
b. at night 
c. near the pool 
4. Samantha stopped teaching because... 
a. the schedule was unpredictable 
b. the salary was too low 
c. the kids were hard to control 
5. Samantha is considering... 
a. going back to school 
b. having children 
c. looking for a less stressful job 
6. Before being an accountant, William... 
a. did manual labor 
b. was a stay-at-home dad 
c. worked at a gym 
 


 
 
Conversation Text 
 
William: Gosh, it's only 10:00 and I’ve 
already got a ton on my plate. 
 
Samantha: Me too, I’m up to my ears in 
paperwork and at least three clients are 
breathing down my neck, wanting to seal 
the deal on their contracts.  
 
William: I remember when I first started 
working here ten years ago. Things were busy 
then, but not like this. Nowadays it feels like 
no matter how many hours you put in, you 
can never make a dent in the workload. 
 
Samantha: Part of the problem is that every 
single client wants the work done, like, 
yesterday. I don’t understand how they can 
give us the work on such short notice and 
expect us to finish so fast. 
 
William: You just have to give it your best 
shot. Sometimes I take work home on the 
weekends, but it sucks to be cooped up in 
my home office while the sun is shining and 
the kids are in the pool. 
 
on my plate = things I am 
responsible for doing 
 
up to my ears in = 
overwhelmed with (work) 
 
breathing down my neck = 
watch/monitor closely, 
putting pressure on 
 
seal the deal = confirm the 
agreement 
 
make a dent in = make a 
little bit of progress in (a 
big project or amount of 
work) 
 
yesterday = very fast, as 
soon as possible 
 
on short notice = with little 
time before the event 
 
give it your best shot = try 
your best 
 
it sucks (slang) = it’s bad, 
it’s annoying. Also: it stinks 
 
cooped up in = staying 
inside a small place 
 
Vocabulary 


 
 
Samantha: Ugh, I feel your pain! This time of year 
makes me long for the days when I was a teacher 
– sure, the semesters were stressful, but we had 
almost three months of summer vacation. 
 
William: Sounds like you had it made! How come 
you stopped teaching? 
 
Samantha: Well, the catch was that the wages 
were lousy. My husband was about to go to grad 
school, so it would’ve been hard to make ends 
meet on a teacher’s salary. 
 
William: Right. So do you think you’ll go back to 
teaching after your husband graduates? 
 
Samantha: I’m not sure. We’re thinking about 
having kids in the next few years, so I might like to 
try my hand at being a stay-at-home mom. 
 
William: That’d certainly be a change of pace… 
only then you’d be on call 24/7 instead of just 9 to 
5! 
 
Samantha: That’s true! How about you, William? 
What did you do before you were an accountant? 
 
long for = desire 
strongly 
 
you had it made! = be 
successful for life, not 
have to worry 
 
the catch (informal) = 
the bad aspect that 
might ruin a good 
situation 
 
lousy = very bad 
 
make ends meet = 
survive financially 
 
a change of pace = a 
difference in routine 
 
on call = available to 
work 
 
24/7 = constantly, all 
the time 
 
 
Vocabulary 


 
 
William: My dad owns a landscaping 
company, so I used to work for him during the 
summers. Doing yardwork, planting trees and 
bushes, that type of thing. 
 
Samantha: Really? Must have been tiring. 
 
William: Nah, the work was a cinch. Then 
again, I was in better shape back then. Now 
I’ve got bad knees and a beer belly! 
 
Samantha: Haha! What was it like working 
for your dad? 
 
William: Well, he was pretty demanding – but 
that’s what made him successful in business, 
because he always went above and beyond 
for his customers. 
 
Samantha: I guess that’s one reason you’ve 
got such a strong work ethic. 
 
William: Yeah. Speaking of which, I should 
probably get back to work. 
 
Samantha: Same here. See you around. 
 
 
 
 
landscaping = the work of 
designing outdoor spaces 
 
a cinch = very easy 
 
Then again = use this 
phrase to introduce 
something that contradicts 
or modifies your previous 
statement 
 
a beer belly = a fat 
stomach due to drinking 
too much beer 
 
went above and beyond = 
made special extra effort, 
did much more than what 
was required 
 
work ethic = a belief that 
hard work is very 
important
