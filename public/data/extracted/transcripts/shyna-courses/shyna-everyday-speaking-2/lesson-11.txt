Lesson 11: Why aren’t you on time? 
Listen to this conversation between Nate and Brad, two co-workers. Do your best to 
answer the comprehension questions below, then learn the expressions. 
Listening Comprehension 
1. How many times total has Brad been late this week? 
a. three 
b. four 
c. five 
2. He was late because... 
a. he overslept 
b. he was sick yesterday 
c. his car broke down 
3. Brad works a second job because... 
a. he has four children and a lot of expenses 
b. he needs money to pay for a car accident he had 
c. he's in debt and he needs to make the minimum payments 
4. Nate wakes up at... 
a. 7 AM 
b. 8 AM 
c. 9 AM 
5. Brad goes to bed late because... 
a. he studies at night 
b. he watches a lot of TV 
c. he has a busy life 
6. The boss said that if Brad was late again, he would... 
a. be demoted 
b. lose his job 
c. receive a pay cut 
7. Brad is lucky because the boss... 
a. didn't see him arrive late today 
b. is a friend of his family 
c. needs his work for the company 


 
 
Conversation Text 
Nate: You’re late again. This is the fourth time 
this week! You’ve gotta get your act together, 
or else you’re gonna be in hot water. 
Brad: I overslept, okay? Why do you always 
gotta get on my case? It’s not my fault that I’m 
tired all the time. I’m working a second job, so 
I’ve been burning the midnight oil. 
Nate: You have to work a second job because 
you rear-ended a BMW while speeding to 
work. Why were you speeding? Hmmmm, could 
it be because you were running late?! 
Brad: The BMW cut in front of me and then 
slammed on the brakes! And my insurance 
won’t cover the entire repair bill; I have to 
cough up fifty percent out of my own pocket. 
Man, I’ll be working a second job for the rest of 
my life. 
Nate: Brad, things like this wouldn’t happen to 
you if only you would get to work on time. Take 
me, for an example. I’m always right here at my 
desk at the stroke of 9. 
Brad: Don’t rub it in, Mr. Perfect. 
get your act together = 
become better organized 
in hot water = in trouble 
get on my case = criticize me 
in an annoying way 
burning the midnight oil = 
stay up late working or 
studying 
rear-ended = hit the back of 
another car 
speeding = driving faster 
than the speed limit 
slammed on the brakes = 
stopped very suddenly 
cough up = give money you 
don’t want to give 
at the stroke of 9 = at exactly 
9:00 
rub it in = keep talking about 
something that makes you 
embarrassed or upset

Nate: It’s not rocket science – all I do is set my 
alarm clock for 7 AM, so I’ve got plenty of time 
to take a shower, have a leisurely breakfast, 
read the paper… and get to work without 
driving like a maniac. 
Brad: Yeah, well I set my alarm for 7, too, it’s 
just that I hit the snooze button a couple of 
times. Plus, I’ve got a life, I’ve got a girlfriend 
who likes to go out… ya know, I’m busy. So I 
usually hit the sack around 2 AM. 
Nate: Hey, you don’t need to explain yourself to 
me. But make sure the head honcho doesn’t see 
you sneaking in at quarter to ten. I overheard 
him tell the HR director that if you were late one 
more time this week, he’d give you the boot. 
Brad: Wait… he said he would fire me? No way. 
…okay, granted, I’m late a lot. But I make up 
for it during the day. I get way more done than 
those slackers in the marketing department. 
Nate: That may be true, but if you can’t even 
manage to show up on time, how can the 
company count on you for anything that’s 
remotely important? If you get fired, then you 
brought it on yourself. 
it’s not rocket science = it’s 
not difficult or complicated 
leisurely = slow and relaxed

minutes 
hit the sack = (slang) go to 
bed 
head honcho = (slang) the 
boss, the leader, the person 
in charge 
overheard = accidentally 
heard a conversation with 
someone else 
give you the boot = 
(informal) fire you; terminate 
your employment 
granted = I acknowledge that 
this is true 
make up for it = compensate 
for it 
slackers = (slang) lazy people 
show up = arrive, appear 
count on = depend on
