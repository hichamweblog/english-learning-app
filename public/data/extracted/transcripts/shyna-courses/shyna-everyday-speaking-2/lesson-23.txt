Lesson 23: Getting a Raise 
Vance and Aaron are co-workers who have the same boss, Mr. Brown. Listen to Aaron 
give Vance advice on getting a raise, and answer these comprehension questions: 
Listening Comprehension 
1. Vance wants a raise because... 
a. he wants to go on vacation 
b. he's going to get married 
c. his wife wants to buy a house 
2. He's been working for the company for... 
a. ten years 
b. twelve years 
c. twenty years 
3. Mr. Brown can best be described as... 
a. arrogant 
b. perfectionistic 
c. strict 
4. How does Aaron recommend approaching Mr. Brown? 
a. by giving him lots of compliments 
b. carefully and indirectly 
c. with clarity and confidence 
5. Aaron asked for a raise because... 
a. another company offered him a better salary 
b. his kids were going into university 
c. he had worked there longer than anyone else 
6. Vance is especially worried about getting fired because... 
a. he has a lot of student loans to pay off 
b. he'll have difficulty getting another job 
c. his wife is going to have a baby 
7. What does he decide to do at the end of the dialogue? 
a. invite Mr. Brown to his house 
b. talk to Mr. Brown today 
c. send Mr. Brown an e-mail 


 
 
Conversation Text 
Vance: I really, really, need a raise. Melinda 
isn’t saying it to my face, but she wants this 
house we looked at on Snowy Lane. She knows 
we can’t afford it unless I get a raise. 
Aaron: So talk to Mr. Brown and ask for one. 
You’ve been with this company for a decade 
now, and you’ve got a great track record. 
You’re honest. You put in overtime when 
needed. You deserve a raise. 
Vance: I know, I know. It’s just that Mr. Brown 
is always fussing about how we need to cut 
costs around here. He’s not exactly the type of 
boss that hands out raises like candy. 
Aaron: Mr. Brown definitely doesn’t tolerate 
any nonsense. But sometimes that’s for the 
best.  
Vance: How so? 
Aaron: Mr. Brown respects men who are 
upfront and straightforward. He doesn’t like 
weaklings. If you go into his office hanging 
your head and beating around the bush he’s 
just gonna kick you out.

You have to march into that man’s office with your 
chin up, look him in the eye, and ask with

Vance: And Mr. Brown gave you a raise? 
Aaron: Not right away. At first he yelled at me and 
then he threw a lot of financial numbers around 
and so on. But I held my ground. My twins were 
about to go to college, with a double tuition bill to 
boot. So I let him know that unless I got a raise I 
would have to leave and find a job that would 
support my family better. 
Vance: I can’t believe you pulled that off. 
Aaron: Look, Mr. Brown isn’t blind. He knows the 
cost of living is going up. He also knows we give it 
our all. If you go in there asking for a raise to buy 
a new boat, then you don’t have a chance. But if 
you explain that you want to buy a new home for 
your pregnant wife, I’m sure he’ll understand.  
Vance: The apartment Melinda and I are living in 
is pretty small – and when the baby arrives, space 
will be very tight. 
Aaron: Then march into Mr. Brown’s office and 
tell him just that. I promise you, he’s a reasonable 
man. Sure he might bark at you, but don’t back 
down.
