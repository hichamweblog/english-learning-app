Lesson 21: Leadership 
Judy is struggling with her leadership role in a project, and Kevin gives her some 
suggestions. Listen to their conversation and mark these statements “True” or “False.” 
 
Listening Comprehension 
True or false according to the dialogue? 
1. Judy knew the project would be problematic from the beginning. 
2. The project is to improve the children's facilities at a library. 
3. Felix wants to modernize the selection of books. 
4. Cecily wants to use fewer shelves. 
5. The group agrees that the playground should be improved. 
6. Heidi wants to add more areas for different activities. 
7. Judy is the leader of the committee. 
8. The group has tried voting before, but it didn't work. 
9. Judy wants to get rid of all the extra plans. 
10. Kevin thinks Judy's idea of delegating is a good one. 
11. Judy has an upcoming meeting very soon. 
 
 


 
 
Conversation Text 
Judy: I thought this new project would be a piece 
of cake, but it’s turning out to be a real pain.  
 
Kevin: Sorry to hear that. What’s making it such a 
headache? 
 
Judy: The committee just can’t seem to agree on 
anything. Everyone has different ideas about 
what we should do; they’re all so stubborn, and 
I’m at my wits’ end! 
 
Kevin: OK, what’s the goal of your project? Fill 
me in on the details, and maybe I can help you 
out. 
 
Judy: We’re trying to improve the Children’s 
Center at the library. Just basic stuff like 
organizing and adding more shelves indoors, then 
renovating the playground outside.  
 
Kevin: Seems pretty simple. So what’s the issue? 
 
Judy: Basically, Felix wants to sort through the 
old books before we start adding more shelves; 
he says the books are outdated. Then Cecily 
insists that we need to return the shelves we 
already bought and get new, safer ones instead. 
Everyone’s keen on improving the playground, 
but half the people say they have back problems 
so they won’t be able to help. And the ones that 
can work on the playground are bickering about 
which materials we should use.

Judy: That’s a good idea. Got any other tips for

Kevin: Another one is to make a clear 
distinction between what NEEDS to be done 
and what people WANT to do. This will help 
keep the group focused.   
 
Judy: I like that. But what should we do about 
all the extra plans? There are some good ones 
in there, and I don’t want people feeling like 
their ideas are always shot down. 
 
Kevin: There are a couple different ways you 
could deal with it. You could always vote on the 
extras and have the group pick the best one. 
Another way is to divide the project into “Phase 
1” – doing only what’s essential to the goal – 
and then “Phase 2” – adding extra stuff later. 
Judy: Hey, and I just thought of something else
