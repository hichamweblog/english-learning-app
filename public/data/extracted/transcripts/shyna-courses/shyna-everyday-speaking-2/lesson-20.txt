Lesson 20: An Embarrassing Moment 
Jim and Fred are at a restaurant, and a waitress comes to take their order. Listen to 
what happens and mark these statements “True” or “False.” 
 
Listening Comprehension 
True or false according to the dialogue? 
1. Jim orders a bowl of clam chowder 
2. Fred orders one of today's specials 
3. Both of them want tap water to drink 
4. The waitress compliments Fred on his shirt 
5. Fred is attracted to the waitress 
6. It has been an easy week at work for Fred 
7. Fred has been wearing this shirt for years 
8. Fred bought the shirt himself 
9. The collar of the shirt is uncomfortable 
10. Fred becomes annoyed at Jim 
11. Fred asks for the waitress' phone number 
 
 


 
 
Conversation Text 
Waitress: Welcome to Rudy’s Diner! Our 
soup of the day is clam chowder, and our 
specials are Cajun chicken and barbecue 
ribs. What can I get for you guys? 
Jim: I’d like the steak – medium-rare – 
with a side of potatoes.  
Fred: And I’ll have the Cajun chicken, with 
extra hot sauce on the side.  
Waitress: All right, and anything to drink? 
Jim: Just tap water for me. 
Fred: A Diet Coke with no ice. 
Waitress: Coming right up! Nice shirt, by 
the way. 
 
Fred: Thank you!  
 
Jim: That IS a nice shirt, Fred. Very trendy. 
Fred: Thanks! You know, the waitress 
couldn’t take her eyes off me… and she’s 
pretty cute. You think I should ask for her 
number? 
Jim: Couldn’t hurt to try, when she comes 
back with the food. 
Fred: Yeah, okay. 
 
Jim: So… long day at work? 
clam chowder = a thick soup 
containing clams (a type of 
shellfish) in a milk or tomato 
base 
Cajun = a type of cooking from 
the New Orleans area, which is 
usually spicy 
barbecue ribs = “barbecue” 
refers to cooking meat over an 
open fire. “Ribs” are the bones 
and meat from the animal’s side, 
near its heart/lungs 
medium-rare = cooking the steak 
only until a point when it is still 
pink inside 
hot = spicy (it doesn’t refer to 
temperature here) 
tap water = regular water from 
the faucet (not bottled water) 
trendy = fashionable 
couldn’t take her eyes off me = 
continued to look at me 
couldn’t hurt to try = trying 
wouldn’t have any negative 
result

Fred: Long week. I thought Friday would 
never come. 
Jim: Same here. Hey, back to your shirt… 
is it new? 
 
Fred: No, I’ve had it for years. First time 
I’ve worn it, though. Think I got it at a 
thrift shop… I dunno. 
 
Jim: Huh. ‘Cause I’ve never seen that STYLE 
of shirt before. 
 
Fred: Oh! Now I remember – it was a gift 
from my Aunt Kay. She’s got an eye for 
fashion. 
 
Jim: Yeah… I’d never thought of how cool 
buttons can look on the back of a shirt. 
 
Fred: Cool, but it makes the shirt hard to 
get into. I couldn’t reach the upper buttons, 
so I had to ask my roommate to give me a 
hand. 
 
Jim: I bet. And that collar. Man! It’s very… 
original.  
 
Fred: Yeah… I got a lot of compliments on 
it at work, but it’s kinda uncomfortable. 
Been tickling my neck all day. 
Jim: I guess that’s the price you pay for 
being a trend-setter, huh? Actually, your 
tag’s sticking out. Let me tuck that in for 
you. 
Same here = I feel the same way 
/ I’m in the same situation 
thrift shop = store that sells used 
clothing and items at low prices 
She’s got an eye for fashion = 
she has good taste / judgement 
regarding fashion 
give me a hand = help me 
(especially with a physical task) 
I bet = I’m completely sure 
collar = the part of the shirt 
around your neck 
original = new and creative, not 
like anything else 
tickling = touching lightly so that 
it’s uncomfortable or provokes 
laughter 
the price you pay = the 
consequences 
trend-setter = someone who 
establishes new fashions 
tuck in = put part of some 
fabric/clothing inside another 
part

Fred: The tag? Oh, that’s why it’s been bothering 
me, it’s—hey, hold on a sec! 
 
Jim: Hahaha! 
 
Fred: Where’s the men’s room? I’ll be right back. 
 
Jim (when Fred returns to the table): Well, it took 
you long enough. Had to ask someone for help 
with the buttons?  
 
Fred: Why didn’t you just tell me?! I feel like an 
idiot. 
 
Jim: Where’s the fun in that? I wanted to keep 
dropping hints until you noticed.  
 
Fred: Some friend YOU are.  
 
Waitress: OK, here’s your steak and potatoes… 
and here’s your Cajun chicken. Hey! Your shirt 
looks much better that way. 
Fred: Uh, yeah. 
Waitress: Enjoy your meal, guys! 
 
Jim: Smooth… real smooth. Weren’t you going to 
ask for her number? I can call her back over and 
ask for some extra napkins… 
 
Fred: Forget it! As if she’d want to go out with 
some doofus who put his shirt on backwards. 
 
Jim: Aw man, that was the funniest thing that’s happened all week!  
 
Fred: Easy for you to say. I’ve never been more embarrassed in my life.

men's room = men’s

You’re not a very good

smooth = (slang) well-

is speaking sarcastically) 
doofus = (slang) 
incompetent person
