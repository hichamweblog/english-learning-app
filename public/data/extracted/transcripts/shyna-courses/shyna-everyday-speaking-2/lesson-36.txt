Lesson 36: Immigration Debate 
Paul and Jennifer are brother and sister. They have different views about 
immigration. Listen to their conversation as you answer these questions: 
 
Listening Comprehension 
1. What is Paul’s view on immigration? 
a. He is in favor of it 
b. He is against it 
c. He is neutral 
 
2. Jennifer insinuates that Paul’s beliefs are affected by his… 
a. parents 
b. social status 
c. romantic relationship 
 
3. Paul's friend... 
a. hasn't been back to his country in thirty years 
b. tried unsuccessfully to get into the U.S. for thirty years 
c. was deported after thirty years in the U.S. 
 
4. Which of these is NOT mentioned as a negative point to 
immigration? 
a. Increased crime 
b. Overcrowding in cities 
c. Fewer jobs for citizens 
 
5. What is the solution proposed for the tax issue? 
a. Reduce the number of public services 
b. Increase taxes on native-born citizens 
c. Make illegal immigrants legal 


 
 
 
Conversation Text 
Paul: Mom and Dad can be so close-minded 
when it comes to certain issues. It drives me 
nuts. 
 
Jennifer: For instance…? 
 
Paul: Well, immigration is the obvious one. 
 
Jennifer: I guess that’s a touchy subject for you 
since your girlfriend’s from Guatemala.  
 
Paul: True, but my position on the topic would be 
the same either way. Hear me out – the United 
States was built by immigrants. It seems so 
hypocritical to now all of a sudden decide we can 
pick and choose who we’ll accept and who we’ll 
shut out.  
 
Jennifer: I think it’s probably more complicated 
than that. 
 
Paul: Seems pretty clear-cut to me. I mean – it’s 
not easy to immigrate to a new country with a 
strange language and leave your family behind. 
People don’t do it because they want to. They do it 
because they have to. Case in point – I know a guy

thirty years. He hasn’t seen his parents in all that 
time, because if he ever goes home, he’s not sure 
he can come back to the U.S. And since he just 
can’t make a living at home, he stays here.

Paul: Exactly. We shouldn’t be such a heartless 
nation that we refuse to let people in, and force them

Jennifer: Well… from what I’ve heard, there have

Paul: Hmm… that’s probably scaremongering by 
conservatives in order to support their points… but 
what have you heard? 
 
Jennifer: Basically, a large influx of immigrants can 
lead to a rise in poverty, an increase in crime and 
more crowding in the cities. Not to mention the

everybody? 
 
Paul: Yeah, but I think that those disaster scenarios 
are so far off we shouldn’t take them seriously – at 
least, not right now. The obvious solution is to make 
it easier to immigrate legally. People always 
complain that immigrants don’t pay taxes, right? 
There’s a simple fix to that – make them legal! 
Immigrants would be happy to pay taxes. It’d be a 
small price to pay in order to live without fear of 
getting deported, and the country would have 
increased revenue. Seems like a win-win to me.
