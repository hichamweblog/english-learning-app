Lesson 33: A Looming Deadline 
 
Ben and Anna are a boyfriend and girlfriend who live together. Listen to their 
conversation about tasks and deadlines, and mark the statements below “True” or 
“False.” 
 
Listening Comprehension 
 
True or false according to the dialogue? 
 
1. Ben and Anna have lived together for eight months 
2. Anna has already cleaned the bathroom 
3. The paper is due tomorrow 
4. Anna has no idea what she’s going to write about 
5. Anna has never wanted to change her habits 
6. Anna doesn't like history 
7. Ben offers to write the paper for her 
8. Ben suggests that she use rewards to motivate herself 
9. Anna thinks that system will work for her 
10. If she starts her next paper early, Ben will take her out to eat 
 
 


 
 
Conversation Text 
 
Ben: Are you… folding laundry? You never fold 
laundry. In the eight months we’ve lived 
together, I’ve always seen you just fling your 
clothes onto the bed. 
 
Anna: I have a paper to write. Nothing 
motivates me to do chores like an urgent 
deadline.  
 
Ben: Ah, that explains why the kitchen is 
spotless. 
 
Anna: Just wait until I take on the bathroom. 
 
Ben: You’d rather scrub our toilet than write a 
paper?! 
 
Anna: I’m just dreading it so bad.  
 
Ben: When’s it due? 
 
Anna: Tomorrow. 
 
Ben: Have you started? Tell me you’ve 
started… 
 
Anna: Kind of. I mean, I’ve got an outline, but 
when I try to actually write the darn thing, it’s 
like I get a mental block. So I get antsy and 
feel compelled to do something productive 
instead – like clean the house. 
looming = being present in a 
threatening way 
 
fling = throw casually and 
carelessly 
 
chores = tasks that must be 
done around the house 
 
spotless = perfectly clean 
 
scrub = rub something hard 
in order to clean it 
 
dreading = anticipating with 
great fear, hate, or 
reluctance 
 
so bad = (informal) very 
much 
 
outline = a plan for the main 
points of the paper 
 
a mental block = your 
thoughts stop and can’t 
proceed or develop 
 
antsy = impatient and 
restless, wanting to move or 
do something 
 
compelled = forced by 
powerful feelings 
Vocabulary 


 
 
 
Ben: Well, I’m not gonna complain about 
your cleaning frenzy, but… your time’s 
running out, and that paper’s not going to 
write itself. 
 
Anna: I know, I know. I told myself I’d stay 
on top of things this semester. I was going 
to get started early on all my assignments 
and not put them off to the last minute. 
 
Ben: So what happened? 
 
Anna: I just couldn’t keep up. My history 
professor kept piling on the homework, and 
I hate history, so I started procrastinating… 
and now the closer the deadline gets, the 
more I panic, and the more I avoid doing it. 
 
Ben: It’s a vicious cycle. But look at it this 
way – let’s say the paper will take you about 
four hours to write. You could either do it in 
a single day, get it over with, and get on 
with your life… or you could spend weeks 
agonizing over it and then still have to put in 
four hours, only now your back is against 
the wall and it’s even harder to concentrate. 
 
Anna: What you’re saying makes so much 
sense… but it’s a little late for that. I wish I’d 
listened to reason and started weeks ago. But 
that is not what happened, and now I’m at 
the end of my rope! 
 
frenzy = period of intense 
activity 
 
stay on top of things = stay 
updated on things and 
complete them on time 
 
put them off = delay, 
procrastinate 
 
keep up = accompany the 
progress 
 
piling on = giving a very large 
amount 
 
a vicious cycle = a situation 
where one bad thing causes 
another, and the second bad 
thing causes even more of the 
first, etc. 
 
get it over with = finish 
something unpleasant quickly 
 
agonizing = suffering mental 
worry or pain about something 
 
your back is against the wall = 
you’re in a difficult situation 
from which it’s impossible to 
escape 
 
I’m at the end of my rope! = 
desperate; don’t have any 
more energy or patience 
Vocabulary 


 
 
Ben: OK, first of all, step away from the laundry. Just 
sit down at your desk, and focus on writing the first 
sentence. Then the first paragraph. Before you know 
it, you’ll have the whole paper written.  
 
Anna: But what about the bathroom? It is filthy, you 
know… 
 
Ben: Right, because we haven’t cleaned it in weeks – 
so that means it can wait another day. Here’s an idea 
– how about from now on you try to bribe yourself 
into starting early on your assignments? 
 
Anna: How would I do that? 
 
Ben: Tell yourself that if you get a certain amount of 
work done by a certain day, then you can indulge in 
something you like. Sushi, maybe. 
 
Anna: I don’t know… I’d probably just miss the 
deadline and have the sushi anyway to reward 
myself for trying. 
 
Ben: Anna! 
 
Anna: What? I know my weaknesses.  
 
Ben: How about this? Next time you have an assignment, let me know and if you get 
started early, I’ll take you out for sushi as a reward. 
 
Anna: OK, but what can I do for you then? 
 
Ben: You can clean the bathroom to your heart’s content. 
 
filthy = extremely dirty 
 
bribe = get something 
you want by offering a 
reward/incentive 
 
indulge in = give in to 
one of your desires 
 
to your heart’s content 
= as much as you want
