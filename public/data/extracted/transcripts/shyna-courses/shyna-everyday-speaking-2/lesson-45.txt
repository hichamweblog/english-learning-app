Lesson 45: Cooking 
 
Listen to this conversation between Mandy and her father, Nikko, who is cooking. Mark 
the statements below “True” or “False.” 
 
 
Listening Comprehension 
 
True or false according to the dialogue? 
 
1. Nikko got the burrito recipe from a cookbook 
2. He used up all the eggs to make the burritos 
3. Nikko threw away the bacon 
4. Mandy doesn't want to eat beans for breakfast 
5. Tofu is a good source of protein 
6. Mandy refuses to try Nikko's burritos 
7. Nikko has seen health benefits from his new diet 
8. Mandy's mother was a vegan 
9. Nikko is also doing exercise

12. Mandy disapproves of Nikko and Sharon’s relationship 
 
 


 
 
Conversation Text 
 
Mandy: Morning, Dad! Mmm, something 
smells good! 
 
Nikko: I’m making vegan breakfast burritos – 
all organic. My friend Sharon taught me how to 
make them last week. She’s a real pro in the 
kitchen. 
 
Mandy: Ah, that’s great! But Dad, what 
happened to all the eggs in the fridge? I 
could’ve sworn we had at least four left. 
 
Nikko: I threw them away. Too much 
cholesterol.  
 
Mandy: Oh. So much for the omelet I wanted to 
make. Hey – the bacon’s gone, too! 
 
Nikko: Sorry, hon. I might’ve gotten a little 
overzealous when purging our food supplies. 
Health takes precedence, though. You really 
should try to go vegan sometime. It’ll help 
unclog your arteries. And Sharon says you can 
get all the protein you need from beans. 
 
Mandy: Maybe. I really don’t think I can 
stomach beans for breakfast, though. 
 
Nikko: If you knew how many rat hairs are 
allowed into each pound of processed meat, 
you’d be saying “good riddance” to the bacon. 
 
burritos = a type of Mexican 
food made of a flour tortilla 
wrapped around a filling 
 
pro = professional; very 
skilled 
 
I could’ve sworn = I was 
quite certain 
 
hon = short for “honey,” a 
term of affection 
 
overzealous = excessively 
enthusiastic 
 
purging = clearing an area of 
things that are unwanted 
 
takes precedence = is more 
important 
 
unclog = remove blockages 
 
stomach = tolerate, accept 
 
good riddance = it’s a good 
thing that’s gone
