Lesson 18: Childhood 
Nora and Jolene are chatting about their experiences as children. Listen to their 
conversation and choose the best answer for each question: 
Listening Comprehension 
1. Nora describes the accidents she had at... 
a. a birthday party 
b. her school 
c. the circus 
2. Jolene's case of mistaken identity happened at... 
a. a gas station 
b. a grocery store 
c. a restaurant 
3. When Jolene stayed up late, she used to... 
a. eat snacks 
b. play outside 
c. read in bed 
4. Nora got in trouble when she... 
a. appeared to be missing 
b. broke something inside a closet 
c. made too much noise and woke her parents up 
5. Jolene usually wasn't punished because... 
a. her behavior was good 
b. her father was lenient 
c. her parents didn't find out about what she did 
6. After school, Nora would... 
a. accompany her mother to various places 
b. attend sports practice 
c. do homework for several hours 
7. How did Nora feel about summer camp? 
a. she loved it 
b. she hated it 
c. she sometimes liked it, and sometimes didn't like it 
 


 
 
Conversation Text 
Nora: I was so clumsy when I was a kid. 
There was this one time my family went to the 
circus, and I remember tumbling down three 
flights of stairs, crashing into the popcorn 
seller, and finally colliding with a girl eating 
ice cream.  
Jolene: Yikes! Yeah, I was pretty 
uncoordinated, too. Did you ever mistake 
strangers for your parents? 
 
Nora: No. Did you? 
 
Jolene: A bunch of times. Once I was in the 
grocery store, and my mom was busy 
rearranging the food in our cart. I saw this 
dark-haired man leaning into a freezer to 
grab some frozen meals. He looked just like 
my dad from the back. So I ran up to him, 
hugged him and said “hi dad!” When he 
turned around, both of us were startled.  
 
Nora: I can imagine! Did you ever stay up 
past your bedtime when you were a kid? 
 
Jolene: You bet. I would turn off my lights, 
then go under my bed sheets with a flashlight 
to read or do coloring books.  
 
Nora: Sometimes me and my two brothers 
would sneak out of our rooms and play hide 
and seek in the dark. We’d have to wait till 
about midnight, to be sure my parents were 
sound asleep.  
clumsy = not graceful or 
physically coordinated; always 
having accidents 
tumbling down = falling in a 
disorganized way 
flights of stairs = each section 
of stairs going from one floor 
to another is called a “flight” 
Yikes! = an expression of 
surprise or mild shock 
A bunch of = several; many 
leaning = inclining the body 
startled = mildly frightened or 
shocked 
stay up = remain awake 
You bet = definitely 
sneak out = go out in a quiet, 
secret way 
hide and seek = a game 
where some people hide and 
one person looks for them 
sound asleep = sleeping 
deeply; not likely to wake up 
 
 
Vocabulary 


 
 
Jolene: Sounds like fun! But did you ever 
get in trouble? 
 
Nora: Yeah, one time when I dozed off 
while hiding in a closet. My brothers 
couldn’t find me, so they gave up and went 
to bed. Then in the morning, my mom saw I 
wasn’t in my room and she flipped out. 
 
 
Jolene: That’s funny! Well, not so much for 
your mom at the time. But I’m sure she was 
glad you were all right. 
 
Nora: Of course she was – but we still got 
punished. All three of us were grounded 
for a week – and we had to do extra chores.  
 
Jolene: I usually managed to weasel out of 
getting punished. My mom was pretty strict, 
but my dad was a total pushover. Every 
time I pleaded with him to let me off the 
hook, he’d eventually give in. It used to 
drive my mom up the wall. 
 
Nora: That definitely wouldn’t fly with my 
parents. When it came to decisions 
involving us kids, they backed each other 
up. 
 
dozed off = fell asleep 
gave up = stopped, desisted 
flipped out = became very upset 
and agitated 
glad = happy 
grounded = a punishment where 
kids/teenagers are not allowed 
to leave the house to meet their 
friends or do fun things 
chores = obligatory tasks 
(especially housework) 
weasel out of = escape an 
obligation in a deceptive way 
a pushover = someone who is 
easily persuaded 
pleaded with = asked with a lot 
of emotion 
give in = stop resisting and agree 
to do what someone else wants 
you to 
drive my mom up the wall = 
make her very annoyed 
wouldn’t fly = wouldn’t work 
backed each other up = 
supported each other

Jolene: Sometimes I wish I could turn back 
the clock and be a kid again. I used to roam 
around the neighborhood without a care 
in the world.  
Nora: Not me. After picking me up from 
school, my mom would drag me along on 
all her errands.  
Jolene: Bummer. What about during the 
summers, did you ever go to summer camp? 
Nora: Every year! I think my parents sent 
us to camp just to get us out of their hair. 
But it was awesome, we got to do stuff like 
horseback riding, canoeing, having water 
balloon fights, the whole nine yards.  
Jolene: Those were the days, that’s for 
sure! 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
turn back the clock = go back to 
how things were in the past 
roam around = move around a 
large area without any particular 
direction 
without a care in the world = 
completely free of worries 
drag me along = take me when I 
wasn’t willing to go 
errands = short trips to perform 
specific tasks for daily life (going 
to the supermarket, bank, post 
office, etc.) 
Bummer. = That’s unpleasant 
get us out of their hair = get us 
away so we would stop 
bothering them 
the whole nine yards = and 
everything included in an 
experience 
those were the days = that was 
a good time in the past
