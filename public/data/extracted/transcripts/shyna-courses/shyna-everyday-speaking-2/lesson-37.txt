Lesson 37: Different Tastes 
 
Two friends, Grace and Erica, discuss their preferences. Listen to their conversation 
and mark the statements below “True” or “False”: 
 
Listening Comprehension 
True or false according to the conversation? 
1. Grace had a very busy weekend 
2. Erica went to the Lucky Lark with her boyfriend 
3. There is plenty of space inside the café  
4. Erica was not pleased with the service 
5. Erica was given the wrong dish twice 
6. Grace thinks the Dragon's Den is awful 
7. Erica especially likes the pizza there 
8. The Dragon's Den is always full during the day 
9. Erica has never tried Mexican food 
10. Erica agrees to have lunch with Grace 
 
 


 
 
Conversation Text 
 
Erica: Hey Grace. How was your weekend? 
 
Grace: Mmm, pretty uneventful. We spent most 
of it tinkering with our new sound system, trying 
to get it all set up. Oh! We did stop by that new 
café near the library – it’s called the Lucky Lark. 
Have you heard of it? 
 
Erica: Yeah, my boyfriend dragged me there last 
week. Not my cup of tea. 
 
Grace: What? Why? The seating area’s a little 
cramped, but we thought it was charming. I 
loved all the old movie posters on the walls. And 
the food was terrific. 
 
Erica: I don’t know, it just seems like it’s trying 
too hard to be trendy. And when we went, our 
server seemed absent-minded – she forgot our 
drinks twice.  
 
Grace: Well, the place is new, and I’m sure they’re 
still trying to work out the kinks in the system.  
 
uneventful = not busy, 
not much happened 
 
tinkering = making small 
attempts to fix or change 
a machine or system 
 
stop by = visit quickly 
 
dragged me = brought me 
when I didn’t want to go 
 
not my cup of tea = I 
didn’t like it 
 
cramped = there is little 
space 
 
charming = nice, 
enjoyable, pleasant 
 
trendy = fashionable, cool 
 
absent-minded = 
forgetful, distracted 
 
work out the kinks = 
resolve the small 
problems or mistakes 
 
Vocabulary 


 
 
Erica: While they’re at it, they might want to 
pay a little more attention to their food. It was 
hit and miss – the pasta my boyfriend 
ordered was all right, but I got a sandwich and 
the bread was stale.  
 
Grace: So why didn’t you send it back? 
 
Erica: Eh, we were already running late, so I 
just decided to grin and bear it. I just don’t 
get how they can charge so much for crummy 
service and food that’s only so-so.  
 
Grace: I thought it was worth every penny. 
They have a really creative cocktail menu, 
and we thought all the food was really 
inventive and fresh. We got this amazing 
cucumber and peach salad, steamed brussels 
sprouts with apple sauce, and stuffed 
butternut squash.  
 
Erica: To each their own. I’d rather go to the 
Dragon’s Den any day. 
 
Grace: Ugh, that hole in the wall? It’s so dark 
and grungy. 
 
While they’re at it = while 
they are doing that 
 
hit and miss = sometimes 
good, sometimes bad; not 
consistently good 
 
stale = if bread is stale, it 
means it is old and hard, not 
very fresh 
 
grin and bear it = stay in a 
good mood while enduring a 
bad situation  
 
crummy = bad 
 
so-so = mediocre, not very 
good 
 
worth every penny = valuable 
enough to justify the price 
 
cocktail = mixed drinks (ex. 
alcohol and fruit juice) 
 
steamed = cooked with water 
vapor 
 
to each their own = every 
person has their own 
preferences 
 
hole in the wall = a small 
place 
 
grungy = dirty, smelly 
Vocabulary 


 
 
Erica: But they have huge cheeseburgers, and the 
best chicken wings in the state. And the service is 
super-fast. 
 
Grace: Probably because you’re the only one in 
there. It’s a mystery to me how that place even 
keeps the lights on. 
 
Erica: They pull a big late-night crowd. Things start 
picking up around midnight. 
 
Grace: Ah, that makes sense. People who are 
wasted aren’t as picky about their food. And they 
don’t care if the kitchen is infested with 
cockroaches. 
 
Erica: All I know is that you can eat your fill for less 
than ten bucks, and the people who hang out there 
aren’t pretentious.  
 
Grace: You don’t have to be pretentious to enjoy 
healthy food. Do they even serve salad at the 
Dragon’s Den? 
 
Erica: They put lettuce and tomato on the burgers. 
 
Grace: That doesn’t count. Is that all you’re willing 
to eat? Burgers? 
keeps the lights on = 
stays in business (has 
enough money to pay 
the bills, such as the 
electric bill) 
 
things start picking up 
= the amount of activity 
starts increasing 
 
wasted = (slang) drunk 
 
picky = selective 
 
infested = full of insects 
 
eat your fill = eat as 
much as you need to 
satisfy your hunger 
 
pretentious = claiming 
or behaving as if you 
are superior to others 
 
 
Vocabulary 


 
 
 
Erica: Nope. I like Mexican food, too. 
 
Grace: OK. I know a Mexican place downtown, 
wanna go there for lunch? 
 
Erica: Depends. Do they have normal Mexican food? 
None of this fusion cuisine nonsense? 
 
Grace: No fusion, but it’s mostly a vegan Mexican 
place. 
 
Erica: Oh. Then I’ll pass. I’m not eating a taco full of 
radishes. I’ll be hungry again in an hour. 
 
Grace: It’s not totally vegan. They have fish tacos. 
Come on… my treat. 
 
Erica: Well… all right. I’ll give this place a try. Hope 
they have good guacamole. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
downtown = in the city 
center 
 
fusion = 
combining/mixing two 
things 
 
nonsense = something 
that is silly and not to 
be taken seriously 
 
vegan = not 
using/eating any animal 
products at all 
 
I’ll pass = I won’t 
accept this 
invitation/opportunity 
 
my treat = I’ll pay for 
you
