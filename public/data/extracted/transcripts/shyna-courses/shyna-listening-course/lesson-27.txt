Activity 1 – Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
insatiable (adj.) = impossible to be satisfied. This word is often used to describe 
desire, hunger, appetite, curiosity, etc. 
Ex) Pete has an insatiable appetite – he ate an entire pizza by himself and still 
had room for dessert! 
burden (n.) = a heavy weight (may be an object that is carried, but is often used 
metaphorically to describe a responsibility that is stressful or difficult to endure) 
Ex) Many middle-aged adults shoulder the burden of caring for both their 
young children and their elderly parents. 
proponent (n.) = someone who supports a cause and encourages others to be in 
favor of it, too 
Ex) Proponents of education are pressuring the government to invest more 
funds in the country’s schools. 
cluttered (adj.) = filled with many, disorganized objects 
Ex) My desk is so cluttered that I can’t even find the documents I put here 
yesterday. 
upkeep (n.) = maintenance 
 
Ex) Old cars are inexpensive to buy, but the upkeep costs a fortune! 
amateur (adj.) = a person who does an activity but not professionally; can also be 
used to describe someone with little experience 
 
Ex) My brother works as an accountant; he’s also an amateur photographer. 


crowdfunding (n.) = when there is an online campaign to get donations from many 
people in order to complete a project. Crowdfunding campaigns are often spread 
through social media. 
Ex) She couldn’t pay her medical bills, so she started a crowdfunding campaign 
to raise money for her treatment. 
loft (n.) = an upper level in a building which has one 
side open to the lower floor. It is often used for 
sleeping or storage. 
salvage (v.) = to save objects from being thrown 
away or destroyed 
Ex) The fire destroyed our entire house; I was 
only able to salvage a few family photos. 
devastating (adj.) = describes something that causes serious destruction. May be 
literal destruction (as in a war or natural disaster) or emotional destruction. 
 
Ex) The devastating force of the hurricane winds ripped the roof off the church.  
Ex) She received the devastating news that her children had died in the 
accident.

Complete Transcript 
Owning a home is part of the "American dream" - but the size of that dream has 
expanded over the years. The average American single-family home is now 40% 
larger than it was three decades ago - despite a decrease in the size of the average 
family.  
This insatiable desire for more space per person has led to some disastrous 
consequences - families take out ambitious mortgages that will take fifteen to thirty 
years to pay off, thus locking themselves into a cycle of debt that may be impossible 
to escape. In fact, banks' extension of easy credit to borrowers who could not afford 
the repayments was one of the causes of the 2007 economic crisis. 
The wake-up call of the recession, coupled with the realization that a growing 
population and consumption of environmental resources are very real problems, 
are both factors that have led to the rise of the tiny house movement - a group of 
individuals and families who pare down their possessions and live in miniature 
homes measuring just 100-400 square feet. To optimize space, tiny houses make use 
of dual-purpose features, such as drawers built into the side of a staircase.  
The financial burden of building and owning a tiny house is certainly much lighter. 
Tiny houses cost an average of twenty to fifty thousand dollars, as compared to 
nearly three hundred thousand for a traditional house. Proponents of the 
movement say they enjoy the peace of mind that comes from living in a simpler, less 
cluttered environment. Less time and mental energy needs to be spent on upkeep, 
and residents of tiny houses can focus more on the things that matter most, such as 
family, hobbies, travel, and leisure. The tiny house movement has attracted a wide 
range of people in the U.S., and it is now gaining increased media coverage and an 
international following as well. 
Tiny houses are often self-built, offering the chance to stamp your identity on your 
home's architecture, layout and design. They can be built from scratch or using a 
trailer as a shell, and can be completed in as little as two months. Tiny houses are 
often built on wheels so they can be ‘transplanted’ to a new location at will - and 
they do not require a building permit. In response to safety concerns about amateur 


builders, workshops teaching safe construction techniques have sprung up across 
the nation.  
Recently in the news was 13-year-old Sicily Kolbeck, who started building her own 
tiny house together with her father. In the middle of the project, he died tragically in 
a car accident – but with the help of local tiny house enthusiasts and a 
crowdfunding campaign, Sicily managed to finish her very own dark blue tiny house. 
It is made entirely of donated and salvaged materials and includes a bathroom, 
kitchen, loft, and solar panels.  
Sicily says she wanted to not only inspire people to live more simply, but also to 
show that it is possible to get right back up and keep going even after life deals you a 
devastating blow. 
 
 
Extra Vocabulary 
“families take out ambitious mortgages that will take fifteen to thirty years to pay 
off” 
A mortgage is a loan from the bank specifically for the purposes of buying a 
house. 
“The wake-up call of the recession” 
 
A wake-up call is a sign or warning that alerts you to a dangerous situation. 
"The tiny house movement has attracted a wide range of people in the U.S." 
This means it has attracted a lot of people who are of many different types, 
social classes, etc. 
"They can be built from scratch" 
To make something from scratch means to make it from the beginning, from 
the very basic ingredients. We usually talk about making food from scratch. 
"even after life deals you a devastating blow." 
A “blow” is another word for a violent impact, and “deals” in this context 
means “gives.”
