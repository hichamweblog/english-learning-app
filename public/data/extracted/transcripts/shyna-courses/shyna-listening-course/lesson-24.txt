Activity 1 – Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
yards (n.) = in the United States, the measurement system is different. Twelve 
inches equals one foot, and three feet equal one yard. A yard is just a little bit 
smaller than a meter. 
offense/defense (n.) = the part of a sports team that tries to score points is the 
offense, and the part of a sports team that tries to stop the other team from scoring 
is the defense. 
tackle (v.) = to grab someone and throw them to the ground, using the weight of 
your own body to do this.  
 
intercept (v.) = get something meant for someone else in the middle of its path. For 
example, if a player throws the ball to a teammate, but someone from the opposing 
team comes in and catches the ball in the middle, the opposing team has intercepted 
the ball. 


referee (n.) = the official who controls the stopping and starting of the game, and 
applies the rules during the game. Referees often wear striped shirts: 
 
season (n.) = the period of time (a period of months during the year) during which 
a sport is played 
in the lead = if a team or player is “in the lead,” it means they are winning… but that 
the game or competition is not yet over 
leap (v.) = jump very high or very far 
helmet (n.) = protective equipment used for your head in sports, while riding a 
bicycle, etc. In the tackle photo above, the boys are wearing black and white helmets

Complete Transcript 
I want to tell you about my favorite sport, American football. Now this game has a 
lot of rules, and I have been watching it for over ten years - and I still don't 
understand every single rule of the game. But I will try to do my best to explain it to 
you in this brief amount of time. 
I think it's easiest if I compare it to soccer. In soccer you have a field, and at the end 
of the field there are two goals, and each team is trying to protect their own goal 
while trying to put the soccer ball into the other team's goal to score a point. 
Football's the same way. The field is 100 yards long, and at either end there is an 
area known as the end zone. Now there is no goal and nets, but if a player manages 
to cross into the end zone while holding the football, they score a touchdown. In 
football, a touchdown is worth six points.  
The goal of the defense is to prevent the offense from moving down the field - and 
they can do this by tackling, and they can do this by intercepting the football - thus 
making them into the offense. The goal of the offense, aside from trying to score a 
touchdown, is to move the ball at least 10 yards from the last legal spot of the ball. 
For example, the referee will position the football at the 20-yard line. The offense 
must get the ball to at least the 30-yard line. The offense only has four chances to 
move the ball those ten yards. These chances are known as "downs" in football. If 
the offense fails to move the football at least 10 yards in those four tries, then they 
have to turn the ball over to the other team, who then goes on offense.  
One of my favorite teams in football is the Cincinnati Bengals. And the only reason 
why this is, is because I was born in Cincinnati Ohio, so I have to root for the home 
team, right? But as I learned more about football, I started to follow other teams. 
And one of my other favorite teams is the New York Giants. There were a lot of 
players on there that I really liked, and so that's what drew me to them. Most of 
them have left, but the quarterback Eli Manning still plays for the Giants. 
One of the most memorable plays that I can recall happened when the Giants faced 
the New England Patriots in Super Bowl 42. The Super Bowl is the championship 
game for the entire football season. The game was a nail-biter; it was going back and 
forth, back and forth. First the Giants were in the lead, then the Patriots.  


In the last two minutes of the game, the Patriots were ahead 14-10, and the Giants 
had to score if they wanted a chance to win. Remember the set of downs that I told 
you about? Well, the Giants failed to move those ten yards on their first three downs, 
so this was one of those situations where they were going to go for it on fourth 
down because if they did not, they were going to lose the game.  
When the quarterback gets the ball at the beginning of a play, ideally they want to 
throw the ball within three to five seconds. Eli Manning had the ball for over 
fourteen seconds, and was nearly tackled three times. The third time, he managed 
to throw the ball - and it sailed really high into the air. And one of his receivers had 
to leap really high into the air.  
And he caught the ball with one hand and brought it down to his helmet. He 
couldn't use his other hand because one of the Patriots defenders had it, and 
pushed him over while he was still in mid-air. So the Giants player sandwiched the 
football between his hand and his helmet, and hit the ground right on his back. And 
miraculously, he did not drop the ball. And this set the Giants up to win the game 
and the Super Bowl, and become champions for that year.  
It was amazing - I think most people were rooting for the Giants because the 
Patriots had already won three Super Bowls in that decade, and a lot of people 
didn't want to see them win the fourth. And sadly for the Patriots, in addition to 
losing the Super Bowl, they lost their perfect season because they had not lost a 
game up until the Super Bowl.

Extra Vocabulary 
“so I have to root for the home team, right?” 
To “root for” a team means you are a fan of that team, you are cheering for 
that team and hoping it will win. The “home team” is the local team in a 
particular city. 
“The game was a nail-biter; it was going back and forth, back and forth.” 
“A nail-biter” is a situation that makes you full of tension and anxiety, because 
you do not know what the result will be, and it could change at any moment. 
When he says it was “going back and forth” it means that first one team had 
the advantage, then the other team, then the first team again, etc. In other 
words, the two teams were very equally matched, so everyone was nervous to 
see the final result! 
“it sailed really high into the air.” 
The verb “sail” is usually used for a sailboat moving through water, but it can 
also be used to describe something moving effortlessly through the air.
