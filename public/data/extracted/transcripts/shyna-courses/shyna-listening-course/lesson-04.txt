PART 1: JULIE TALKS ABOUT EXERCISE MISTAKES 
Activity 1: Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
cardio (n.) – Short for “cardiovascular” exercise, meaning exercise that makes your 
heart beat much faster. For example, running. 
metabolism (n.) – the processes of how your body stays alive – processing 
nutrients, giving energy, growing, etc. 
carbohydrates (n.) – a type of nutrient found especially in bread, rice, pasta, grains, 
and sugars. 
protein (n.) – a type of nutrient found especially in meat, fish, eggs, milk, and beans. 
sore (adj.) – when your muscles hurt after exercising (or after an injury) 
fuel (n./v.) – a substance that provides power/energy

Complete Transcript 
There are a couple of common exercise and fitness mistakes I see regularly. One of 
those mistakes is too much cardio. If you really wanna see great results, you have to 
diversify. For instance, three days a week do weight training; three days a week, do 
cardio.  
Weight training is so important because it builds your foundation of muscle, and 
that muscle dictates your metabolism. As we get older, we start to lose muscle mass 
naturally, so by keeping up your weight training, you prevent that from happening.  
To me, the best schedule is alternating between weight training and cardio. Monday, 
Wednesday, Friday - are great days to do weight training. Tuesday, Thursday, and 
Saturday, do your cardio. Always make sure you take a rest day. 
Speaking of rest, after 30 minutes of an intense workout, you should have a 
recovery drink. The recovery drink is a 4:1 ratio of carbohydrates to protein. For 
example, if your drink has 40 grams of carbohydrates, it should have 10 grams of 
protein - like chocolate milk.  
Without really getting into the details of the science behind the recovery drink, 
basically you won't be as sore and you'll have more energy the next day.  
Lastly, the biggest mistake is in the kitchen. Yes, that's right - it's in the kitchen. You 
can't out-work a bad diet. If you're not seeing the results that you want in your 
fitness, then make sure you're fueling your body like the hard-working machine it 
is.

way to say it is “out of shape.” The opposite is “in good shape” or simply “in shape” – 
meaning you have a good fitness level; you are strong and healthy.  
lungs (n.) = the organs inside your chest that help you breathe

Complete Transcript 
I'm answering the question, "Do you like to exercise?" Well, I like to exercise, but I 
don't do it as often as I should. I only ride my bike from time to time, every once in a 
while. Last week, for example, I had my first surf class, and the teacher was amazed 
when I told him I didn't exercise regularly. He told me what everybody knows - we 
need to exercise to live longer.  
Later on, when I was at the sea, I realized I was in bad shape. I got tired very, very 
quickly. After four or five attempts, I could no longer get up on the board.  
But back to the question - I always feel good after exercise. It seems that it opens 
the lungs, right? We breathe better, we get more alert with the eyes wide open. We 
feel physically tired, but happy. I guess I should do more exercise. 
And I mainly like team sports. Running alone or even riding a bike are okay, but I 
like team sports such as football - or soccer as they say in the United States - like 
handball and basketball.  
I feel that team sports have a different competitive side. They are a collective game 
with many people working together to win something. In this case, sweat has a 
purpose - to win.  
Well, but either way - please exercise. You end up always winning. You win years of 
life.
