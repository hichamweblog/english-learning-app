Lesson 32: Grandparents 
Warm-Up: 
Write a little bit about your grandparents. What are they like? What do you know 
about their lives? 
 
 
 
Activity 1 – Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
top-notch (adj.) = excellent, great quality 
 
Ex) The report was very well-researched; it was some top-notch journalism. 
frail (adj.) = physically weak, delicate 
Ex) My uncle used to be a very strong man, so it was a shock to see how frail the 
disease had made him.  
neglect (v.) = ignore, fail to care for 
Ex) She neglected the house plants, forgetting to water them for weeks, and 
they finally died. 
come around (phrasal verb) = change your opinion or position 
Ex) The president of the company didn’t want to invest in training for the 
employees, but he came around once he understood that it would likely result 
in higher profits. 
well-off (adj.) = financially comfortable, moderately rich 
 
Ex) That private school is full of kids from well-off families. 
rave about (phrasal verb) = say wonderful things about 
 
Ex) The new movie is apparently really good; all my friends are raving about it. 
golden years (n.) = the years of retirement, usually after age 65 
Ex) My father has worked hard his entire life, so he’s looking forward to having 
more time to pursue his hobbies during his golden years.

Complete Transcript 
My grandmother - my mother's mother - is 93 years old, but people often guess that 
she's no older than 70 or 75. Honestly I think one reason she looks so young is 
because of her positive attitude; she's just a really happy and vibrant person. I think 
some people, once they start getting older, they sort of give up on life - but not her, 
she really enjoys every day and loves having new experiences. 
My grandma lives in a senior living facility where she has her own apartment. She 
brought all her own furniture so she was able to set it up to look and feel like home, 
and the housekeeping cleans her place every day, so she doesn’t have to do any 
housework.  
The center offers all sorts of activities - classes, concerts, movies... there's also a 
library on the premises, an exercise room, a game room, and a dining hall that 
serves three meals a day. It's a really top-notch place, and she says she feels like 
she's living like a queen! 
But the decision to move there wasn't easy for her. After my grandfather died, she 
was alone in her house which was pretty far from my parents' – it was an hour and 
a half away. And my parents were worried that if she took a spill or had an accident, 
she wouldn't be able to get help. She’s in great health, but she is getting kinda frail, 
as expected at her age. Also she was starting to get pretty isolated because she was 
driving less and less, and many of her old friends had either moved out of the area 
or else passed away. 
So my parents were trying to convince her to move to this facility, but she was very 
resistant to it. I think the reason was not so much that she was emotionally 
attached to her house – I mean, I'm sure that played a part, too – and more that she 
thought this senior living place would be one where the residents are neglected and 
have nothing to do.  


I could understand why she was under this impression because this kind of facility 
is a relatively recent invention; places like this didn’t exist in the past, you know, 
where the residents are still pretty independent but there’s care available if they 
need it. Eventually my grandmother came around after visiting the place, seeing it 
with her own eyes, and talking with some of the people who live there. 
The actual move took a while because she had lived in the same house for over 40 
years, and she grew up during the Great Depression, a time when nothing was 
wasted and nothing was thrown away – so she had just accumulated a ton of stuff 
and she had to go through it all and choose what she wanted to keep, what to sell, 
what to donate, and what to just get rid of.  
Once she moved in to this senior apartment, though, she loved it, and three years 
later she’s still raving about it. Now, the place is pricey. My grandparents weren’t 
especially well-off, but they did manage their money well and they were able to 
save up enough to take care of themselves in their golden years. 
 
 
Extra Vocabulary 
"She's a really happy and vibrant person." 
 
The word “vibrant” means full of energy. 
"there's also a library on the premises" 
The word “premises” means in the building or on the land owned by the 
company/organization. When used in this way, it’s always plural. 


"My parents were worried that if she took a spill or had an accident, she wouldn't 
be able to get help." 
“Take a spill” is an informal way to say “fall down.” It’s only used for people, 
not for objects falling.
