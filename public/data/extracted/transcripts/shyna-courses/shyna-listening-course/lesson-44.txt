Warm-Up 
If you were the president of your country, what 
would you change, and why? How do you think the 
population would react to your changes? 
 
 
 
Activity 1 - Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
mentality (n.) = a way of thinking 
 
Ex) You'll never be successful with such a negative mentality. 
blanket statement (n.) = a generalized statement which doesn't account for 
exceptions or possible variations 
 
Ex) Yes, Sarah got into an accident, but you can't just make a blanket 
 
statement and say "all women are terrible drivers." 
exploit (v.) = make use of something in a selfish way - which benefits you, but not 
the person/thing you're using 
 
Ex) The colonizers exploited slave labor to build their cities. 
incentivize (v.) = motivate someone to do something by offering an 
incentive/reward 
Ex) We want to incentivize good behavior, so every time my son cleans his 
room, we take him out for ice cream. 


hold accountable (v.) = to "hold someone accountable" for something is to put the 
responsibility on them and/or check frequently to make sure they are acting 
correctly and taking care of that responsibility 
Ex) My personal trainer holds me accountable for going to the gym three 
times a week. If not for her, I'd probably never go! 
term (n.) = the length of time an elected official can stay in power. 
 
Ex) U.S. senators serve six-year terms. 
stagnant (adj.) = not moving, showing little activity (has a negative connotation) 
 
Ex) The economy has been stagnant, with hardly any growth in the past  year. 
complacent (adj.) = comfortable and satisfied (especially with your own 
accomplishments) so that you relax and don't work as hard 
Ex) We've received a million dollars of investment for our startup company, but 
this is no time to be complacent - now is when the real work begins!

Complete Transcript 
I think the main problem with my country is that it has become weak both as a 
nation and as a people. First, I would work domestically to improve the lives of the 
citizens. One cannot expect a nation to be strong if the citizens are weak. 
What I mean by this is that I believe the mentality of the American citizen today is 
that they should be able to have everything without working hard for it. Now, this 
does not cover every citizen - blanket statements never do. But I think a majority of 
people are starting to believe that they should get something for nothing.  
This is evident in systems like welfare, food stamps, Medicare and Medicaid - these 
systems are being exploited more and more every day, to the point where they're 
not only bankrupting the country, but they are making slaves out of people who do 
not even realize that they have become addicted to these programs. I would keep 
these programs in place, but I would make them as unattractive as possible to be 
on. Far too many people get into these programs and never get out. 
I have a little bit of experience with this, as my family is on food stamps right now. 
What started out as extra money every month to help us get to a better place has 
become something that we now would be in serious trouble if we did not have. In 
order for us to move beyond food stamps, not only would we have to make what we 
get in food stamps every month, we would have to make even more than that to 
make it worth our while. If I go out and expend effort only to make what I was 
getting for free - most people would not bother. 
Another thing that has happened with food stamps is that they are no longer food 
stamps; they are credit cards, which makes them less embarrassing to use. Now, I do 
not want to embarrass people; I am not saying that I would take those things away - 
but I don't want to incentivize people to accept that this is their lot in life. I want 
these programs to be safety nets and not ways of living. 
Another way that I would help empower the people is to reduce the scope of 
government; I believe in a limited government. This nation was founded upon the 
idea of freedom of people, and the federal government has expanded far beyond its 
powers than it was ever intended to be. Most of the power of the government was 
supposed to be tied up in state and local governments.  


The reason this is, is that the closer the people are to the government, the more 
accountable they can hold their government in both the laws that they pass and the 
way that they spend their money. The farther away you are from the money that is 
being spent, the less likely that you are to do anything about it. It doesn't seem real 
to you. 
Another thing that I would do is to put term limits on every elected official. There 
have been people in the government for 40 and 50 years of their life. They have 
grown stagnant and complacent; they are more focused on gaining power than they 
are in making the lives of average Americans better.  
By placing term limits on these people, they will not be focused on their long-term 
gain of power, but instead focused on what they can do for their terms that they 
have been elected for, which will be limited to one or two - just like the president of 
the United States. He can only be elected for a total of two terms. 
I believe these are some of the major points that I would address as president. I 
would have a hard sell, because there would be a lot of short-term pain, and people 
are always operating in the here and now more than in the future - and that's why I 
believe my country has run into these problems. So I don't know that I would be a 
popular president in the present.  
But if I were elected and was able to implement these programs and these changes, 
I believe that future generations would look to me and applaud me and thank me 
for having the courage to do what so many government officials do not have the 
courage to do. So thanks for listening to me, and if I ever run for president, I hope I 
have your vote! 
 
 
 
 
 


Extra Vocabulary 
"This is evident in systems like welfare, food stamps, Medicare and Medicaid" 
 
The word "welfare" is a general term referring to government assistance 
 
for poor people. "Medicare" and "Medicaid" are the names of two U.S.-
 
government-sponsored health care programs. 
"I don't want to incentivize people to accept that this is their lot in life." 
 
Someone's "lot in life" means their general circumstances and condition 
 
in life, which was destined for them to have. 
"I would have a hard sell, because there would be a lot of short-term pain, and 
people are always operating in the here and now more than in the future" 
If something is a "hard sell," it means it's very difficult to convince people to 
accept it. The expression "in the here and now" means in the present 
moment; it's often used to describe when people are focusing only on current 
things and not thinking about the future.
