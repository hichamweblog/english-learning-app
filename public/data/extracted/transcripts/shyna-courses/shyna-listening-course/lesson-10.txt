Lesson 10: Life in New York City 
Warm-Up: 
What are some of the things you love/hate about where you currently live? Do you 
now live in the same city where you grew up, or have you moved? 
 
 
 
 
Activity 1 – Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
obscure (adj.) = not well-known – the opposite of famous/popular 
Ex) She did her research on the work of an obscure 17th-century poet; it was 
hard for her to find any information on his life. 
vibe (n. – slang) = a feeling that you get from a person, situation, or environment 
Ex) Even though I didn’t know anybody at the party, it had a great vibe because 
everyone was very welcoming. 
on the flip side (n. – slang) = considering the opposite side of the issue (like the 
expression “on the other hand”) 
Ex) Working from home is nice because there are very few distractions – but on 
the flip side, it can get lonely at times. 
overwhelming (adj.) = so intense that it makes you feel weak and powerless, like it 
is too much for you to accept or handle. 
Ex) My history professor wants us to read two 300-page books before the exam 
next week – AND prepare a presentation! This class is so overwhelming.

Complete Transcript 
The best part about living in New York City is that anything you could possibly 
want, you can find there. There are so many different cultures and subcultures. You 
can try food from pretty much any country or ethnicity on the planet, and it's just a 
train ride away. Any type of activity or interest – no matter how unusual or how 
obscure – I’m sure you can find it in New York. The city has something for everyone. 
It's impossible to be bored. And even though it's one city, every neighborhood is 
different, they all have their own unique kind of vibe. 
Another thing I loved about New York City is that it has a special kind of energy 
about it, it's very dynamic. And I think part of this comes from the fact that a lot of 
people in the city aren't originally from there. So not only do you have a lot of 
immigrants from other countries, but also people from other places in the U.S. who 
have moved to New York City - and they usually have some sort of goal or mission 
that they’re trying to accomplish, whether for their career or for personal reasons... 
so New York is just full of go-getters and people who are doing exciting things. You 
won't really find a lot of people who are passive and just let life pass them by. 
Now, the flip side is that living in New York can stress you out if you're not careful. 
They call it “the city that never sleeps” because there is something going on 24/7, 
and it's just pretty fast-paced in general. So that could be a shock if you're not used 
to it – people walk fast, they expect fast customer service, and everything to be 
really snappy and efficient. So if you’re living at this speed and you also have a 
demanding job, then you really need to consciously make room in your schedule to 
relax and plan to have some downtime. 
And then there’s financial stress, too, because New York is definitely pricey. Mainly 
rent – like, compared to other places in the country, you'll pay three times as much 
for a ridiculously small apartment. But it’s not out of reach, you just have to budget 
and be adaptable… and you probably won’t be spending much time in your 
apartment, anyway, so in my opinion, it’s worth it. 
If you're thinking of living in New York, my main suggestion is to get plugged in to 
some sort of group, club, or community, or take up a new activity to meet people, 
‘cause otherwise the city can be overwhelming and you can end up feeling kind of 
isolated. On the streets people usually keep to themselves. Everyone minds their 
own business, so at first glance it seems like New Yorkers are pretty closed off – but 
it’s way easier to make friends if you join a group or take a class, you know? You 


should definitely take advantage of the chance to do something you've never done 
before, maybe that you’ve always wanted to try, and get outside your comfort zone 
a little bit. 
 
Extra Vocabulary 
“There are so many different cultures and subcultures” 
A “subculture” is a distinct smaller cultural group within a larger cultural 
group. Subcultures usually form around particular activities, interests, or 
beliefs, and have their own typical style, appearances, and slang/expressions. 
“New York is just full of go-getters” 
A “go-getter” is a person who takes action and makes things happen.  
“and everything to be really snappy and efficient.” 
 
“Snappy” is an informal word meaning “quick and energetic.” 
“plan to have some downtime.” 
 
“Downtime” means a period of rest, relaxation, and little activity. 
“on the streets people usually keep to themselves. Everyone minds their own 
business” 
Both “keep to themselves” and “minds their own business” are ways of saying 
that people don’t interact very much with others; they don’t get involved in 
other people’s situations. 
“get outside your comfort zone a little bit.” 
Your “comfort zone” is the collection of activities/situations that you are 
already comfortable with, so to “get outside your comfort zone” means to do 
something that challenges you or makes you a little uncomfortable.

Complete Transcript 
I moved from Sydney to New York City about ten months ago. Even though I come 
from a city, it's nothing compared to NYC. The very first thing I noticed was how 
fast-paced everything was. Everyone was always in a rush, no one had time to just 
stop, relax, and just enjoy their day. 
One time I made a suggestion to my husband for us to open up a car wash cafe, just 
like all the cafes that are all over Sydney. And he's born and bred in Brooklyn - he 
laughed and he said "No one in New York is gonna have time to just sit and sip a 
coffee while their car is being washed. We need our car washed in record time, so 
we can get to where we need to be." 
There's always somewhere you need to be; there's always something to do. That's 
why it's called the city of dreams. There's opportunities around every corner and if 
you slow down, the next person's gonna get ahead of you. 
 
Extra Vocabulary: 
"he's born and bred in Brooklyn" 
This is an informal expression meaning “born and raised” – her husband was 
born in Brooklyn and also grew up there. 
"There's opportunities around every corner" 
A “corner” is the place next to an intersection, where two roads come 
together. The expression “on every corner” or “around every corner” means 
that something is very common/frequent in that area.
