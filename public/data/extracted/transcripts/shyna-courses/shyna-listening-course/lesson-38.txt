Lesson 38: Vacation 
Warm-Up: 
Describe a vacation you’ve taken in the past – or a dream 
vacation you would like to take in the future! 
 
 
 
 
Activity 1 – Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
a ton of = this is an informal way to say “a lot of”; a large number or amount 
 
Ex) A ton of people were trying to get the celebrity’s autograph. 
thrilling (adj.) = very exciting 
Ex) This book tells the thrilling story of one man’s escape from a war-torn 
country. 
wetlands (n.) = a low area where the ground is saturated with water – also called a 
“marsh” or a “swamp” 
 
packed (adj.) = very crowded or full to capacity 
Ex) The movie theater was packed on the opening night for the film. There 
wasn’t a single empty seat. 
 


overbook (v.) = when an airline, hotel, or event takes more reservations than it has 
places available 
Ex) The hotel was overbooked, so they transferred our reservation to another 
hotel in a different neighborhood. 
voucher (n.) = a certificate that allows you to claim some credit or benefit 
 
Ex) The government gives meal vouchers to low-income families. 
bankrupt (adj.) = when a person or company is in complete financial ruin, having 
no more money and not able to pay its debts 
Ex) The business went bankrupt thanks to the CEO’s terrible financial decisions.

Complete Transcript 
My family didn't take a ton of vacations when I was growing up, but my parents 
would usually save up and take us all on a big trip once every two or three years. 
One time we went to the state of Colorado for a camping trip, and we went up one of 
the highest mountains in the U.S. And another year we went to California, where 
my parents had lived at one point – and we still have some relatives there. And then 
there was the time that we went to Florida for New Year's. 
We went together with my best friend's family. My parents and her parents have 
known each other for more than thirty years, and so she and I have been close 
pretty much ever since we were born. And our families always spend New Year's 
together - usually at their beach house, which isn't terribly thrilling in the winter - 
but one year our fathers decided to take us all down south. 
Now, I don't remember much about what we actually did in Florida, except for 
taking a boat ride through the wetlands and seeing the alligators. But the process of 
getting there was kinda funny. 
Our flight was on December 26th, so the day after Christmas, and the airport was 
packed with holiday travelers. When we got to the gate we found out that the 
airline had horribly overbooked the flight, and there weren’t enough seats for 
everyone. So people were arguing with and even shouting at the attendants … it 
was a mess. 
Luckily, the eight of us – my family and my friend’s family – were able to board the 
plane and get our seats. After the plane was full, the pilot made an announcement 
saying that if anyone would voluntarily give up their seats and take a later flight, 
they would get a voucher for a free round-trip ticket anywhere in the country. 
Nobody moved a muscle.  


A couple minutes later, they made another announcement offering TWO free 
round-trip tickets – and still, nobody budged. When they upped the offer to three 
free tickets, my dad and my friend’s dad started eyeing each other and wondering if 
we should take them up on it. And when they offered four free tickets per person, it 
was just too good to pass up – and we all sprang up out of our seats. 
So they put us on another flight, which wasn’t as full, a few hours later – and they 
even gave us extra snacks while we were waiting. We weren’t even missing out on 
anything, because it happened to be raining in Florida on that day.  
But there’s one last twist to the story. We had won a grand total of 32 free round-
trip tickets, and of course we were excited to use them. Well, sadly, not long after we 
got back, that airline went bankrupt and the tickets became worthless. I think my 
dad hung onto the vouchers for a couple of years just on the off-chance that another 
airline would take them, but no such luck.  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 


Extra Vocabulary 
"Nobody moved a muscle." 
This is an expression that means nobody moved at all, even a little bit. 
Everyone stayed perfectly still. 
"my dad and my friend’s dad started eyeing each other and wondering if we should 
take them up on it." 
The word “eye” can be used as a verb meaning to look at or watch closely, 
often with some intention – in this case the intention was to communicate 
through eye contact. To “take someone up on” an invitation or offer means to 
accept it, say “yes” to it. 
"when they offered four free tickets per person, it was just too good to pass up – 
and we all sprang up out of our seats." 
If you “pass up” an opportunity, it means you let it go by without accepting it. 
The verb “spring” (or “sprang” in the past) means to go up with energy. 
"But there’s one last twist to the story." 
 
A “twist” in a story means a change in direction, an unexpected change. 
"the tickets became worthless." 
 
“Worthless” means without value. 
"I think my dad hung onto the vouchers for a couple of years just on the off-chance 
that another airline would take them" 
To “hang onto” something means to keep it, and the word “off-chance” is an 
informal expression for a very, very small chance.
