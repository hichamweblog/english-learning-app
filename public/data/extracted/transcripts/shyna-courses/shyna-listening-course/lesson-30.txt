Warm-Up 
Describe a nice memory you have from your childhood. 
 
 
 
 
Activity 1 – Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
deprived (v./adj.) = lacking something necessary or important 
Ex) I’ve been sleep-deprived for the past week – been working until 3 AM every 
day and then waking up at 6. 
quest (n.) = an adventurous search or pursuit of a goal 
Ex) The company is investing a lot of money in its quest to build the world’s first 
driverless car. 
cranky (adj.) = upset, annoyed, in a bad mood, and rather immature, getting 
irritated about little things 
 
Ex) Chris always gets cranky when people don’t follow his instructions. 
scoreboard (n.) = a large board that shows the score (number of points) of the 
competitors in sports or competitions: 
 


petition (n.) = a formal written document making a request to the authorities, often 
signed by multiple people 
Ex) Animal rights activists are making a petition for a law to prohibit testing of 
new cosmetic products on animals. 
deflect (v.) = if an object is in movement, and you deflect it, it means you put it off 
the direct path of its movement 
Ex) (In soccer) The goalkeeper deflected the ball just in time to prevent the 
opposing player from scoring a goal.

Complete Transcript 
Some of my favorite childhood memories involve playing with my brother, David, 
who’s three and a half years younger than me. We always got along really well and 
we had so much fun together. When we were growing up in the early 90s, video 
games were starting to get really big, but my parents wouldn't let us have them. We 
were each allowed 30 minutes of computer time, and my mother would enforce 
that pretty strictly. But we didn’t feel too deprived – what we did instead was make 
up our own games, and we would get really creative. 
We used to act out some of our favorite TV shows; for example, I would construct 
these " mission impossible" quests for David, where he had to follow clues around 
the house and complete a list of tasks by a certain time, otherwise there would be 
some dire consequence, like a bomb would go off – an imaginary bomb, of course. 
We also loved this program called "American Gladiators" which involved athletic 
competitions and obstacle courses, and we would try to set up and recreate those 
challenges in the family room. 
David went through a phase when he was a really bad sport. He used to get all 
cranky if he lost a game or a competition to me, and wouldn't want to play anymore. 
So I came up with this system where I’d take a piece of paper and make a 
scoreboard with the names of ten or twelve countries, and I'd say that it was the 
Olympics.  
Each time we played a game or took a turn in some contest, we would pull a slip of 
paper out of a hat with the name of one of the countries, and that would be the 
country we would represent - so whoever won the game, any points we won would 
be given to that country.  


So it took the focus off the direct competition between my brother and me, and 
made it a competition among the countries instead. And it worked – David was 
happy to compete in the Olympics, and he was no longer upset when he lost. 
One of our favorite games was playing balloon volleyball in the family room. At the 
time, the room had this big open space in the middle, so it was perfect and we’d play 
for hours on end. Later on, my parents actually remodeled the family room and we 
were so mad that the new arrangement of the furniture was going to do away with 
our volleyball court, that we wrote them a petition asking them not to change the 
room! 
I remember one time we were playing and David hit the balloon towards me pretty 
hard, and it went over my head. I jumped up and my fingers just barely tipped the 
balloon, but I deflected it enough to make it hit a lamp and break it.  
Our mom heard the noise and came in to see what happened, and my brother and I 
both pointed a finger blaming each other for it. To this day we still argue playfully 
about whose fault it was that the lamp got broken. But we really enjoyed each 
other’s company and we had a blast together as kids. 
 
 
Extra Vocabulary 
"video games were starting to get really big" 
This is an informal way to say that video games were starting to become very 
popular and common. 
"otherwise there would be some dire consequence" 
 
The word “dire” describes a result that is terrible or disastrous. 
"David went through a phase when he was a really bad sport" 


When we say someone is “going through a phase,” it means he or she is 
experiencing a temporary period in their life in which they behave a certain 
way. Describing someone as a “bad sport” means the person reacts to losing 
in an angry and immature way. The opposite, a “good sport,” is someone who 
reacts to losing politely and diplomatically. 
"my parents actually remodeled the family room" 
To “remodel” a room, house, or building means to completely change its 
structure and/or appearance. 
"we had a blast together as kids" 
 
This expression means “we had a wonderful time, we had a lot of fun.”
