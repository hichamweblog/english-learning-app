Activity 1 – Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
ponder (v.) = think a lot about something, in a deep and reflective way 
 
Ex) As she gazed out at the ocean, she pondered the meaning of life. 
elaborate (adj.) = complex, with many different details 
Ex) The stone is covered with elaborate carvings. 
 
bubbles (n.) = little balls of air surrounded by water 
 


hoard (v.) = keep/collect a lot of something for yourself, without sharing with 
anyone else, in order to use it later 
 
Ex) Hey, stop hoarding all the snacks! I’m hungry, too! 
shackles (n.) = the locked metal cuffs used to secure the hands or feet of a 
prisoner or slave 
 
altruistic (adj.) = caring about other people, not just yourself 
Ex) Bill Gates is a billionaire who is known for his altruistic work; he 
contributes to many organizations that help the poor. 
log (n.) = a thick, round piece of wood used in construction 
 
mourn (v.) = crying and being sad over a death or loss 
 
Ex) The entire country mourned the death of one of its most famous singers. 
termite (n.) = a type of insect that eats wood 


puzzle (n.) = a problem – usually done for fun – that requires thought and 
creativity in order to solve it. One of the most common types is a jigsaw puzzle, 
where you have to put the pieces together. However, there are many other types 
of puzzles.

Complete Transcript 
Throughout history, humans have pondered animal intelligence and especially the 
possibility of consciousness in other species. Previously an animal’s brain size was 
often used to determine its capacity for intelligence, but a better indicator of 
intelligence is brain complexity. Experiments are also used to test animals' ability 
to learn, memorize, solve problems, and communicate. 
Dolphins are one of the smartest sea creatures. In the wild, dolphins exhibit 
complex play behavior involving making and playing with elaborate rings of 
bubbles in groups. They also communicate through a “language” of clicks and 
whistles, using distinctive signals to identify and call each other. 
One incredible example of dolphin cleverness was demonstrated in a captive 
environment. In the Institute for Marine Mammal Studies in Mississippi, the 
dolphins are rewarded for keeping their own tank clean. They do this by 
retrieving rubbish and bringing it to a keeper, receiving a fish as a reward. One 
dolphin showed the ability to plan for the future – instead of delivering the trash 
right away, she started hoarding it under a rock at the bottom of the pool. Then, 
she would bring it up one small piece at a time whenever she wanted a fish. 
The elephant is another highly intelligent animal – in fact, some parts of an 
elephant’s brain are more complex than our own. Research in Japan has shown 
that elephants have the ability to count and even handle rudimentary arithmetic. 
There have been numerous demonstrations of elephants’ problem-solving 
abilities, such as an elephant in a zoo in Asia that worked out how to unlock the 
shackles on her feet – and even helped other elephants escape! 
Elephants are incredibly altruistic and often give aid to other species in need. In 
India, an elephant was trained to help in a construction project by lifting logs and 
placing them into holes in the ground. At one point, it refused to insert a log into a 
hole – and when the supervisor went to investigate, he found a dog sleeping at the 
bottom of the hole. The elephant would only insert the log after the dog had been 
removed. 
Finally, elephants are the only species besides humans to have a recognizable 
ritual around death. They have been observed mourning and covering the body 
of a dead elephant with dirt and leaves. Elephants show strong interest in the 
bones of their own kind, and even visit “elephant graveyards.” 
Chimpanzees, our close genetic relatives, also demonstrate remarkable 
intelligence. They can make and use simple tools – for example, using rocks as 


hammers to crack open nuts, and using sticks to extract termites from their nests. 
Chimps can also learn to use sign language to communicate with their human 
trainers, although their vocabulary is limited and they can only express simple 
concepts. 
In some memory tasks, chimpanzees actually outperform humans. In one 
experiment, the numbers one through nine appeared on a computer screen in 
random positions. The numbers were only visible for about a second, and then 
disappeared. Chimps were able to place all the numbers back in the correct 
positions with a high degree of accuracy, whereas humans who took the test 
could remember no more than a few. 
Perhaps the most interesting finding is that chimps appear to enjoy an 
intellectual challenge. During an experiment in London, chimps voluntarily 
worked on puzzles of increasing difficulty, even when they did not receive a 
reward for completing them. This suggests that they enjoy solving brain teasers 
for fun, just as humans do. 
 
Extra Vocabulary 
"In the wild, dolphins exhibit complex play behavior..." 
“In the wild” means the dolphins are in their natural habitat, not in zoos or 
in human captivity. 
"One incredible example of dolphin cleverness..." 
“Cleverness” is a little bit different from “intelligence” – it means intelligence 
that is especially creative and brings an advantage to the person/animal 
using it. 
"elephants have the ability to count and even handle rudimentary arithmetic." 
 
“Rudimentary” means very basic. 
"In some memory tasks, chimpanzees actually outperform humans." 
 
“Outperform” means to perform better.
