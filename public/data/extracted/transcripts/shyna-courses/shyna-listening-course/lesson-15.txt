Activity 1 – Learn Vocabulary  
saga (n.) – a long detailed story or report 
 
Ex) This book tells the saga of the royal family in 17th-century England. 
iconic (adj.) – very famous for a long time in culture and society 
 
Ex) Russell Crowe starred in the iconic film Gladiator. 
motto (n.) – a short phrase that expresses a main guiding principle or ideal, 
which a person or organization always tries to follow 
 
Ex) The motto of the city police department is “to protect and serve.” 
patent (n./v.) – official legal protection for an idea or product, so that only one 
person/company has permission to use, produce, and sell it for a period of time 
Ex) George patented his invention to prevent it from being copied by 
competitors. 
ingenious (adj.) – intelligent, skillful, and creative (can describe a physical 
product, or an idea or plan) 
Ex) Sarah created an ingenious program in order to get the students more 
interested in reading. 
medieval (adj.) – related to the Middle Ages (with castles, knights, etc.) 
 
Ex) My kids loved attending the medieval fair. 
overtly (adv.) – obviously, visibly 
Ex) Bob overtly dislikes Alan; he doesn’t even try to hide his feelings. 


license (v.) – get official authorization or permission 
Ex) In order to open a restaurant, you need to get licensed by the city after 
an inspection by the health department.  
plethora (n.) – many, an excess 
Ex) I received a plethora of e-mail inquiries after posting pictures online of 
six adorable puppies available for adoption. 
landmarks (n.) – famous points in a city or country 
 
Ex) The Statue of Liberty is one of the landmarks of New York City. 
milestone (n.) – an important event or achievement in a process 
Ex) The opening of a new community center was a milestone for our 
organization. 
acclaim (v.) – publicly approve and praise 
Ex) Rebecca is an internationally-acclaimed artist; her work has been 
featured in twenty-five countries.

Complete Transcript 
From its humble beginnings in a poor carpenter’s shop to its current status as the 
world’s third-largest manufacturer of toys, the LEGO saga spans more than 80 
years.  
This iconic global brand was founded in Denmark in 1932 by a carpenter named 
Ole Kirk Christiansen. The name LEGO derives from two Danish words “leg” and 
“godt” meaning “play” and “good” – with the intention of transmitting the 
company’s motto ‘play well.’ The original LEGO toys were made of wood. 
LEGO was not very profitable in its early days due to the Great Depression, and 
Christiansen’s workshop and toy factory burned down not once, not twice, but 
three times. After World War II, plastic became available in Denmark and LEGO 
began selling plastic toys. However, these were initially not well-received by the 
public. Not only were sales poor, but many purchased products were returned. 
The company’s key innovation, patented in the late 1950s, remains the simple yet 
ingenious LEGO brick, which has been named ‘Toy of the Century’ on two 
occasions. The brick’s unique interlocking facility offers endless possibilities for 
creative play.  
LEGO has released thousands of sets with dozens of themes such as space 
exploration, pirates, the medieval world, undersea exploration, the wild west, and 
town and city. Notably absent are LEGO sets with an overtly military theme – this 
is intentional, based on Christiansen’s belief that children’s toys should not 
encourage war.  
LEGO has also introduced a number of special product lines, such as their DUPLO 
range for players under the age of five, and has also licensed rights to use 
characters and themes from films and cartoons such as Star Wars, Harry Potter, 
and Toy Story. 
In 1968, the LEGOLAND theme park opened its gates in Billund, Denmark with a 
plethora of rides and hands-on educational activities. It now receives 1.6 million 
visitors annually and is the largest tourist attraction in Denmark outside of the 
capital.  
In 1988 the first official LEGO World Cup building championships were held with 
the participation of 38 children from 17 different countries. In 1992, two Guinness 
World Records involving LEGO products were set: a castle built from nearly half 
a million LEGO bricks, and a LEGO railway line 545 meters in length. 


LEGO is not only the domain of children. Many architects use it to model their 
ideas, and artist Warren Elsmore demonstrated the vast potential of our much 
loved plastic bricks by building more than 70 models of international landmarks, 
eventually publishing a book about his labor of love entitled Brick City: Lego For 
Grown-ups. 
In the 2000s, LEGO began developing robotic LEGO sets that connect to computers 
via USB cables to allow for education in programming. Another milestone was the 
2014 production of the LEGO Movie. A massive global success, the movie 
surpassed the $400m mark in its first month in theaters and has been universally 
acclaimed by both critics and viewers. A sequel is already being planned. 
 
Extra Vocabulary 
“From its humble beginnings in a poor carpenter’s shop” 
When a project or person begins in a very poor or simple form, we say it has 
“humble beginnings” 
“The company’s key innovation” 
 
In this context, “key” is an adjective that means “important; essential” 
“Notably absent are LEGO sets with an overtly military theme” 
“Notably” means that something is worth noticing; it is interesting to pay 
attention to. 
“eventually publishing a book about his labor of love”  
A “labor of love” is work done because you love it or you are very interested 
in it, not for financial gain 
“A sequel is already being planned.” 
A “sequel” is a movie that comes after the first movie, and continues the 
story.
