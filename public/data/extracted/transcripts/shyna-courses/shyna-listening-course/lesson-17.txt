Lesson 17: 
Tattoos in Different Cultures 
Tattoos have been used in many different ways among the 
various cultures of the world. Some have celebrated tattoos; 
others have despised them. What do you think about tattoos? 
How are they viewed in your culture? 
 
 
 
 
  
Activity 1 – Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
mummy (n.) – a dead body that has been preserved from ancient times 
Ex) While digging a foundation for a new building, workers discovered two 
mummies that may be around 4,000 years old. 
hypothesis (n.) – an idea or explanation that can be tested to determine if it is 
true or not 
Ex) The scientist’s hypothesis, which was first published in a journal ten 
years ago, was then disproved by later experiments. 
deity (n.) – a god or goddess 
 
Ex) Some religions perform animal sacrifices to please their deities. 
labor (n.) – the process of giving birth to a baby, beginning with contractions of 
the mother’s uterus and ending with the baby being born 
 
Ex) Ryan rushed to the hospital when his wife went into labor. 
nobility (n.) – the royal social class in a society; the people who are born with or 
granted high rank and high status 
 
Ex) This palace was built as a summer residence for the country’s nobility. 


technique (n.) – the systematic procedure for performing a task 
Ex) The surgeon developed a new technique for performing what was once 
a rather dangerous operation. 
lineage (n.) – historical family line; ancestors 
Ex) I can trace my lineage back to the 1800s, but before that there are no 
reliable records. 
turbulent (adj.) – describes something that is very agitated and chaotic 
Ex) My brother and his wife have a very turbulent relationship – one week 
they’re fighting, and the next week they’re totally in love. 
outlaw (v.) – prohibit by law; make illegal 
Ex) Some people believe that outlawing guns will not, in fact, reduce 
violence because criminals will still find a way to get them. 
stigma (n.) – a perception of social disgrace 
Ex) Despite many campaigns against racism, there is still an unfortunate 
stigma in society against people with darker skin tones. 
deviant (adj.) – different from the norms of society, outside of what is 
considered acceptable (this word has a negative connotation) 
Ex) The dictator imprisoned people who were spreading ideas that he viewed 
as deviant. 
mainstream (adj.) – related to the main current of thought or belief in society; 
common and accepted by the majority of people 
Ex) I don’t watch the news because I believe the mainstream media doesn’t 
give an accurate representation of the facts.

Complete Transcript 
Tattooing is an age-old way of creating permanent art on the body. The oldest 
known tattoo was discovered on the ‘The Iceman,’ a 5,200-year-old mummy 
found on the Italian-Austrian border.  
When inspecting the seemingly random pattern of dots and small crosses on the 
Iceman’s lower back and right knee and ankle, Professor Brothwell at the 
University of York, England, found that the tattooed body parts corresponded 
with the worst areas of stress-induced damage. This leads to the hypothesis that 
the tattoos had an essentially therapeutic role in that ancient culture; the 
markings may have been believed to alleviate joint pain. 
Among Egyptian mummies, tattoos were almost exclusively on females – this 
fact, combined with evidence of social status, led to the belief that tattoos were 
used to mark prostitutes in ancient Egypt. However, the theory that tattoos also 
had a supernatural protective role at the time of pregnancy and childbirth has 
recently gained popularity. The distribution of tattoos around the abdomen, 
thighs, and breasts – especially the figure of a deity considered to be the 
protector of women in labor – lends support to this theory.  
Other ancient cultures, such as the tribes living in the Altai Mountain region 
tattooed themselves with mythical animals and marks of nobility. The absence of 
tattoos was considered a mark of low birth. On the other end of the spectrum, the 
Greeks and Romans often used tattoos to mark criminals.  
Of course, in ancient times no machines were used and tattoos were applied by 
hand, using a sharp point with a wooden handle. This technique is still used 
among the indigenous Maori people of New Zealand today. 
These tattoos served a crucial role in traditional Maori culture, where facial 
tattoos represented one’s lineage and marked an important rite of passage into 
adulthood. Men were usually tattooed on their faces, buttocks, and thighs, while 
the women were historically tattooed around their lips and noses, as this 
prevented the skin from becoming wrinkled with age. 
The history of tattoos in Japan has been an especially turbulent one. Initially 
tattoos were fashionable and represented high social status, then they became 
markers for prisoners, and were later outlawed. For many years they were 
associated with criminals and gangs. Although tattoos have experienced a 
resurgence in popularity among Japanese youth, there is still a stigma on them 


among the general population, and people with tattoos may be banned from 
certain businesses, such as fitness centers and public baths. 
In the Americas, the pre-Columbian Native American cultures also used tattoos 
on women to help protect them during pregnancy. For a number of years, tattoos 
were mainly found on sailors, and heavily tattooed people were part of “freak 
shows” in American circuses. In recent decades, tattoos have gone from a 
somewhat deviant practice to a mainstream part of Western fashion, 
transcending genders, economic classes, and age groups. 
Humans have used tattoos for myriad reasons for thousands of years. Perhaps 
this is essentially what tattoos represent today: a link to our ancestral roots. 
 
Extra Vocabulary 
“the Italian-Austrian border.” 
The border is the dividing line between two countries, or two areas of 
land. 
“This leads to the hypothesis that the tattoos had an essentially therapeutic role 
in that ancient culture.” 
Something described as therapeutic is related to treating disease/injury 
or maintaining good health. 
 “the indigenous Maori people of New Zealand” 
The word indigenous refers to the people who originally lived in an area, 
before the arrival of outsiders, colonizers, etc. 
“an important rite of passage into adulthood” 
A rite of passage is any event or ritual that signifies a transition from one 
life stage to another – often, from being a child/adolescent to being an 
adult 
“tattoos have experienced a resurgence in popularity among Japanese youth” 
A resurgence is a renewal; in the context of this sentence it means that 
tattoos are now becoming popular again (after a long time when they were 
not popular) 


“people with tattoos may be banned from certain businesses” 
Banned, in this sentence, means “prevented from entering.” It can also 
mean “prohibited” as in the case of an object or action (“The new law bans 
using your cell phone while driving.”) 
“heavily tattooed people were part of “freak shows” in circuses.” 
The word freak refers to someone/something that is completely abnormal 
(not normal). It usually has a negative connotation, implying that 
something is different from the norm in a bad way.
