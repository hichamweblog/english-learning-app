Lesson 6: Worst Job 
Warm-Up: 
Describe the worst job you’ve ever had. Why was it so bad? 
 
 
 
 
Activity 1: Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
bed and breakfast (n.) = a type of small hotel which is a family’s home, and the 
family rents out a few rooms for travelers/visitors to stay overnight 
Amish (n./adj.) = the Amish people are a religious/cultural group that live in the 
rural areas of the northeastern United States. They have a simple lifestyle and do not 
use modern technology. 
 
tight-knit (adj.) = this adjective describes a group whose members are very closely 
and firmly integrated with each other 
Ex) There is a small but tight-knit community of Chinese immigrants in this 
town – since there aren’t very many of them, they tend to stick together. 
quaint (adj.) = old-fashioned in a charming and pleasant way 


Ex) We stayed in a quaint little village in the mountains. 
 
achy (adj.) = painful. This is the adjective form of ache (which means “pain”) 
Ex) I’ve been sitting at the computer for the past eight hours and my back is all 
achy. 
handle (n.) = an attachment to an object that can be used with the hand 
 
knock (v.) = hit a surface (usually a door or window) to make a sound and get the 
attention of someone inside

Complete Transcript 
The worst job that I ever had was actually the first job that I ever had. I was fifteen 
years old and I was a plate-washer at a bed and breakfast in Amish country. And 
where I grew up in the mid-west, there were these tight-knit Amish communities 
that were far-off from everything; far from electricity, and from buzzing cars and 
noise, and it was the perfect setting for a little quaint bed and breakfast. People 
would come in from the city and they would spend a weekend or spend some time 
there and enjoy nature.  
But part of the quaintness was this cozy little restaurant that didn't have a menu; 
that served one thing every meal. There were no options, they just cooked it and 
brought it to you. And I was the dish washer. And I would go there with my brother, 
who was old enough to drive, we would go there and would always just have the 
most exotic things come back on plates. I felt like you couldn't get plates any dirtier 
with this food.  
I had to not only rinse everything off in scalding hot water, but dry it in this 
industrial dishwasher and dryer that would just burn your hands every time you 
touched a plate - but they kept coming, so you had to wash them and get them in 
and then get them out. And I remember my hands being burnt and just achy all the 
time. I knew I was gonna get burnt touching these plates. 
And then there was also the other extreme, there was a freezer, a walk-in freezer 
that you had to go into to get certain things, certain supplies, it was the grunt work 
to get things. And actually my first day there, I got locked in the walk-in freezer. 
There was no handle on the inside, but instead there was a button that no one had 
ever really showed me, and when I was in there I actually got stuck in there. 
Thankfully there was a small window in the middle that I was able to knock on and 
you try to get somebody's attention - but I was in there before 5 or 10 minutes 
before anyone came and got me. 
And long story short, I didn't last very long in the plate-washing industry. It was not 
my career of choice. I only worked there for about six months; made enough money 
to buy a new bike, and I was outta there. 


Extra Vocabulary 
"far from electricity, and from buzzing cars and noise" 
 
The word buzz (n./v.) refers to the ZZZZZ noise made by insects and machines. 
"cozy little restaurant" 
 
The word cozy (adj.) means small, warm, and comfortable. 
"I had to not only rinse everything off in scalding hot water" 
To rinse (v.) is to pass water over something to clean it. If water is scalding 
hot, it is extremely hot – hot enough to burn you. 
"it was the grunt work to get things" 
“Grunt work” refers to tasks that are not very interesting, and not appreciated. 
They are the things that nobody wants to do, so it’s usually the employee with 
the lowest status who has to do them. 
"And long story short…" 
This is a shortened version of the expression “to make a long story short” – 
meaning “I will give a short summary/conclusion, because all the details would 
take a long time to tell you.” 
"I only worked there for about six months; made enough money to buy a new bike, 
and I was outta there." 
This expression means “I left” – usually, leaving very fast because it’s not good 
for you to stay. A similar expression is “Let’s get outta here!” meaning “Let’s 
leave.”
