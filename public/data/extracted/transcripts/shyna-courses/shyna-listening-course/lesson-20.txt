Lesson 20: Just in Time! 
Warm-Up: 
Describe a situation in which you resolved a situation in the nick 
of time – exactly at the right moment, just before it was too late. 
 
 
 
Activity 1 – Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
bumpy (adj.) = full of bumps (raised, rounded spots that make a surface uneven). In 
this picture, the ground is “bumpy”: 
 
stall (n.) = many public bathrooms have several small compartments, with a toilet 
in each one. These individual compartments are called “stalls.” 
 


elation (n.) = very great happiness/joy. The adjective form is “elated.” 
 
Ex) I was elated when I found out I’d won a free trip to Hawaii. 
peek (v.) = to look quickly; usually either looking at something secret or looking 
from a hidden/secret place  
Ex) Bob cheated on his test – he peeked at his friend’s paper and copied the 
answers. 
startled (adj.) = lightly frightened by a sudden noise or disturbance 
Ex) I was concentrating so hard on my book that it startled me when my 
husband tapped me on the shoulder. 
predicament (n.) = a complicated, difficult, and unpleasant situation 
Ex) Ellen’s in quite a predicament – she slept with three different men last 
month and now she’s pregnant with no idea who the father is.

Complete Transcript 
I was on a mission trip in the country of Haiti. We had just finished our business in 
the city of Port-au-Prince, and we were about to head out to the city of Port-de-Paix. 
It was a long trip - I believe it took us about 12 hours to go from Port-au-Prince to 
Port-de-Paix. It was a very, very bumpy trip, and we were probably about 15 
minutes into this trip - and of course, that's when it dawned on me that I needed to 
use the restroom. 
Well, after about another fifteen minutes, we were getting further and further away 
from the city, and my need to use the restroom was only growing greater. 
Eventually I got to a point where I knew that I would NOT be able to wait until we 
arrived in Port-de-Paix 12 hours later - so I kept saying that I needed to stop and 
use the restroom. 
My youth coach, Tangi, said "Well, I'm sorry, but there's nothing along the way, and 
we can't afford to stop, we have to keep our schedule.” Well, this just made it all the 
worse - there was no way that I was going to be able to hold this for twelve hours.  
The stars aligned - we came across a restaurant out in the middle of nowhere, and I 
moved out of that van faster than anything I had ever seen. And before I knew it, I 
was in the restaurant, in the bathroom, and on the toilet. 
Now, this was a single bathroom; there was only one stall. And once I caught my 
breath, I was thanking God so hard and so much for providing this miracle. There 
was nothing more precious in my life at that moment, or that I could ever recall - 
but that elation would not last very long. Eventually, I realized that it was time to go 
- and I reached for the toilet paper. But much to my distress, I realized there was no 
toilet paper. There was not even a cardboard tube hinting that there was once toilet 
paper.  
As I said, there was no other stalls in the bathroom, and my mind began to race - 
what was I going to do? What am I going to do? I peeked out of the stall to see if 
there was any hand towels by the sink, and from what I observed, this bathroom 
had not been stocked in quite some time. 
I began going through my options - what can I possibly do here? And then it dawned 
on me that the only thing that I could do was to use my shirt. I thought to myself, "I 
have no other options, but... if I use my shirt, what is it going to look like when I walk 
out of the restaurant without my shirt? They'll laugh at me; I will never, ever live 
down the fact that I used my shirt." 


But I couldn't sit in there forever - so after a time I took a deep breath and I slowly 
began to take my shirt off. Well, before I even had it off, there was a knock at the 
door and I was startled. The panic in my voice probably showed through my 
question: "Who is it?" 
A familiar voice answered, "Hey Chris, it's Frank. Are you okay, buddy? You've been 
in there a while." I replied "Yes, yes, I'm fine - just a few more minutes." I wasn't sure 
if I was ready to tell him about my predicament. Well, he came in and he said, "We 
thought you might need this." I cracked the stall to the bathroom, and from around 
the corner I saw a nice, new, fresh roll of toilet paper! It was even more beautiful 
than the toilet I had found only a short time before. 
I was so grateful that my friend knew of my need, and I know my shirt is grateful. I 
don't know what happened to it, but I know it didn't get left in the garbage in a 
bathroom in Haiti. 
 
Extra Vocabulary 
“that's when it dawned on me that I needed to use the restroom.” 
 
The expression “it dawned on me” means “I realized/perceived.” 
“The stars aligned - we came across a restaurant out in the middle of nowhere” 
“The stars aligned” means that something lucky happened. It is usually used 
when various circumstances come together to give a fortunate result. “In the 
middle of nowhere” is an expression meaning “in a very isolated area,” with 
virtually nothing around it. 
“my mind began to race” 
 
This means that he began to think very quickly and nervously. 
“this bathroom had not been stocked in quite some time.” 
 
This means the bathroom had not been provided with supplies. 
“I will never, ever live down the fact that I used my shirt.” 
The phrasal verb “live down” means “to overcome shame from something 
embarrassing over a period of time.” It’s usually used in the expression “I’ll 
never live it down,” meaning, “I will never overcome this shame/humiliation.”
