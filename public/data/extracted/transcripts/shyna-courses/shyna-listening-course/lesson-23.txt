Lesson 23:  
The Amazing Human Body 
How well do you know your own body? Today’s 
lesson talks about some interesting facts and recent 
discoveries about the human body. 
What do you like and dislike about your body? What 
do you do (or need to do) to stay in good health? 
 
 
 
 
Activity 1 – Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
disparity (n.) – difference, inequality 
Ex) There is a large economic disparity in this country – the richest 5% 
control 90% of the nation’s wealth. 
replenish (v.) – refill or make complete again 
 
Ex) Eating fruit can help you replenish your energy quickly after exercising. 
seep (v.) – when something (like liquid) slowly passes through small openings 
 
Ex) I put a bandage on the injury, but the blood is still seeping through it. 
concrete (n.) – a material used in construction: 
 


cube (n.) – a three-dimensional square shape 
 
Ex) Put a couple of ice cubes in the drinks. 
chronic pain – pain that is continuous and lasts for a long time 
Ex) My dad has chronic back pain after years of sitting at the computer 
with bad posture. 
trigger (v.) – activate, produce a result or reaction 
 
Ex) The door automatically opens when a person triggers the motion sensor. 
antibodies (n.) – proteins in your blood that fight disease 
Ex) Vaccines help your body create antibodies that prevent you from getting 
sick. 
alleviate (v.) – provide relief, especially from pain 
Ex) This is an all-natural medicine that will alleviate a headache within 
twenty minutes. 
cold sore (n.) – a small irritation on your lips or inside your mouth 
 
cognitive (adj.) – related to thought and mental ability 
Ex) All new students at this school must take tests to measure their 
cognitive skills. 
counteract (v.) – have contrary action, action against something 
Ex) We need to protect the environment and counteract the effects of 
human activity on the planet. 
superficial (adj.) – related to the surface; not deep, profound, or complex 
Ex) The company is making some superficial changes to its policy, but 
nothing that will really have a big impact.

Complete Transcript 
The human body is full of intriguing surprises. For example, you are actually tallest 
when you first get out of bed. The height difference between the early morning 
and late night averages around one inch. The reason for this disparity is that while 
you sleep, the spinal fluid between the discs in your vertebral column is 
replenished. As you go about the daily grind the discs compress and the spinal 
fluid seeps out, causing you to lose a bit of height. 
Did you ever stop to consider how vital your skeleton is? Your bones hold you 
together. Without them, we’d be sacks of tissue incapable of bearing our own 
weight or moving around. Human bone is about 75% water, and you may be 
surprised to learn that bone is four times stronger than concrete. A small 3 inch 
cube of bone is capable of supporting nearly 20,000 pounds of weight! The largest 
and strongest bone in your body is your femur (thigh bone). 
There may actually be a scientific basis to the saying that “Laughter is the best 
medicine.” Laughter is proven to reduce chronic pain – partially due to being a 
distraction, but also because it triggers the release of endorphins, chemicals in 
your brain that produce a sense of general well-being.  
Studies have also shown that laughter strengthens your immune system – people 
who laugh more have greater numbers of antibodies; they are thus less susceptible 
to disease and more easily able to recover from illness. Finally, laughter relaxes 
the muscles and the mind, thus alleviating stress and its many detrimental effects 
on the body. 
If you suffer from cold sores, watch out – scientists at Columbia University have 
discovered that people with cold sores were more likely to experience cognitive 
decline, such as the development of Alzheimer’s disease and dementia. The good 
news is that physical activity counteracts this effect and reduces the risk of losing 
your mental abilities. 
Finally, people in developed nations tend to worry about aging. We spend a 
fortune on anti-wrinkle cream and hair-loss treatments. This may seem 
superficial, but a recent study showed that the more wrinkled your skin, the higher 
your risk of suffering from blood pressure related conditions. The solution to both 
these problems isn’t a cosmetic quick fix – instead, maintaining a healthy lifestyle 
and diet, as well as quitting smoking, will give you both a more youthful 
appearance and a healthier heart. 
 


Extra Vocabulary 
"As you go about the daily grind" 
The expression “daily grind” refers to the tasks of daily life and routine, 
which often make you tired 
"laughter strengthens your immune system" 
The immune system is the human body’s system of fighting diseases and 
protecting you from infection 
"thus alleviating stress and its many detrimental effects on the body" 
If something is detrimental, it causes damage or harm 
"the development of Alzheimer’s disease and dementia." 
The word dementia refers to the general loss of mental abilities (memory, 
concentration, judgment) - usually when people get older. Alzheimer's 
disease is a specific form of dementia; it is the most common one. 
"We spend a fortune on anti-wrinkle cream and hair-loss treatments." 
The expression spend a fortune means spend a lot of money, an exaggerated 
amount of money. 
"The solution to both these problems isn’t a cosmetic quick fix" 
A “quick fix” is a fast and easy solution to a problem.
