Activity 1 – Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
tryout (n.) = a test or trial period to see if an athlete or performer is good enough to 
be accepted. (For the performing arts, this can also be called an “audition”) 
Ex) Our dance group is looking for some new members; we’re holding a tryout 
on Friday. 
regular (n.) = if you describe a person as a “regular”, it means he/she frequently 
goes to that place or participates in that activity. 
 
Ex) The bartender knows all the regulars at the bar by name. 
edible (adj.) = describes something that can be eaten 
 
Ex) Do you know which of these plants are edible? 
hassle (n.) = a difficult/annoying task 
Ex) Trying to register on the website was a hassle – I had to fill out five 
different forms. 
lid (n.) = a removable cover for a box, pot, or other container 
 


hit-or-miss (adj.) = sometimes succeeding, sometimes not 
Ex) He thinks he’s funny, but his jokes are hit-or-miss. Some of them are 
hilarious and others, not so much. 
dud (n.) = something that is disappointingly ineffective or unsuccessful 
Ex) The advertising campaign was a dud. It didn’t bring in any new clients. 
batch (n.) = a group or set of similar objects, often all coming at the same time 
 
Ex) If you want more pancakes, I can make another batch.

Complete Transcript 
One thing I’d say I’m good at is sports. I’ve been involved in some type of sport 
pretty much ever since I was six. I played soccer for about ten years, and I was 
captain of my high school team. My parents wouldn’t let me play in any of the really 
competitive soccer leagues, though, because they wanted me to focus on my 
studies. When I went to college, I played terribly during tryouts and didn’t make the 
team – so that’s when I started getting more into volleyball instead. 
There was a group of students and teachers that would play twice a day – at 
lunchtime, and again in the evenings. It started out as a casual pickup game, but we 
played so much that all the regulars ended up getting really good. We even 
organized a tournament, and my team got all the way to the finals – which we lost 
by just a couple of points. But I really loved those volleyball games and I looked 
forward to them every day. 
I’ve also been into martial arts for many years, and I’ve gotten to train with some of 
the best masters in the world. Two sports I’ve only tried a few times but would love 
to do more of are skiing and rock climbing. The first time I went climbing, I thought I 
was already in pretty good shape, but boy, did I feel it the next day! It must have 
worked muscles I don’t normally use. 
As for what I’m not good at, the first thing that comes to mind is cooking. I mean, I’m 
not terrible at it, I can make things that are edible – but I’m not a natural. I guess 
I’ve just never taken the time to learn. But it’s always felt like a hassle to me, and I 
get especially anxious when I have to cook for other people.  
One time I was gonna make dinner for a group of friends, and I decided to make 
brisket, which is beef that’s slow-cooked with lots of onions and garlic. My mom told 
me it’s virtually impossible to mess up, the only thing I had to remember was to 
always keep the pot covered and never remove the lid. 


Well, I decided to test the recipe a week before the dinner, and I followed my 
mother’s instructions religiously. However, the pot I was using had a lid that didn’t 
fit properly, and all the water inside evaporated, so the meat ended up sticking to 
the bottom and burning. I couldn’t bring myself to eat it, and I think I ended up 
giving it to the dog. 
Nowadays I can make a decent brisket, but chicken and fish have been sorta hit-or-
miss. I think some of the recipes I find on the internet are duds, because sometimes 
even when I follow them perfectly, it still doesn’t turn out right. Luckily I married a 
man who knows his way around the kitchen; he’s a much better cook than me. But 
I’ve been experimenting with baking lately, and I made two successful batches of 
cookies – so maybe there’s hope for me yet! 
 
Extra Vocabulary 
“It started out as a casual pickup game” 
A “pickup game” is one that is informal, with the participation of anyone who 
is available. 
“I followed my mother’s instructions religiously” 
If you do something “religiously,” it doesn’t necessarily have to do with 
religion – in this context, it means to do it while being very careful to follow 
the rules. 
“I couldn’t bring myself to eat it” 
The expression “I couldn’t bring myself” to do something means I tried or I 
wanted to, but I wasn’t able to force myself to do it.
