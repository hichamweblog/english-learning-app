Lesson 22: Broken Bone 
Warm-Up: 
Have you ever broken a bone? Describe what happened. (If you 
have never broken a bone, describe a time when you got sick or 
injured). 
 
 
 
Activity 1 – Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
curfew (n.) = a rule that you must be home by a certain time. Parents often set 
curfews for their kids. 
Ex) When I was a teenager, I could never stay late at parties because I had a 10 
PM curfew. 
doze off (v.) = fall asleep, usually a light sleep 
 
Ex) The lecture was so boring that half the class dozed off. 
excruciating pain = extremely intense pain; pain that is difficult to endure 
Ex) I felt excruciating pain when I accidentally slammed my finger in the car 
door. 
writhing in pain = this refers to a movement, when someone is twisting and 
moving around (often while lying down) because of the pain 
Ex) After the explosion in the factory, several victims were lying on the ground, 
writhing in pain. 
subside (v.) = become less intense 
Ex) It’s hard to keep working on a project after the initial excitement has 
subsided. 
hobble (v.) = to walk with difficulty – like an old person, or a person with an injury 
 
Ex) Cars honked impatiently as the old woman hobbled across the street.

Complete Transcript 
I want to tell you about a time when I broke a bone. I was probably about 14 or 15 
years old, and my younger brother would've been about 12 or 13. In those days, we 
had a curfew. 
Well, on this particular evening my parents decided to go to bed before my brother 
was home, trusting that he would honor the agreement. Well, after they had gone to 
sleep, I got a call from him saying that he wanted to stay out just a little bit later with 
his friends, and wondered if I would stay up to let him in because he did not have a 
key.  
So being a good big sister, I said that I would. I grabbed my snacks and a blanket 
and I sat on the couch and stayed up watching TV. After a while, it got to be pretty 
late, and I started to doze off.  
Well, it was about the time that I was about ready to fall asleep when I heard a loud 
sound - and I was startled. I thought to myself that it must be my brother getting 
home. So I shot up as fast as I could, kicked my feet off the couch, and instantly felt 
the most excruciating pain I had ever felt in my life.  
I would have screamed so incredibly loud, but I knew I couldn't because I would 
wake my parents and they would discover that my brother was not home. So I lay 
there on the floor writhing in pain with my mouth wide open, just wanting to 
scream but staying silent.  
After a time, the pain subsided, and I hobbled my way over to the front door. And 
when I opened it, wouldn't you know it, he was not there. In fact, he was never there 
- and wouldn't be home for a couple more hours.  
It turns out that I had broken my toe when I'd kicked my feet off the couch and hit 
an exposed part of the wooden frame of the couch. So I eventually did let my 
brother in the house that night, and my parents never discovered that he was home 
late - but I sure did pay the price for that rule being broken as I hobbled around on a 
broken toe for the next couple of weeks. 
 
 
 


Extra Vocabulary 
"trusting that he would honor the agreement." 
To "honor" the agreement means to obey it - so, Chris's parents were trusting 
that his brother would obey the rule of being home by a certain time. 
"I grabbed my snacks and a blanket" 
The verb "grab" means to take something with your hand quickly 
"I sure did pay the price for that rule being broken" 
To "pay the price" is an idiomatic expression meaning to experience the 
unpleasant results of doing something.
