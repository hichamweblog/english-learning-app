Lesson 36: Mistakes 
Warm-Up: 
Write about a mistake you have made at some point in your 
life (can be at work, at school, in relationships or social 
situations, etc.) 
 
 
 
 
Activity 1 – Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
deadline (n.) = the final date by which a project must be finished 
Ex) You need to submit your article by the deadline in order for it to be 
published in this month’s issue of the magazine. 
draft (n.) = a version of a paper or project during its development. The word “draft” 
usually means it is not finalized yet – except for the final version, which is called the 
“final draft.” 
Ex) I just sent you a draft of the report – please look it over and reply with your 
comments. 
bio (n.) = short for “biography” – a description of a person’s life and work 
 
Ex) Most books have an author bio on the back cover or inside flap. 
it bugs me = this is an informal way to say “it annoys me” 
 
Ex) It really bugs me when people interrupt someone who’s talking. It’s so rude! 
hindsight (n.) = looking back on past events, now with more understanding than 
you had at the time 
Ex) In hindsight, I should have ended that relationship much earlier. We were 
terrible together – but I thought I was in love at the time. 
give the green light = this expression means to give permission or approval for a 
project to go ahead 


Ex) The mayor has given the green light for building a new highway across the 
city. 
typo (n.) = an error in typing (typing is writing using the keys on the computer) 
Ex) There are several typos in this brochure – how embarrassing for the 
company.

Complete Transcript 
Today I'm going to tell you about two memorable mistakes I've made. The first one 
happened when I was living in New York City and working in publications. We had 
one project where we were making a cover for a DVD which was of a debate 
between two speakers, so we had to get approval from both of them on the design.  
Well, there were a lot of revisions and changes, and the whole thing just dragged on 
and on until we were right up against the deadline. So when the designer sent me 
the final draft late one afternoon, I checked it over, it all looked good, and I 
approved it for printing.  
The problem was that I had forgotten to run it by the speakers who were featured 
in the DVD, and I later got an e-mail from one of them insisting that some details be 
changed in his bio. I tried to talk him out of it, but he wouldn’t budge. By that point a 
couple thousand DVD covers had already been printed – and we had to throw them 
away and print them all over again after making the changes.  
It was my fault, I should have followed the procedure – and it really bugs me when I 
make a mistake on something where I feel like I should've known better. But I was 
also worried because I was pretty new on the job – I had only been there a couple of 
months – and here I was making a mistake that actually cost the company money. 
So I was even wondering if I’d lose my job over it.  
I called my mom and she really helped put things in perspective. She pointed out 
that nobody got hurt, nobody got killed, and it’s not like it would cause any 
permanent damage to the company’s reputation. And luckily they didn’t fire me, my 
manager just said, “Don’t let it happen again!” 
The second mistake is funny in hindsight, but seemed like a disaster at the time. I 
was working for a religious organization, and we were going to send an e-mail to 
about 20,000 pastors across the country offering to give a presentation in their 


churches. I wrote a draft of the e-mail and sent it to the program manager for her to 
review. She said it was fine, and so I sent it to the director of publications, too – and 
she also approved it. So I clicked “send” and the message went out. 
Within hours we discovered that there was a typo in the phone number, which we 
had asked the pastors to call in order to schedule a presentation. Two of the 
numbers were switched, so instead of 5784 it said 5874 or something like that. Both 
the manager and the director had looked at it and given the green light, and none of 
us thought to check the phone number.  
Well, it wouldn’t have been so bad if the wrong number went to somebody’s house, 
or to a pizza delivery place or something… but unfortunately it led straight to a 
phone sex line, and we had just sent it out to thousands and thousands of religious 
leaders and encouraged them to call! 
So after a few panicked messages back and forth, we decided to send out another 
email with the subject line, “We made a big mistake!” and giving the correct phone 
number. What’s interesting is that more people actually opened the second e-mail 
than the first one – so all in all, no lasting harm was done. 
 
 
 
 
 
 


Extra Vocabulary 
"the whole thing just dragged on and on until we were right up against the 
deadline" 
The phrasal verb “drag on” means to last an unnecessarily long time. By 
repeating “on and on” it further emphasizes how long the process took. 
"I tried to talk him out of it, but he wouldn’t budge." 
To “talk someone out of” something means to convince them NOT to do it. 
However, the expression “he wouldn’t budge” means the person did not 
change his attitude; he stayed firm in his position. 
"she really helped put things in perspective" 
This expression means to consider things from a reasonable and objective 
perspective (not an emotional or exaggerated one) – usually when we are 
trying to make something that seems terrible appear not as bad by 
comparison.
