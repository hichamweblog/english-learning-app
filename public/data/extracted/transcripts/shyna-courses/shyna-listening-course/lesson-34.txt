Lesson 34: Achieving Your Goals 
Warm-Up: 
Describe a goal that you have worked hard for and 
accomplished. 
 
 
 
 
Activity 1 – Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
beat (v.) = to win against an opponent or overcome a challenge (this word has many 
meanings/uses, this is only one of them) 
Ex) The youngest swimmer in the competition managed to beat last year’s 
champion. 
throw in the towel = this idiom means to give up, to accept defeat and stop trying 
to achieve a goal 
Ex) After trying and failing to start eight different companies, Jeremy finally 
threw in the towel and got a regular job. 
overwhelmed (adj.) = to feel like an excess of things is too much for you to handle; 
it makes you feel stressed 
Ex) Parents with a new baby often feel overwhelmed by everything needed to 
take care of a newborn. 
milestone (n.) = an important point in a process, or a significant accomplishment in 
the course of history 
 
Ex) The invention of antibiotics was a milestone in modern medicine. 
stick with it (v.) = be persistent and continue doing something difficult 
Ex) I know that learning English can be frustrating, but if you stick with it 
you’ll see good results in time! 


procrastinate (v.) = delay doing a task that you need to do (often until the last 
moment) because you are lazy or not enthusiastic about doing it 
Ex) I know I need to study for the test tomorrow, but I’ve been procrastinating 
for the entire afternoon.

Complete Transcript 
Both of my parents have graduated with their associate degree, so it was really 
important for me to graduate with my bachelor's degree at least. So right out of 
high school I went to the University of Cincinnati, but I only made it about two 
semesters there before I gave up. I can't recall why, but I just decided that it wasn't 
for me to be there. 
I went on about, working some jobs, and then eventually I enrolled into a trade 
school for TV and radio - but I still always had it in the back of my mind that I 
wanted to graduate from college, and I'd heard the statistic about if you didn't go to 
college right outside of high school, then you probably wouldn't go back. So I was 
determined to beat that statistic. 
So eventually in 2005 I enrolled at a private college called the College of Mount 
Saint Joseph. Since I had been out of school for a couple of years at that point, it was 
a welcome change. I really enjoyed doing the work, and I actually joined to major in 
computer science - and I did pretty well for a couple of semesters. But then I had 
some very difficult personal circumstances arise and my grades suffered severely. I 
failed a couple of classes and I nearly failed a few more. 
I was about ready to just give up and throw in the towel, but I was determined to see 
this through, and so I reconsidered what I wanted to do in college. I ended up 
changing my major over to communications. I was still overwhelmed with the 
thought of completing it - I still had three years at that point. So what I decided to 
do was just kind of take everything one semester at a time, not focus on the long-
term goal of graduating, but focusing instead on the smaller short-term goals, 
giving myself these milestones to reach. 
Some classes were extremely boring to me, but I had a lot of classes that were a lot 
of fun. I especially enjoyed my creative writing classes and any opportunity where I 


had the chance to just use my imagination. I used the classes that I enjoyed to help 
me get through the classes that I didn't enjoy. 
It was a lot of hard work, there were a lot of sleepless nights, there were a lot of 
times when I procrastinated way too much and fell behind, but I stuck with it. I 
didn't let the hard times get me down. I kept my eye on the milestones and the goals 
that I had set for myself. 
Eventually I got to my senior year, it was the last semester of school. And we had 
our biggest project - we had to use everything that we had learned up until that 
point and put it together into a big presentation for the class. And I was so very 
nervous. I really do enjoy getting up in front of people and speaking, but the 
moments leading up to that are very stressful for me. 
So I did what I do best, and I used my imagination and I made this project as fun as 
I possibly could. I talked about the benefits of YouTube and its applications in the 
learning environment at the college. I met all of the scholastic requirements but I 
tried to make it as much fun as I could. And everybody loved it, it was a great 
presentation. 
And in May of 2008 I graduated from the College of Mount Saint Joseph with my 
bachelor's in communications. I was so happy to have finally accomplished the goal. 
It was such a long journey - I beat that statistic. I'm really happy that I can look back 
over my life and say that I went to college and I finished.  
 
 
 
 
 


Extra Vocabulary 
"Both of my parents have graduated with their associate degree, so it was really 
important for me to graduate with my bachelor's degree at least." 
An “associate degree” is a 2-year college degree, and a “bachelor’s degree” is a 
4-year college degree; it is considered more advanced. 
"I enrolled into a trade school for TV and radio" 
 
A “trade school” is one that teaches the skills for a specific profession. 
"I was determined to see this through" 
To “see something through” means to keep working on something until it is 
complete. 
"I kept my eye on the milestones and the goals that I had set for myself." 
To “keep your eye on” something is an expression that means to watch, focus 
on, or pay attention to it.
