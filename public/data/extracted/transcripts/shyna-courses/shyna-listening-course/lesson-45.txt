Activity 1 – Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
anomaly (n.) = something that is not normal 
 
Ex) The data is mostly consistent, but there are a few anomalies that I can’t 
explain. 
 
devise (v.) = create an idea or plan 
 
Ex) Dr. Evans devised a theory as to why the results of the experiment were 
different from what was expected. 
 
drawback (n.) = disadvantage, negative point 
 
 
Ex) One drawback of living in a big city is all the noise. It’s hardly ever quiet. 
 
gadget (n.) = a small mechanical or electronic device 
 
Ex) This little gadget shows you a digital display of the current temperature 
and humidity. 
 
meticulously (adv.) = very carefully and precisely 
 
 
Ex) The baker meticulously decorated the cake with hundreds of tiny flowers.  


mole (n.) = a small dark spot on the skin 
 
 
 
pediatrician (n.) = a doctor who specializes in treating children 
 
 
Ex) We took our son to three different pediatricians before getting a diagnosis.  
 
ventricle (n.) = the inner compartments of the heart are called “ventricles” 
 
 
 
viable (adj.) = able to be useful, successful, practical 
 
 
 
Ex) Bio-fuel is a viable alternative to gasoline.

Complete Transcript 
The world of medicine continues to make innovative leaps forward in identifying, 
treating, and even preventing certain diseases and conditions. Here are three 
developments that show great promise. 
 
Every year, about 1,000 children in the U.S. are born with only half of a functioning 
heart – a condition known as hypoplastic left heart syndrome. It is the result of a 
genetic anomaly that causes the left ventricle of the heart, which is the main 
pumping system, to be underdeveloped and unable to function at all.  
 
Without surgery, it is fatal – and even with surgery to redirect the blood flow into 
the right ventricle, over 30% of children born with this condition do not survive into 
adulthood. The problem is that the right side of the heart normally handles only the 
low-pressure pumping into the lungs, and it cannot sustain both the high- and low-
pressure systems for many years.  
 
However, researchers at Boston Children’s Hospital have devised a new method that 
takes advantage of the body’s natural healing processes. In a series of four 
surgeries, they meticulously route the blood flow into the left ventricle rather than 
away from it. This actually helps the left side of the heart grow and develop, and 
twelve of the patients in the experimental study ended up with two fully-functional 
ventricles. It does require longer hospitalization, but the long-term benefits 
definitely outweigh this drawback. 
 
Another new technique has the potential to revolutionize the treatment of cancer, 
which kills eight million people per year worldwide. While we still do not have a 
viable cure for cancer, there is a new type of genetic testing that can make 


treatment more specific and personalized. It tests a tumor sample for 280 genetic 
mutations that may affect the tumor’s growth.  
 
The results can identify medications that may work, even though they are not 
typically used for a particular type of cancer. For example, a woman with pancreatic 
cancer was tested with this new method, and her tumor was found to respond to a 
medication that is used primarily in breast cancer patients. These surprising 
connections lead to treatments that can target each patient’s tumor more 
effectively. 
 
A third trend in medicine aims to put health care literally in the palm of your hand – 
using a smartphone. There is one device that connects your smartphone to 
electrodes, records your heart rhythm and stores the data for a doctor to analyze. 
Although not as powerful as an electrocardiogram (EKG) machine, the smartphone 
gadget is still sufficient for routine testing or general heart rate monitoring.  
 
Another app helps patients track moles for signs of skin cancer, and one company is 
developing an iPhone-based instrument that will allow parents to check the inside 
of their children’s ears for infections and send photos to a pediatrician. Perhaps the 
biggest impact of this technology is its potential to reduce health care costs and 
empower people to take better care of their health on a day-to-day basis.
