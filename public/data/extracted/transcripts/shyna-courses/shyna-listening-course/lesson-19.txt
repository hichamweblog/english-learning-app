Lesson 19: The Origin of Santa Claus 
Do you know where the famous Christmas character 
of Santa Claus comes from? His origins come from a 
combination of cultural traditions and folkloric 
figures, and he has changed a lot over the years!  
To warm up, describe the Christmas traditions in 
your country/family: 
 
 
 
Activity 1 – Learn Vocabulary 
mythology (n.) – a collection of myths (stories and legends) about a 
people’s/culture’s origins, gods, heroes, etc. 
 
Ex) In Greek mythology, Zeus was the most powerful god. 
assertion (n.) – statement 
Ex) This article is not accurate; it makes a number of assertions that have 
no evidence to support them. 
chimney (n.) – the tube in your house that takes the 
smoke from the fireplace up and out. On Christmas 
Eve, Santa Claus is said to enter the house through 
the chimney, in order to deliver the presents. 
tale (n.) – story 
 
Ex) She entertained us at dinner with tales about her travels. 
depict (v.) – to represent in words or pictures 
Ex) Many European paintings depict Jesus as white, but in reality he 
probably had an olive-skinned complexion. 
far-fetched (adj.) – not easy to believe because of elements that are not likely 


Ex) Thanks to advancements in technology, many ideas that seemed far-
fetched in the past are now becoming reality. 
compelling (adj.) – very interesting and intriguing 
Ex) I’m watching a compelling documentary about history’s greatest 
unsolved mysteries. 
dowry (n.) – in some cultures, when a woman gets married, she or her family 
must give a gift of money or property to her husband. This gift is called a dowry. 
folkloric (adj.) – The word “folklore” refers to the traditional stories, beliefs, and 
myths of a people group – so something “folkloric” is related to these traditional 
stories, which may be true, imaginary, or exaggerated.  
Ex) In Mexico, we saw a folkloric dance group perform some of the country’s 
traditional dances. 
mischievous (adj.) – A person who is “mischievous” is playful and likes to have 
fun and get into trouble, often bothering other people or playing jokes/tricks on 
other people for their own amusement. 
 
Ex) My kids are very mischievous and often get in trouble at school. 
ascertain (v.) – come to a conclusion after observation or examination 
Ex) Scientists monitored the animals to ascertain the effect of the chemicals 
on their health. 
typify (v.) – be typical of something, be the perfect representation of something 
Ex) This painting typifies the artist’s work – it is very characteristic of her 
style. 
treat (n.) – something that is a special source of delight or pleasure – can be a 
special food, or a special experience 
Ex) I grew up in a big city, and it was a real treat to go camping in the 
countryside every once in a while. 
wish list (n.) – a list of things you want or hope for. These are usually objects 
that you want to buy (or hope to receive as gifts) but can also be 
events/experiences 
 
Ex) Wow, I love those shoes – they’re definitely going on my wish list.

Complete Transcript 
The story of the origin of our dear old Santa Claus is fascinating – there are several 
major characters that have contributed to the creation of this cultural legend.  
Most people in the West have been told that Santa Claus is simply a caricature of 
Saint Nicholas, who lived in Asia Minor around 270-343 AD. However there are a 
huge number of elements and symbols surrounding Santa Claus that are derived 
from Norse mythology.  
For example, in Scandinavian households the god by the name of Odin was usually 
said to have left presents for the children, and the assertion that Santa climbs 
down into your house through the chimney developed from a tale in Old Norse 
legend wherein the goddess Hertha manifested in the fireplace and brought good 
luck to the home. 
Odin was depicted as a tall, old man with a white beard, wearing a cloak, who rode 
the skies and the seas on his lightning-fast eight-legged horse – you can see that 
the relation to our modern concept of Santa Claus soaring through the sky with 
his reindeer is not too far-fetched.   
Later, as Christianity evolved, the compelling figure of Saint Nicholas comes 
along. Saint Nicholas was a Christian bishop who lived in the region of modern-day 
Turkey. He was known for his benevolence and for performing miracles to help 
the poor. One tale says that he miraculously supplied gifts of gold to three girls as 
marriage dowries so that they could avoid the fate of becoming prostitutes. Saint 
Nicholas’ feast day (and perhaps his birthday) was in early December. 
Over time and after the Reformation, Saint Nicholas was largely forgotten, except 
for in Holland – where he had become the folkloric figure of Sinterklaas; a wise and 
kindly old man with a white beard, dressed in white and red clothing and riding 
the skies and roofs of the houses on his horse, accompanied by his mischievous 
helpers.  
These helpers, which would later become elves, had the uncanny ability to 
ascertain your behavior and know whether you deserved presents or harsh 
punishment – this is thought to be a Christian disciplinarian element. If you were 
bad, you were said to be sent in a bag to Spain, where Sinterklaas was said to 
reside.  
In the 17th century, the Dutch took to the seas and arrived in North America, 
bringing their Sinterklaas stories with them. This name was anglicized to Santa 
Claus, and merged with the English figure known as “Father Christmas,” a large 
man in fur-lined robes who typified the spirit of good cheer at Christmastime, 
bringing peace, joy, and good food and wine. 


Sometime in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, the eight-legged horse was 
replaced with a fleet of reindeer and Santa Claus’ residence was moved from Spain 
to the North Pole. In 1930, the designer for Coca Cola was asked to come up with a 
winter advertisement for the company using Coca-Cola’s traditional red and white 
colors, thus popularizing the modern image of Santa Claus. 
Today, it is traditional for children to leave out treats for Santa such as milk and 
cookies, as well as to write him letters with their Christmas wish lists. When 
mailed, these letters are actually answered by postal workers and volunteers; in 
2009 Canadian workers replied to more than 1 million letters to Santa Claus 
written in 30 different languages. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 


Extra Vocabulary 
“Santa Claus is simply a caricature of Saint Nicholas” 
caricature (n.) – an exaggerated representation of a person 
“Odin was depicted as a tall, old man with a white beard, wearing a cloak” 
cloak (n.) – a long item of outer clothing that covers most of the body 
“so that they could avoid the fate of becoming prostitutes.” 
fate (n.) – destiny, end result, something that happens to you which you 
cannot control 
“These helpers, which would later become elves, had the uncanny ability to 
ascertain your behavior” 
uncanny (adj.) – so unusual that it seems a little bit supernatural, and may 
be disturbing 
“This name was anglicized to Santa Claus, and merged with the English figure 
known as Father Christmas.” 
anglicize (v.) – make more similar to English in form or style. For example, 
many immigrants who have names that are difficult for English speakers to 
pronounce “anglicize” their names when they come to the U.S. 
merge (v.) – combine, when two things mix and come together into one 
“a fleet of reindeer” 
 fleet (n.) – a group of things operated as a unit. 
We usually refer to a “fleet” of ships on the ocean. 
reindeer (n.) – the animals that are said to pull 
Santa’s sleigh 
“popularizing the modern image of Santa Claus.” 
popularize (v.) – to make popular and common, make something known 
by many people
