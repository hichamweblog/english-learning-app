Activity 1 – Pre-Learn Vocabulary 
creepy (adj.) = producing an unpleasant sensation of uneasiness or fear 
 
Ex) I don’t like to walk through the cemetery at night; it’s creepy. 
propagate (v.) = spread something (often information or a belief) to a large 
number of people  
 
Ex) I don’t think kids should play video games that propagate violence. 
debunk (v.) = expose to be false or exaggerated 
Ex) Modern science has debunked many beliefs that were commonly 
accepted as truth in the past. 
striking (adj.) = strongly attracting your attention, making a strong impression 
on you 
 
Ex) Janet has black hair and strikingly beautiful blue eyes. 
harness (n.) = the equipment worn on the body to prevent you from falling – 
usually consisting of straps around your waist and legs: 
 


priest (n.) = a religious leader who leads rituals and ceremonies – usually in the 
Catholic church, but this term may be used in other religions as well. 
 
Ex) The priest said a prayer for the sick child. 
gruesome (adj.) = causing horror, frightening and shocking (often related to 
death or injury) 
Ex) The police broke into the house to find a gruesome scene – ten dead 
bodies in various states of decay. 
drain (n.) = the holes in the floor or ground where water can go down: 
 
strangle (v.) = kill someone by squeezing their neck so they can’t breathe 
 
Ex) After the gun malfunctioned, the murderer strangled his victim.

Complete Transcript 
An “urban legend” is a story that is often creepy or sensationalistic and which may 
or may not be true. These stories are retold by many people, picking up different 
variations along the way. Urban legends usually propagate by word of mouth, 
which is now accelerated by email and social media. They have even inspired the 
creation of websites and TV shows aiming to debunk them. 
However, there are a striking number of traditional urban legends that have their 
basis in reality. One such story is about events in a horror film happening for real 
on set. A prominent example of this is The Exorcist, a film about the demonic 
possession of a 12-year-old girl, which suffered a number of mysterious problems 
during its filming.  
Linda Blair, who played the afflicted young girl in the movie, was injured as her 
safety harness inexplicably failed, and a large fire (one of several during filming) 
destroyed much of the set. In addition, two of the film’s actors died prematurely 
shortly after filming – all this despite the director inviting a priest to give a 
blessing and reassure the cast. The problems continued during the later 
production of the prequel, when the two directors both experienced the same 
disturbing visions!  
Outside of Hollywood, perhaps one of the most gruesome urban legends is the one 
where you and your loved one check into a motel. You are getting comfortable in 
bed when you begin to notice a noxious odor coming from somewhere in the room. 
You check the bathroom to see if the smell is coming from the drain or toilet, and 
imagine that there might be a dead rat under the floorboards. But you’re too tired 
to complain to the motel staff, so you make do and drift off to sleep – and all the 
while, a dead body lies decomposing beneath your mattress.  
This is a horrible notion, and even worse is the fact that it has actually happened 
on multiple occasions in the U.S. in a number of different cities! One of the oldest 
“corpse under the bed” incidents is from 1982, involving three men who ran a 


criminal operation together. Two of them decided to kill the third, so they fed him 
a hamburger  containing cyanide while staying in a motel. 
The poison apparently took too long to produce its desired effect, and the two 
criminals, impatient to finish the deed, strangled their victim and hid the body 
under the bed. It was found four days later – after several other guests had stayed 
in the room. They had all put up with the foul smell, but no one – not even the 
cleaning staff – had bothered to look under the bed. 
So next time you hear an urban legend, make sure to check the facts before passing 
it on – but remember, there’s a slim chance that it might be true! 
 
Extra Vocabulary 
“her safety harness inexplicably failed” 
 
The word “inexplicably” means “without an explanation or reason.” 
“But you’re too tired to complain to the motel staff, so you make do and drift off 
to sleep” 
The expression “make do” means you manage to accept an inconvenient or 
difficult situation and continue to do what you had planned. 
“a dead body lies decomposing beneath your mattress” 
The verb “decomposing” describes the process of something that was once 
alive, but is now dead, breaking down and decaying over time  
“They had all put up with the foul smell” 
The phrasal verb “put up with” means “tolerated,” and a “foul” smell is a 
disgusting smell.
