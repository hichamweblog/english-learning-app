Activity 1 – Learn Vocabulary 
crops (n.) = a general word for plants that are cultivated for food such as rice, corn, 
fruit, vegetables, etc. 
 
sever (v.) = cut off 
Ex) There was a terrible accident at the factory; a worker’s arm was severed 
by a machine. 
fossil fuels (n.) = petroleum, coal, and natural gas – hydrocarbon substances that 
are used for providing power 
 
Ex) Wind energy and solar energy are natural alternatives to fossil fuels. 
dwindle (v.) = to become less and less until nothing remains 
Ex) After being unemployed for ten months, his savings have dwindled and 
now he’s even more desperate to find a job. 
revitalize (v.) = to give new energy or excitement 


Ex) The country is doing a big international advertising campaign in order to 
revitalize its tourism industry.  
pesticides (n.) = chemicals used to kill insects so that 
they don’t damage plants 
gravitate (v.) = to be strongly attracted to something as 
if by an invisible force that you can’t resist 
Ex) Taylor is a natural leader; people just seem to gravitate towards him. 
aesthetic (adj.) = related to appearance and beauty 
Ex) The surgery wasn’t medically necessary; she had the operation for 
aesthetic reasons. 
hub (n.) = a center of activity 
Ex) Silicon Valley in California is considered the technology hub of the United 
States; many of the world’s largest tech companies are located there.  
greenhouse (n.) = a special building, usually made of glass, for growing plants, 
flowers, and vegetables inside

Complete Transcript 
Urban agriculture is, quite simply, the practice of cultivating food in a town or city 
environment. It is not a new concept; for many centuries, humans have tended to 
crops and raised animals in and around cities.  
However, urban farming fell out of favor in the second half of the twentieth 
century. City planners in many countries – especially in the US – have sought to 
sever the links between the city and the country, creating the idea of the modern 
metropolis as being distinct from the rural and agricultural areas. In addition, 
economic development has led to a shift in the workforce; nowadays, not as many 
people pursue farming as their main livelihood. But now the ancient practice of 
urban agriculture is coming back into vogue. 
One reason for the renewed interest in urban farming is the rising prices of fossil 
fuels, which makes it expensive to transport food from faraway farms into the 
cities. This is prompting people to look for more local food sources – such as fruits 
and vegetables grown in backyards and on balconies. As environmental resources 
dwindle, urban agriculture offers a potential solution for providing food to the 
global population. It is projected that sixty percent of the world’s people will live 
in cities by the year 2030. 
Another factor that has revitalized the urban agriculture movement is the growing 
demand for organic food. Urban gardens often use sustainable farming practices 
and avoid pesticides. Nowadays, more and more consumers are gravitating to 
fresh, locally-grown food as opposed to the mass-produced and industrialized 
items found in big supermarkets. Food grown in urban gardens is also more 
nourishing because of the decrease in time between production and consumption 
– in the 5-10 days it takes to transport food from the farm to the dinner table, 30-
50% of the nutrients can be lost. 
Besides the practical benefits, urban agriculture also has an element of aesthetic 
appeal – city gardens add a cheerful touch of greenery to the stark urban 
landscape. Urban farming projects can also improve the overall health of a 
neighborhood. People who participate in caring for community gardens report 
less stress, better social relationships, and increased community pride. In some 
cases, the crime and suicide rates even go down in areas that practice urban 
agriculture. 
Even New York City, one of the largest cities in the world, is becoming a hub for 
urban agriculture, with more than 600 projects throughout the city’s 


neighborhoods. Fruit and vegetables are grown in rooftop greenhouses and 
agricultural spaces within city parks.  
Most of the programs are run by volunteers, and a number have free educational 
programs in which participants can learn about nutrition and farming. There is 
even a restaurant which serves food from its own rooftop garden; the menu 
changes based on which foods are in season. 
 
Extra Vocabulary 
"humans have tended to crops and raised animals in and around cities" 
 
In this context, “tend to crops” means to care for them. 
"urban farming fell out of favor" 
 
If something falls out of favor, it means it becomes generally unpopular 
"not as many people pursue farming as their main livelihood." 
Your “livelihood” is your occupation or employment; the way you earn 
money to live. 
"the ancient practice of urban agriculture is coming back into vogue." 
 
If something is “in vogue,” it means it is popular and fashionable. 
"Urban gardens often use sustainable farming practices" 
“Sustainable” practices are ones that help preserve the environment and 
resources. 
"city gardens add a cheerful touch of greenery to the stark urban landscape." 
Describing something as “stark” means it has little or no beauty or 
decoration. 
"the menu changes based on which foods are in season." 
If a fruit or vegetable is “in season,” it means it is currently the right time of 
year to get that fruit or vegetable naturally.
