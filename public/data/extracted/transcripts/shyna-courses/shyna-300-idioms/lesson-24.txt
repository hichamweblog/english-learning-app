Lesson 24: Idioms with DO / DON’T 
 
Introductory Quiz 
Guess the correct meaning of each idiom from the context. It's OK if you get a lot 
of answers wrong – what’s important is to do your best in trying to figure it out! 
1) I did a double-take when my husband shaved off his beard - I almost 
didn't recognize him! 
Do a double-take means... 
A. disapprove strongly 
B. look twice due to surprise 
C. make a shocked noise 
 
2) The hurricane really did a number on our property. Five of our trees fell 
over. 
Do a number on something means... 
A. cause multiple things to fail 
B. damage or harm 
C. take a long time to pass 
 
3) The boss always comes up with great ideas, but then he makes us do the 
dirty work. 
Do the dirty work means... 
A. do extra work 
B. do something illegal 
C. do the unpleasant tasks 
 


4) When the heating system was blowing out cold air, we replaced the filter 
and that did the trick. 
Do the trick means... 
A. accomplish what is needed 
B. cost some money 
C. do something surprising 
 
5) That product wasn't particularly profitable, so the company decided to do 
away with it. 
Do away with something means... 
A. change it 
B. eliminate it 
C. give it for free 
 
6) When I was a teenager, I rebelled against my parents' expectations that I 
always do their bidding. 
Do someone's bidding means... 
A. do a favor for someone 
B. do the household chores 
C. do what the person has ordered 
 
7) Six months after the breakup, Gary is still hoping his ex-girlfriend will give 
him another chance. I told him, "Don't hold your breath." 
Don't hold your breath means... 
A. don't focus too much on one thing/person 
B. something will probably never happen 
C. you can't do anything to improve the situation 
 


8) I borrowed $5 from Tim and promised to pay him back the next day, but he 
said, "Don't sweat it!" 
Don't sweat it means... 
A. don't get angry 
B. don't go too fast 
C. don't worry about it 
 
9) I was about to get a soda from the vending machine, but then my co-worker 
said, "Don't bother - it's been broken for weeks." 
Don't bother means... 
A. don't wait for something to happen 
B. it's not worth trying 
C. stop annoying me 
 
10) I hated living in Canada. Don't get me wrong - the people are great, but I 
couldn't stand the weather. 
Don't get me wrong means... 
A. don't correct me 
B. don't disagree with me 
C. don't misunderstand me 
 
 
 
 
 
 


Explanation 
If you do a double-take, (B) it means you look at something two times 
because you are surprised or shocked, and you almost couldn't believe it the 
first time you saw it. So you look again to check and confirm that what you're 
seeing is really correct. 
To do a number on something (B) means to seriously damage or harm that 
thing. We can also say that problems or tragedies do a number on a person if 
they severely affect that person's emotional well-being. 
Doing the dirty work (C) refers to doing unpleasant or tedious tasks that 
most people wouldn't want to do... but someone has to do them! 
Saying that something did the trick (A) means it accomplished what you 
were trying to do. We often use this expression when something works 
successfully to solve a problem. In the example, replacing the filter did the 
trick to fix the problem in the heating system.  
To do away with something (B) means to eliminate it or discontinue it; to 
stop having it. In the example, the company did away with an unprofitable 
product - it eliminated a product that wasn't making much money. 
If you do someone's bidding, (C) it means you do what the person has 
ordered you to do. Soldiers have to do the bidding of their commanding 
officers, and employees have to do the bidding of their bosses. 
We can only hold our breath (stop our respiration) for a very short time; it's 
impossible to hold your breath for a long time. So telling someone "Don't hold 
your breath" (B) means "Don't wait, because this thing will probably never 
happen, even if you wait a long time." 
Don't sweat it (C) is an informal way to say, "Don't worry about it," don't 
spend time/energy being upset or anxious about it. 
If you tell someone "don't bother," (B) it means "don't try, because it won't 
work or it's not worth the effort." In the example, the vending machine is 


broken, so my coworker says, "don't bother" putting in money, because it will 
have no result. 
"Don't get me wrong" (C) is a way to say, "don't misunderstand me" - we can 
use this when we say something that a person might be confused or offended 
by. In this example, saying you hated living in Canada could be offensive 
because maybe you hated the people. So you can say, "Don't get me wrong" 
and then clarify that you hated the weather, not the Canadians themselves.
