Lesson 21: Idioms with MAKE 
 
Introductory Quiz 
Guess the correct meaning of each idiom from the context. It's OK if you get a lot 
of answers wrong – what’s important is to do your best in trying to figure it out! 
1) Gary bought a house on the beach 20 years ago, and made a killing when 
he sold it last month. 
Make a killing means… 
A. do something very quickly 
B. make a lot of money 
C. take advantage of someone 
 
2) Some of the clothing that was in style when I was a teenager is now making 
a comeback. 
Make a comeback means… 
A. be available at a lower price 
B. remind you of the past 
C. return to being popular/successful 
 
3) I didn’t stop at the new restaurant, but I made a mental note of the name 
so that I could go there later. 
Make a mental note means… 
A. consider whether something is good 
B. make a special effort to remember 
C. think something is interesting 
 


4) There was one punctuation mistake in the published article, and my boss 
yelled at the editing team for an hour. He likes to make a mountain out of a 
molehill. 
Make a mountain out of a molehill means… 
A. be obsessed with perfection 
B. have an unpleasant personality 
C. treat something as worse than it really is 
 
5) The professor was fired after he made a pass at one of his attractive

Make a pass at someone means… 
A. flirt with 
B. give special treatment 
C. have sex with 
 
6) This cake is delicious! Did you make it from scratch? 
Make something from scratch means… 
A. make it for the first time 
B. make it yourself with original ingredients 
C. make it with professional training 
 
7) When I told my kids they couldn’t have dessert, they made a scene and we 
had to leave the restaurant. 
Make a scene means… 
A. behave overly dramatically 
B. create a mess 
C. run away 
 


8) The supermarket was out of brown sugar, so I’ll have to make do with 
regular white sugar. 
Make do means… 
A. cook a meal 
B. find something 
C. use the resources available 
 
9) After I lost my job, I took on some part-time work just to make ends meet. 
Make ends meet means… 
A. occupy time 
B. start saving money 
C. survive financially 
 
10) I can’t make up my mind on whether to buy the red shirt or the blue 
shirt. 
Make up your mind means… 
A. decide 
B. imagine 
C. have time to think about 
 
Explanation 
“Make a bundle,” “make a killing,” and “make a fortune” (B) are all 
informal ways to say, “make a LOT of money.” We can use these expressions 
for money gained from working, from selling something, or from investing. 


“Make a comeback” (C) refers to this situation: when something was popular 
or successful in the past, then the popularity or success decreased for a time, 
and now the popularity is growing again. When the thing is becoming 
successful for the second time, we say it is “making a comeback.” You can also 
use this idiom in sports: if a team is losing 2-0 in the beginning of the game, 
they can “make a comeback” and win 3-2. 
“Make a metal note” (B) means that you make a special effort to remember 
something – as if you were “writing it down” inside your head – because the 
information will be useful in the future.  
A “molehill” is a very small pile of dirt made by an animal called a mole, and a 
mountain is huge. So to “make a mountain out of a molehill” (C) means to 
treat something – usually a problem – like it was bigger and much worse than 
it really is.  
  
 
To “make a pass at” (A) someone means to flirt or to act/comment in a way 
that implies you are romantically or sexually interested in that person. 


If you “make something from scratch,” (B) then you make it yourself from 
original ingredients. This idiom is usually used with food, when you prepare 
the food yourself instead of buying a ready-made dish. 
To “make a scene” (A) means to be loud or badly behaved in public, or acting 
in an overly dramatic way that attracts the attention of other people. This is 
not considered a good thing; it is not considered acceptable to make a scene in 
public because it shows a lack of self-control or social awareness. 
The expression “make do” (C) means manage to do something with the 
resources available, when those resources aren’t ideal. If you don’t have the 
things you need, you’ll have to “make do” with what you have and find a 
creative way to accomplish your purpose anyway. 
To “make ends meet” (C) means to have enough money to pay for basic 
expenses to survive. This expression is usually used when someone is having 
financial difficulty, so they are having trouble making ends meet, or they can 
barely make ends meet. 
The expression “make up your mind” (A) simply means to decide. It’s a very 
common way of talking informally about decisions.
