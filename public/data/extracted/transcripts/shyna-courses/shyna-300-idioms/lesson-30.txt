Lesson 30: Idioms with LIKE 
Introductory Quiz 
Guess the correct meaning of each idiom from the context. It's OK if you get a lot 
of answers wrong – what’s important is to do your best in trying to figure it out! 
1) My best friend lives in New York City, and she felt like a fish out of water 
when I took her on a camping trip. 
Like a fish out of water means... 
A. away from one's normal environment 
B. in a dangerous situation 
C. in the middle of nature 
 
2) Barbara's singing is nothing special, but she can dance like nobody's 
business. 
Like nobody's business means... 
A. not professionally 
B. very much or very well 
C. without anybody watching 
 
3) I've never spoken in front of a big audience, and I'm worried that once I'm 
on stage I'll just stand there like a deer in the headlights. 
Like a deer in the headlights means... 
A. difficult to see 
B. paralyzed with fear 
C. the center of attention 
 
4) My mother loves buying unique things, so when I took her to a big antiques 
shop, she was like a kid in a candy store. 


Like a kid in a candy store means... 
A. excited 
B. hungry 
C. immature 
 
5) Basketball is such a fast-paced game for fans, but baseball is like watching 
paint dry. 
Like watching paint dry means... 
A. boring 
B. easy 
C. simple 
 
6) Trying to find a single document in these disorganized boxes of archives is 
like looking for a needle in a haystack. 
Like looking for a needle in a haystack means... 
A. almost impossible to find 
B. making a big mess 
C. needing a lot of help 
 
7) Alan once got sick from eating pork, and he's avoided it like the plague 
ever since. 
Avoid it like the plague means... 
A. avoid it completely 
B. avoid it for a good reason 
C. avoid it temporarily 
 
8) I can't stand taking the subway at rush hour because everyone's packed 
like sardines - you barely have room to breathe. 


Packed like sardines means... 
A. extremely crowded 
B. in a rush 
C. smell bad 
 
9) When I brought out a big bowl of candy, all the kids came running like 
moths to a flame. 
Like moths to a flame means... 
A. there are too many 
B. with intense interest/desire 
C. without thinking 
 
10) My son is quite adaptable; when we moved to another country with a very 
different culture, he took to it like a duck to water. 
Take to it like a duck to water means... 
A. do many different activities 
B. get used to something easily 
C. travel in the local area 
 
Explanation 
The natural home of a fish is in the water - so to feel/be like a fish out of 
water (A) means to feel out of place, because you are outside your normal 
environment. This feeling can be a little awkward or uncomfortable because 
you are not used to the situation. 
To do something like nobody’s business (B) means to do it a lot (very 
intensely) or do it very well, have a lot of skills in that activity – as in the 
example, where Barbara can’t sing very well, but she is an extremely talented 
dancer. 


This animal is called a deer. If a deer is crossing the street and a car 
approaches with its headlights on, the deer will often "freeze" (stop moving) 
because it is afraid. If you are like a deer in the headlights, (B) it means you 
are paralyzed with fear or surprise. 
 
A kid (child) in a candy store (A) would be excited and eager, wanting to eat 
all the candy - so this expression is used to describe when someone is very 
excited, especially when they have the opportunity to try or buy many 
different things that they love. 
Like watching paint dry (A) is a way to describe something that is extremely 
boring. Paint takes a long time to dry and there would be nothing interesting 
about watching the process. 
 
A needle is very small, and a haystack is a pile of dry grass often found on 
farms. It would be very difficult - almost impossible - to find a single needle 
hidden in a large haystack, so “like looking for a needle in a haystack” (A) is 
used to describe anything that's almost impossible to find. 


The word plague refers to any disease that is dangerous and can spread from 
person to person. If there was a plague in your area, you would do everything 
possible to avoid catching the disease! So the expression avoid something 
like the plague (A) means to avoid it completely, avoid it at all costs. 
Sardines are a small type of fish that is often sold in cans or tins, in which 
there are many sardines in a small space. When we say people are packed 
like sardines, (A) it means the space is extremely crowded and 
uncomfortable. 
 
Flying insects like moths are attracted to light, such as the flame of a fire. 
When people come to something like moths to a flame, (B) it means they 
come close with intense interest or desire. 
  
 


A duck can swim in water easily and naturally. When we say someone "took 
to (a situation/activity) like a duck to water," (B) it means the person 
adapted or got used to the situation/activity easily and naturally, without 
difficulty.
