Explanation 
#1 - at a crossroads = (A) – at a point where a decision needs to 
be made 
 
The word crossroads means an intersection - a place where two or more 
streets come together, so you need to decide which way to go.  
 
The idiom "at a crossroads" can be used in any situation when you need to 
make a decision - especially a decision that will have significant effects in the 
future. 
#2 - jump on the bandwagon = (A) – do what is popular 
 
The word bandwagon comes from the 19th century and refers to a vehicle 
that carried the musical band for a circus. Because the circus was very 
popular, lots of people followed the bandwagon - so the expression "jump on 


the bandwagon" describes doing what is popular and what everyone else is 
doing. 
#3 - steer clear of = (B) – avoid 
 
You use the steering wheel of a car to control the vehicle and steer 
(maneuver) away from dangerous obstacles - so the idiom "steer clear of" 
means to avoid something. It can be used in any situation, not just driving - for 
example: 
• "To lose weight, steer clear of fried foods." 
• "Vanessa's in a terrible mood today - try to steer clear of her!" 
 
#4 - put the brakes on = (C) – slow the progress 
 
The word brakes refers to the equipment used to stop or slow the movement 
of a vehicle such as a bike, car, motorcycle, or train - so putting the brakes on a 
project or a process means to slow it down. 
#5 - running on fumes = (C) – very tired 
 
If a car has no more gasoline, you can say it is "running on fumes" - fumes are 
vapors, so you are saying that there is no more gasoline in the tank, only 
gasoline vapors.  


This idiom is used both when talking about literal cars and when talking about 
yourself - if you are running on fumes, you're very tired and you don't have 
much energy. 
#6 - rock the boat = (B) – disturb a stable situation 
 
A boat needs to be perfectly balanced to stay stable in the water. The verb 
"rock" means to move from side to side - and if you do this movement while 
on a boat, you run the risk of the boat turning over and everybody falling into 
the water. 
 
People often say, "don't rock the boat" meaning "don't do anything to disturb 
this situation, because it could result in disaster." 
#7 - in a rut = (A) – in an established way of living that never 
changes 
 


A rut is a depression in the ground or in a road. If you start driving in a rut, it's 
hard to get out. So if your life is in a rut, it means that you feel stuck or trapped 
in a path that doesn't change. 
 
#8 - right up your alley = (B) – a perfect match for your 
interests and abilities 
 
An alley is a narrow street or corridor. If a job or activity is "right up your 
alley," it means it is perfect for your specific interests and abilities. 
 
#9 - jump ship = (A) – abandon a job or activity suddenly 
 
In the past, sailors were contracted for the full duration of the ship's voyage. If 
they wanted to abandon their responsibility, they couldn't do it easily because 


of the contract obligation - so they had to jump off the side of the ship and 
swim to shore!  
The idiom "jump ship" is most often used today when someone suddenly quits 
their job or leaves a group or activity. 
#10 - dead in the water = (A) – completely stopped 
 
If a ship is damaged and it cannot move anywhere, it is said to be "dead in the 
water." You can use this idiom for any project that is completely stopped - 
especially when there appears to be no obvious chance to get the process 
moving again. 
#11 - that ship has sailed = (B) – that opportunity has passed by 
 
If you want to take a trip, but you arrive late at the harbor and the ship has 
already sailed away, then you have missed your opportunity to travel. The 
expression "that ship has sailed" describes any opportunity that has passed 
and is no longer available to you. 
#12 - in the same boat = (C) – in the same situation 
 
This idiom is pretty self-explanatory - if two or more people are "in the same 
boat," then they are in exactly the same situation. This idiom is often used 
when the shared situation is problematic in some way: 
• “The teacher forgot to tell us what's going to be on the test, so we're all 
in the same boat - we have no idea what to study.”
