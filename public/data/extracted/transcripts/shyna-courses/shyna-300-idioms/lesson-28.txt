Lesson 28: Idioms with KEEP 
Introductory Quiz 
Guess the correct meaning of each idiom from the context. It's OK if you get a lot 
of answers wrong – what’s important is to do your best in trying to figure it out!

up from the train station. 
Keep someone posted means... 
A. continue to inform them 
B. give them your contact information 
C. take their opinion into account 
 
2) I'd like to buy my husband a tablet, so I'm keeping my eyes peeled for a 
sale on electronics. 
Keep your eyes peeled means... 
A. actively searching in various places 
B. hoping for something good 
C. watching until something appears 
 
3) When something incredibly unfair happens to me, I find it very hard to 
keep my cool. 
Keep your cool means... 
A. avoid speaking 
B. ignore something 
C. stay calm 
 
4) Celebrities often try to keep a low profile while on vacation so that they 
don't get bothered by photographers and fans. 


Keep a low profile means... 
A. avoid attracting attention 
B. go to remote places 
C. take security measures 
 
5) When I saw that my boss had toilet paper stuck to his shoe, I could barely 
keep a straight face. 
Keep a straight face means... 
A. avoid smiling or laughing 
B. look away from something 
C. take someone seriously 
 
6) I'm sending in my application for a major scholarship - keep your fingers

Keep your fingers crossed means... 
A. help write something 
B. shake hands 
C. wish for good luck 
 
7) She prefers running and swimming over team sports because she likes to 
keep to herself. 
Keep to yourself means... 
A. keep a secret 
B. take care of your health 
C. tend to be solitary 
 
8) If he didn't take medication to keep his disease in check, it would just get 
progressively worse. 


Keep something in check means... 
A. control and restrain it 
B. monitor it 
C. resolve a problem 
 
9) There are so many irregular verbs in English that it's impossible to keep 
them straight! 
Keep things straight means... 
A. create a system for organizing them 
B. understand them clearly without confusing them 
C. use them correctly all the time 
 
10) The guy is on the suspected terrorist list, so the police have been keeping 
tabs on him for years. 
Keep tabs on means... 
A. ask questions 
B. try to capture 
C. watch and monitor 
 
Explanation 
To keep someone posted (A) means to continue to inform them, to update 
them with the most current information about a situation that may change or 
develop over time. Another idiom for this is "keep someone in the loop." 
If you're keeping your eyes peeled, (C) it means you're watching the general 
area until something appears (something you are waiting for or want to 
notice). Another expression for this is to "keep an eye out for something." 
Keep your cool (C) is an informal way to talk about staying calm. The 
opposite is “lose your cool” - that's when you get angry and lose control of 


your attitude/behavior. For staying calm, you can also say, "keep a cool 
head" or "keep a level head." 
To keep a low profile (A) means to do things to avoid attracting attention. 
You keep a low profile when you don't want people to notice you, as in the 
example of celebrities keeping a low profile while on vacation so that they 
won’t constantly be approached and bothered. 
If you keep a straight face, (A) it means you avoid smiling or laughing. We 
usually try to keep a straight face in situations where it would be 
inappropriate to show amusement, or where it would embarrass the other 
person if we started laughing at them. 
To keep your fingers crossed (C) means to wish for good luck. Sometimes 
people will do the literal gesture of crossing two fingers when saying or 
hearing this phrase. You can keep your own fingers crossed (wishing good 
luck for yourself) or you can keep your fingers crossed for someone else 
(wishing good luck for that person). 
A person who keeps to himself/herself (C) tends to be solitary, they prefer 
to spend most of their time alone. Don't get this expression mixed up with the 
phrase "keep it/this/that to yourself," which means to keep a secret. 
To keep something in check (A) means to control or restrain it. Without this 
controlling/restraining action, the thing would progress too much, as in the 
example of a disease being kept in check by medication. 
Keep things straight (B) means to understand them clearly without 
confusing them. When there are many things, or similar things, it can be 
difficult to keep them straight in your head (in your mind/understanding). 
To keep tabs on someone or something (C) is an informal way to describe 
watching and monitoring it over time. We might keep tabs on 
someone/something potentially dangerous (as in the example of a possible 
terrorist) or on something neutral that we just want to know the status of, like 
keeping tabs on your friends through Facebook.
