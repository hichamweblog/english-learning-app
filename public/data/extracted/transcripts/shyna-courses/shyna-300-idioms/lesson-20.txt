Lesson 20: Idioms with HAVE 
 
Introductory Quiz 
Guess the correct meaning of each idiom from the context. It's OK if you get a lot 
of answers wrong – what’s important is to do your best in trying to figure it out! 
1) Claudia can convince anyone to change their mind. She really has a way 
with words. 
Have a way with words means… 
A. be a talented communicator 
B. have a charismatic personality 
C. have a large vocabulary 
 
2) I’m not usually a fan of pop music, but I have a soft spot for Taylor Swift 
because her show was the first one my boyfriend and I saw together. 
Have a soft spot for someone/something means… 
A. always want to see/hear them 
B. feel a special affection for them 
C. remember them very well 
 
3) We’re going to clear out the basement this weekend. It hasn’t been cleaned 
in three years, so we’ve got our work cut out for us! 
Have (got) your work cut out for you means… 
A. the work is clearly defined 
B. the work is difficult/extensive 
C. the work has not been done for a long time 
 


4) Diana has a big mouth. Don’t tell her anything about your personal life, 
unless you want everyone in the company to know. 
Have a big mouth means… 
A. has a wide smile 
B. can’t keep a secret 
C. is extroverted and talks a lot 
 
5) I have a bone to pick with you. Every time you leave the house, you forget 
to turn off the lights. 
Have a bone to pick with someone means… 
A. have a complaint to discuss 
B. notice a mistake 
C. personally dislike 
 
6) My daughter absolutely loves learning about animals and plants. I have a 
hunch she’ll be a biologist when she grows up. 
Have a hunch means… 
A. greatly hope 
B. have an intuitive feeling 
C. want to encourage 
 
7) Try not to say anything that might offend Bob. He has a short fuse. 
Have a short fuse means… 
A. get angry quickly and easily 
B. have strong and inflexible opinions 
C. not be very smart 
 
8) He has really good taste in clothes. All his outfits make him look great. 


Have good taste means… 
A. be up-to-date 
B. have good judgment/preferences 
C. spends a significant amount of money 
 
9) This laptop is very old and slow, and the screen is cracked. It has seen 
better days. 
Have seen better days means… 
A. can’t be fixed 
B. is currently in bad condition 
C. is not very useful 
 
10) I don’t have time to play around on Facebook. I have bigger fish to fry. 
Have bigger fish to fry means… 
A. have more important things to do 
B. have to prepare some food 
C. enjoy more sophisticated entertainment 
 
Explanation 
If someone “has a way with words,” (A) it means that they are a talented 
communicator – someone who speaks or writes in a unique, interesting, and 
effective way. 
When you “have a soft spot for” (B) someone or something, it means you feel 
a special affection for them. You may or may not know the specific reason why 
you have this feeling.  
If you “have (got) your work cut out for you,” (B) it means you have a 
difficult task to do in the future. This can be used for things at your job, or 


other tasks in life – as in the example of cleaning out the basement after 
several years. 
If someone “has a big mouth,” (B) it doesn’t mean their mouth is literally big 
– it means they can’t keep a secret, and like to gossip. In other words, they 
tend to share private and/or scandalous information about other people. 
If you “have a bone to pick with” (A) someone, then you have a complaint to 
discuss with that person. This is just an informal way of describing the 
situation. 
To “have a hunch” (B) means you have an intuitive feeling about something. 
Sometimes you can identify the logical reasons behind the hunch, but other 
times you can’t explain it rationally, but you feel it’s true. 
Someone who “has a short fuse,” (A) gets angry quickly 
and easily. The small string on a piece of dynamite is 
called a fuse, and if the fuse is short, it will not be long 
before it explodes. 
When you say someone “has good taste,” (B) you’re not talking about eating 
food. In this expression, the word “taste” means a person’s preferences and 
judgment about what is of good quality. It’s often used when it comes to visual 
things (clothes, decoration, etc.) and books/movies/music. 
If an object “has seen better days,” (B) it means that in the past it was in 
good condition, but now it’s in bad condition because it has been used so 
much over time. 
The expression “have bigger fish to fry” (A) means “have more important 
things to do.” This idiom is usually used in contrast with the thing you view as 
less important, as in the example of playing on Facebook vs. having more 
important ways to spend one’s time.
