Lesson 22: Idioms with PUT 
 
Introductory Quiz 
Guess the correct meaning of each idiom from the context. It's OK if you get a lot 
of answers wrong – what’s important is to do your best in trying to figure it out! 
1) Everyone likes to make predictions about what’s going to happen during 
the next ten years, but I don’t put a lot of stock in them. Nobody can really 
know the future. 
Not put a lot of stock in means… 
A. not believe to be true/reliable 
B. not participate 
C. not think about 
 
2) We got sick during our vacation, which kind of put a damper on our trip. 
Put a damper on something means… 
A. have to change plans 
B. make it less fun 
C. prevent it from happening 
 
3) After I lent my friend money three times and he didn’t pay me back, I put 
my foot down and said I wouldn’t give him anything more. 
Put your foot down means… 
A. get very annoyed 
B. run out of money 
C. take a strong and firm position 
 


4) There’s something strange about that guy, but I can’t put my finger on it. I 
just have a weird feeling about him. 
Can’t put your finger on something means… 
A. can’t identify it specifically 
B. can’t tell anyone about it 
C. can’t tolerate it 
 
5) My professor put in a good word for me with the academic director, and 
helped me get the internship. 
Put in a good word for someone means… 
A. compliment the person on their accomplishments 
B. help the person learn to speak better 
C. say good things about the person to someone in authority 
 
6) When our 15-year-old cat got cancer that couldn’t be cured, we decided to 
put her to sleep. 
Put an animal to sleep means… 
A. keep it in a comfortable place 
B. kill it so that it won’t suffer 
C. take very good care of it 
 
7) I think my son might be cutting classes. I wouldn’t put it past him – he’s 
always been more interested in sports than in schoolwork. 
Wouldn’t put it past someone means… 
A. not agree with their choices 
B. not be surprised by their behavior 
C. not take them very seriously 
 


8) You should spread out your investments among different companies. Don’t 
put all your eggs in one basket. 
Put all your eggs in one basket means… 
A. have only one source of income 
B. focus on just one opinion/perspective 
C. place all your resources in a single area 
 
9) Don’t put the cart before the horse – you haven’t even been accepted to 
the university yet, so there’s no point in planning what courses you’re going to 
take there. 
Put the cart before the horse means… 
A. believe something incorrect 
B. do things in the wrong order 
C. take action immediately 
 
10) The jewelry vendor put one over on me – he sold me a gold necklace, but 
it’s actually just gold-plated. 
Put one over on someone means… 
A. be smarter than the person 
B. deceive the person 
C. do business with the person 
 
 
 
 
 
 


Explanation 
If you “don’t put a lot of stock in” (A) a piece of information, it means you 
don’t believe it to be true or reliable. You are skeptical, you don’t trust that 
information. 
Inside a piano, there is a piece called a “damper” that decreases the sound or 
stops the sound from coming out. And the idiom to “put a damper on” (B) an 
activity is to make it less fun or less enjoyable.  
If you “put your foot down,” (C) you take a strong and firm position on 
something (and you probably won’t change your mind). We usually use this 
expression with a bad situation that has gone on for some time without you 
taking action, but finally you put your foot down and take a firm position. 
If you “can’t put your finger on something,” (A) it means you can’t quite 
identify it specifically. You might have a general feeling, opinion, or 
inclination, but it’s hard to understand or say the specific reasons for it. 
To “put in a good word for someone” (C) means to say good things about 
that person to someone in authority. We often use this for recommending 
someone to a supervisor in order to help that person get a job or another 
opportunity. 
To “put an animal to sleep” (B) or “put an animal down” is when an animal is 
very old or sick, so you agree for the veterinarian to give the animal an 
injection that will kill it without pain. 
If you say that you “wouldn’t put it past someone,” (B) it means you 
wouldn’t be surprised if the person did a certain action (which you usually 
mentioned in the previous sentence). Knowing the person’s behavior or 
personality, you think it’s possible that the person would do such an action. 
If you literally “put all your eggs in one basket” (C) and then you drop the 
basket, all the eggs will break and you’ll lose everything. So this expression 
more generally refers to making everything dependent on one thing, or to put 


all of your resources into one place. People usually warn others NOT to do 
this. 
 
In the past, people traveled in horse-drawn carts – the horse must be ahead of 
the cart in order to pull it. If the cart is in front of the horse, it won’t work. So 
the idiom “put the cart before the horse” (B) means to do things in the 
wrong order – to do step 2 before step 1. 
 
To “put one over on” (B) someone is to deceive, fool, or trick that person.
