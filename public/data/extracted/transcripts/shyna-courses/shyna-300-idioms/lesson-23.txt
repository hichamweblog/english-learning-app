Lesson 23: Idioms with TAKE 
 
Introductory Quiz 
Guess the correct meaning of each idiom from the context. It's OK if you get a lot 
of answers wrong – what’s important is to do your best in trying to figure it out! 
1) The washing machine is broken? Let me take a stab at fixing it before we 
call a repairman. 
Take a stab at something means… 
A. find out how 
B. learn to do it 
C. make an attempt 
 
2) I take issue with your assumption that women don’t work as hard as men. 
That’s a very sexist view. 
Take issue with something means… 
A. oppose or object to it 
B. think it is not important 
C. want to know more about it 
 
3) I’d always taken the ability to walk for granted – until I broke both legs 
and was in a wheelchair for months. 
Take something for granted means… 
A. fail to appreciate it 
B. enjoyed for a long time 
C. believed it was normal 
 


4) I’ve wanted to take dance classes for years, but I was nervous. Well, I finally 
took the plunge and signed up for lessons. 
Take the plunge means… 
A. begin something new and challenging 
B. find an opportunity to do something 
C. stop worrying about something 
 
5) I’m grateful to Fred for taking me under his wing during my first few 
weeks at my new job. 
Take someone under your wing means… 
A. be an inspiration to the person 
B. have high expectations for the person 
C. help and protect the person 
 
6) In the first few months after my son was born, all my hobbies took a back 
seat. 
Take a back seat means… 
A. become less of a priority 
B. become less enjoyable 
C. take up too much time 
 
7) My daughter didn’t take kindly to having to wear a school uniform – she 
said it was ugly. 
Not take kindly to something means… 
A. not react well to it 
B. not think it’s a good idea 
C. refuse to do it 
 


8) I took great pains to write nice, personalized thank-you notes to everyone 
who gave me a wedding gift. 
Take great pains to do something means… 
A. do something you don’t want to 
B. have a serious obligation 
C. make a careful, dedicated effort 
 
9) All this stress is taking its toll – I’m having a hard time sleeping at night. 
Take a toll means… 
A. be excessive 
B. cause harm 
C. cause worry 
 
10) I’ve never been to Switzerland, but I’ll take your word for it that it’s a 
beautiful country. 
Take someone’s word for it means… 
A. believe the person 
B. listen to the person 
C. promise the person 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 


Explanation 
To “take a stab at” (C) something means to make an effort, to try to do it. We 
also have the expressions “take a crack / swing / shot at” which all mean to 
try. 
If you “take exception to” or “take issue with” (A) something, it means you 
disagree and you oppose or object to it. You can take exception to / take issue 
with a belief or an action. 
To “take something for granted” (A) means fail to appreciate its value. We 
often take things for granted when we are accustomed to having them in our 
lives, so we don’t think about them much or take the time to be grateful. But if 
that good thing is removed, then we realize how much we took it for granted 
before. 
“Take the plunge” literally refers to jumping into water. Once you jump, 
there’s no going back – you WILL enter the water. The general expression 
“take the plunge” (A) means to begin something new, especially after you 
have hesitated for some time. We often use this for things that will require 
some courage or a big commitment. 
Birds can keep their babies under their wing to protect them. So if you “take 
someone under your wing,” (C) it means you help and protect a new or 
inexperienced person. This is used when someone with more experience cares 
for and guides someone with less experience, thus helping the less 
experienced person feel more comfortable in the situation. 
 


If one thing “takes a back seat” (A) to something else, it means it is less 
important, because the other thing takes priority. Another expression for this 
is to “put something on the back burner.” 
If you “don’t take kindly to” (A) something, it simply means you don’t like or 
don’t react well to it.  
“Taking great pains” (C) to do something means trying very hard and being 
very careful to do it. It doesn’t mean the thing is painful, it just means you give 
it extra effort and importance. 
The word toll refers to when cars need to pay some money to use a certain 
road or bridge. It’s not pleasant to pay tolls, because it’s an extra cost. More 
generally, when we say something “takes a toll,” (B) it means it causes harm, 
suffering, or damage.  
 
You can use the phrase “I’ll take your word for it” (A) meaning you believe 
what the other person says, even though you don’t have direct experience of 
what they are talking about.
