Lesson 29: Idioms with CALL 
Introductory Quiz 
Guess the correct meaning of each idiom from the context. It's OK if you get a lot 
of answers wrong – what’s important is to do your best in trying to figure it out! 
1) When my father had a heart attack at a young age, it was the wake-up call 
he needed to take better care of himself. 
A wake-up call means... 
A. an event that brings awareness 
B. beginning to look for other options 
C. when you're convinced by other people 
 
2) That was a close call - the driver wasn't paying attention and nearly hit the 
guy crossing the street. 
A close call means... 
A. a dangerous situation that almost happened 
B. a situation someone doesn't know about 
C. a thing that is difficult to see 
 
3) Duty calls - I've gotta pick up my kids from school. Talk to you later! 
Duty calls means... 
A. being very busy 
B. having to leave for an obligation 
C. receiving a phone call 
 
4) We played soccer for an hour but when it started raining hard, we had to 
call it quits. 


Call it quits means... 
A. delay until later 
B. go home early 
C. stop doing something 
 
5) When a mistake was found in the procedure, the results of the experiment 
were called into question. 
Call into question means... 
A. cancel or ignore 
B. be completely wrong 
C. the possibility of doubt appears 
 
6) I'm not sure which of the top two skaters had the better performance; it's a 
tough call. 
A tough call means... 
A. beyond someone's understanding 
B. difficult to evaluate/decide 
C. not pleasant to watch 
 
7) We can all make suggestions for the promotional campaign, but the 
marketing director is ultimately calling the shots. 
Call the shots means... 
A. control and make the decisions 
B. doing the work 
C. organizing the tasks 
 
8) I used to work as an executive assistant, so I was at the president's beck 
and call. 


To be at someone's beck and call means... 
A. available and ready to obey them 
B. doing everything in their place 
C. working very closely with them 
 
9) Anyone else want me to get them something from the cafe? Last call - I'm 
about to leave. 
Last call means... 
A. almost ready 
B. final opportunity 
C. tell what you want 
 
10) You bought a Toyota? Good call. They're very reliable cars. 
Good call means... 
A. approve of a decision 
B. in the same situation 
C. think positively 
 
Explanation 
A wake-up call (A) is an event that brings awareness - it makes you pay 
attention to something you were previously ignoring or didn't think much 
about. Sudden shocking events or tragedies can often serve as a wake-up call. 
A close call (A) is a dangerous situation that almost happened, but didn't 
happen. It's a potential disaster that came close and was barely avoided. 
If you say duty calls, (B) it means you need to stop what you're doing and go 
take care of an obligation. The word duty is another word for work or a task 
that you must do, and the duty is "calling" you away from your current 
activity. 


The expression call it quits (C) simply means to stop doing something. A 
somewhat similar phrase is "call it a day," which means to stop working for 
the day (and often resume the work tomorrow). 
When something is called into question, (C) it means that the possibility of 
doubt appears - so the thing must be reevaluated to see whether it is true or 
false, good or bad. 
The expression a tough call (B) refers to a situation where it is difficult to 
evaluate or decide. It's often used when two things are very similar, or when 
the situation is very complex and it's not easy to know what is right, true, or 
best. 
If someone is calling the shots, (A) it means he/she is making the main 
decisions. This person has the authority to decide what must be done. 
To be at someone's beck and call (A) means to be available and ready to 
obey that person. It implies that you must always be ready to do everything 
that person asks or orders. 
Last call (B) is simply an informal way to alert people to their final 
opportunity or last chance to get or do something. 
Saying something was a good call (A) means you approve of the decision; you 
think it was a good idea or good choice – as in the example of buying a reliable 
car.
