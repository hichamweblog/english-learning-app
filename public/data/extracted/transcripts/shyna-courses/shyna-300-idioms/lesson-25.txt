Lesson 25: Idioms with GO 
 
Introductory Quiz 
Guess the correct meaning of each idiom from the context. It's OK if you get a lot 
of answers wrong – what’s important is to do your best in trying to figure it out! 
1) That singer is really popular among teen girls. Whenever he appears on 
stage, his fans go bonkers. 
Go bonkers means... 
A. act crazily 
B. make a lot of noise 
C. take pictures 
 
2) The author's long-awaited second book went down in flames; critics hated 
it, and almost nobody bought it. 
Go down in flames means... 
A. be of bad quality 
B. damage one's reputation 
C. fail spectacularly 
 
3) If you want to start your own business, you can't do it half-heartedly; you 
need to go for broke. 
Go for broke means... 
A. borrow money 
B. have a good idea 
C. try as hard as possible 
 


4) It's the final week of the presidential campaign, and the candidates are 
going into overdrive in their efforts to gain voters' support. 
Go into overdrive means... 
A. appearing in the media 
B. traveling to many places 
C. work more actively than normal 
 
5) My son was born 6 weeks early. His condition was touch-and-go for a 
while, but now he's healthy and strong. 
Touch-and-go means... 
A. needing medical attention 
B. unknown 
C. unstable and uncertain 
 
6) My sister absolutely loves hiking. She wakes up at sunrise raring to go. 
Raring to go means... 
A. earlier than other people 
B. eager to take action 
C. in excellent physical condition 
 
7) The Australian soccer team played well from the get-go and maintained 
their lead throughout the game. 
From the get-go means... 
A. from another country 
B. from the beginning 
C. with everyone's participation 
 


8) The self-driving car will go down in history as one of the most important 
inventions for everyday people. 
Go down in history means... 
A. be very important and remembered 
B. fail in the long-term 
C. take years to finish 
 
9) My math teacher tutored me every day after school. She went above and 
beyond to help me understand the subject. 
Go above and beyond means... 
A. be very patient 
B. do much more than the minimum 
C. have good communication skills 
 
10) Disease almost always goes hand in hand with poverty. You can't deal 
with one problem without considering the other. 
Go hand in hand means... 
A. be closely connected 
B. be an immediate problem 
C. spread from person to person 
 
 
 
 
 
 


Explanation 
Go bonkers (A) means to act crazily. We have a number of other expressions 
for this: go berserk, go bananas, go nuts. These can be used for when 
someone acts crazy with anger, or acts crazy in general (as in this example 
when the fans are acting crazy with joy and excitement). 
In war, when a ship or plane is hit by a missile, it starts burning and goes 
down in flames. (C) The idiom in general means when something fails in a 
spectacular way. 
To go for broke (C) means to try as hard as possible, to commit 100% of your 
effort to that goal. Don't get go for broke confused with go broke. Go broke 
means to lose all your money. 
Go into overdrive (C) means to work more actively than normal. You can 
imagine a car driving at maximum speed, much faster than it would usually go.  
 
If something is touch-and-go, (C) it means the situation is unstable and 
uncertain. We do not know what the final result would be, and circumstances 
could change at any time. 
When a person is raring to go, (B) it means they are eager and excited to take 
action. This action could be going to a place, or it could be any activity, like 
raring to go on a project or hobby. 
From the get-go (B) is a very informal way to say, "from the beginning." 
Some people pronounce this more like from the git-go. 
If something goes down in history, (A) it means it will be very important and 
remembered for a long time. It will have an impact on history, and people 
studying history in the future will probably learn about it. 


When someone goes above and beyond, (B) it means they do a lot more than 
the minimum or what is required. They make a special extra effort and 
dedicate extra time/energy beyond what would be expected. 
If two things go hand in hand, (A) it means they are closely connected; one 
thing accompanies the other.
