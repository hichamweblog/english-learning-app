Lesson 26: Idioms with COME 
Introductory Quiz 
Guess the correct meaning of each idiom from the context. It's OK if you get a lot 
of answers wrong – what’s important is to do your best in trying to figure it out! 
1) Victor couldn't stand the guilt anymore, and finally came clean about 
cheating on the final exam. 
Come clean means... 
A. be very sorry 
B. improve a situation 
C. tell the truth 
 
2) I have a little flashlight on my keychain; it comes in handy when I'm going 
up steps in the dark. 
Come in handy means... 
A. be useful 
B. hold something 
C. shows the right way 
 
3) Two fans of rival sports teams were swearing at each other in the bar, and 
they almost came to blows. 
Come to blows means... 
A. be expelled from a place 
B. call the police 
C. start hitting each other 
 
4) He was able to deal with most of the other problems calmly, but when his 
son was arrested for drug dealing, then he came unglued. 


Come unglued means... 
A. be extremely disappointed 
B. destroy a relationship 
C. lose emotional control 
 
5) My sister was talking about dropping out of college for a while, but I'm glad 
she finally came to her senses and decided to finish her degree. 
Come to your senses means... 
A. follow someone's advice 
B. start thinking sensibly 
C. want to improve your position in life 
 
6) Our family dog recently died, and I need to help my kids come to grips 
with the loss. 
Come to grips with means... 
A. begin to accept 
B. forget about 
C. have a strong experience 
 
7) The senator came under fire for her history of making discriminatory 
comments about other races. 
Come under fire means... 
A. be criticized intensely 
B. become very unpopular 
C. lose your job 
 
8) When you marry someone, you commit to staying with them come hell or 
high water. 


Come hell or high water means... 
A. forever 
B. regardless of any obstacle 
C. with hope for a better future 
 
9) I know you have very specific plans for your future, but remember that 
when you have kids, their needs will come into play. 
Come into play means... 
A. become a factor 
B. have a positive effect 
C. take first priority 
 
10) You shouldn't treat your employees badly; it might come back to bite 
you one day. 
Come back to bite someone means... 
A. be morally or ethically wrong 
B. cause someone to have a bad reputation in their field 
C. for a past situation to cause problems in the present/future 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 


Explanation 
To come clean (C) means to tell or reveal the truth - often about something 
you did wrong, and/or something you wanted to hide because it's bad or 
embarrassing. 
Come in handy (A) is an informal way to talk about being useful. You can also 
use handy as an adjective - for example, "This oven has a handy self-cleaning 
feature." 
The noun "blow" can mean a hit or an impact, and if two or more people come 
to blows, (C) it means they start fighting and physically hitting each other. 
 
“Glue” is a sticky substance that holds things together and keeps them stable. 
When we say someone comes unglued, (C) it means they lose their emotional 
control and stability, and they start showing extreme emotions and/or 
behaving crazily. This expression is also occasionally used when something 
fails by falling apart into pieces. 
When someone comes to their senses, (B) they start thinking in a sensible 
and reasonable way (often after having silly ideas or plans that were not a 
good idea in the past). 
To come to grips with something (A) means to begin to accept and tolerate 
something (usually something difficult). We can also say come to terms with 
something. 
If someone comes under fire, (A) it means they are being criticized intensely. 
In a battle, if soldiers are "under fire," it means the enemy is shooting guns at 


them. More generally, coming under fire means you are experiencing a 
widespread "attack" of criticism. 
 
Come hell or high water (B) is an informal idiomatic expression that means 
"regardless of any obstacle." In other words, you are determined to do 
something even if terrible things happen in the future - you are still 
committed to doing it or reaching your goal. 
If something comes into play, (A) it means it becomes a factor; it has some 
sort of effect on the situation that must be considered. 
If you treat a puppy badly, it might come back to bite you when it is an adult 
dog. In the same way, something that comes back to bite you (C) is 
something from the past that is now causing you problems or bad 
consequences in the present/future.
