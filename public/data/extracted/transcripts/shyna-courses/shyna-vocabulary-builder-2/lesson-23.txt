Lesson 23: Prefixes 
As you learned in the last lesson, a prefix is placed at the beginning of a word 
to modify its meaning: 
• polite / impolite (not polite) 
• market / supermarket (big market) 
• sleep / oversleep (sleep too much) 
• cultural / multicultural (many cultures) 
• view / preview (view before) 
It’s important to have some notion of these prefixes, since they can make a 
difference between similar-looking words: 
• uninformed = have no information 
• misinformed = have the wrong information 
In today’s lesson, I’m going to teach you twelve prefixes, once again using a 
few words you probably already know, and then adding some new ones.  
1. contra-/counter- = against 
• contradiction = two things that conflict with / go against each other 
• counterattack = return attack against the enemy 
• countercultural = against the dominant culture 
• contraband = items prohibited by law from being imported/exported 
• counterfeit = fake, not real (ex. counterfeit money) 
2. dis- = negation  
• disadvantage = opposite of advantage 
• disapprove = not approve 
• disoriented = not oriented; lost one’s sense of direction 
• do (someone) a disservice = do something that causes harm instead of 
helping 


 
 
- EspressoEnglish.net 
• disconcerting = makes you uneasy, unsettled emotionally, a bit 
uncomfortable 
3. en-/em- bring into the condition of 
• enrage = make angry 
• envision = imagine 
• entrap = make someone trapped, imprisoned 
• enlighten = give spiritual or intellectual insight/knowledge 
• engrossed = completely occupied or absorbed; it is taking all your 
attention; you’re not easily distracted 
• embitter: cause to become bitter 
• embellish: cause to become beautiful; decorate by adding things 
• embark: enter a ship, set out on a journey 
4. hyper- = more than normal 
hypo- = less than normal 
• a hyperactive child has more energy than normal and has difficulty 
sitting still or being quiet 
• hypertension is the more formal name for high blood pressure 
• to hyperventilate means to be breathing much faster and harder than 
normal (often after exercise that was too intense, or due to emotional 
agitation) 
• hypothermia = abnormally low body temperature. People stranded in 
cold climates often get hypothermia as they wait to be rescued 
• hypocrite = a false person who says one thing and does another; is not 
what he/she pretends to be. Ex) Someone who preaches humility, but 
they themselves are very arrogant. 
• hypochondriac = a person who believes themselves to be sick or likely 
to become sick, even though they have few, minor, or no symptoms  
5. in-/im-/il- = not, without 
• inappropriate = not appropriate 


 
 
- EspressoEnglish.net 
• imprecise = not precise 
• illiterate = can’t read 
• impasse = a situation where no progress can be made (ex. in 
negotiations) 
in/im/il can also have the meaning like “en”: to bring about: 
• inject = put something in 
• impregnate = to get someone pregnant 
• impoverished = poor, in poverty 
• illuminate = make light, make luminous 
6. inter- = between/among 
intra- = inside/within 
• interactive = action/relation between people/things 
• international = among various countries 
• interdisciplinary = a study involving various disciplines (subjects) 
• intranet = network within a company/organization 
• intravenous = within the vein – usually called IV 
7. out- = surpassing, exceeding 
• outrun = run faster than someone/something else 
• outperform = perform better than someone/something else 
• outweigh = be more important than something else 
There are a few situations where “out” means “outer” or “outside”: 
• outskirts of the city = the outer/most distant parts from the center 
• outpatient = not requiring an overnight hospital stay 
8. pro- = in support of, put forward 
• pro-immigration = in support of immigration 
(When used for "in support of," it often appears with a hyphen.  
The opposite is anti-) 


 
 
- EspressoEnglish.net 
• prognosis = prediction of a future situation/outcome 
• procrastinate = put tasks forward, in the future 
• proselytize = try to convince someone to join your religion/belief 
9. sub- = lower 
• submarine = vessel that travels under the water 
• substandard = below the standards; not good quality 
• subconscious = below the level of your consciousness/awareness 
• subcontract = contract someone else to do some of the tasks of your 
contract (ex. a company contracts you to design a website, and you 
contract a developer to do part of the programming)  
10. syn-/sym- = together 
• synonyms = two words that mean the same thing 
• symmetrical = a design that is equal/balanced on both sides 
• symphony = a piece of music for an orchestra (instruments playing 
together) 
• symposium = a conference for discussion of a topic 
• synergy = energy from interaction together 
11. trans- = cross 
• translate = cross from one language to another 
• transvestite = someone who dresses in clothing of the opposite gender 
• transgender = someone who identifies as a member of the opposite 
gender 
• transfusion = transfer of blood from one person to another 
• transplant = to move something from one place to another 
12. fore- = before 
• forecast = prediction 
• forerunner = something that came before (records were the 
forerunners of CDs) 


 
 
- EspressoEnglish.net 
• foresight = perception of things before they occur 
• forewarn = warn in advance
