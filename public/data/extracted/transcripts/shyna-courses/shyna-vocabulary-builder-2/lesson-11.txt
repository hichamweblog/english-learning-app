Lesson 11: Books & Writing 
Do you like to read? Today’s lesson is all about describing books: specific 
categories of books as well as vocabulary words for commenting on the 
positive and negative points of the writing. 
Book Genres 
Remember the word “genre” from yesterday’s lesson on movies? It means a 
type or category. Many of the same genres from movies also apply to books, 
like science fiction, thriller, and mystery. Here are a few more specific literary 
genres: 
• A biography is the story of a person’s life. If that person wrote the book 
about themselves, then it’s called an autobiography. 
• A memoir is similar to an autobiography in that the author recounts 
his/her experiences, but it may focus on one specific time period rather 
than the entire lifetime. Memoirs tend to have a more personal and 
emotional tone, while an autobiography tends to be factual and 
chronological. 
• A chronicle is a detailed non-fiction account of historical events, 
focusing on facts without commentary from the author. 
• An anthology is a collection of pieces (like short stories, poems, or 
plays) in a single book. 
• A novel is a long fictional story. 
• A self-help book teaches you how to improve yourself and your life.

• A satire is a work that makes fun of 
human society or an institution, often 
pointing out its flaws through 
exaggeration and humor. Political 
cartoons are examples of satire. 
 
Sometimes a book tells the untold story of 
a particular person or event – meaning a 
story that has never been revealed to the public. If a story is a first-hand 
account, that means it’s written by someone who experienced the situation 
personally. 
Finally, if a book is one of the most popular and many people have bought it, it 
is called a bestseller. 
People in the Writing Industry 
A person who writes books can be called an author or a writer. We also have 
words for writers of more specific types: 
• Someone who writes novels is a novelist.  
• Someone who writes poetry is a poet. 
• Someone who writes plays is a playwright. 
 
The company that produces and 
publishes books is called the 
publisher or publishing house. The 
publisher often has a team of editors 
to help improve the books and 
proofreaders to check for mistakes.

Book Reviews: Positive and Negative 
If a book was exciting and interesting, you can call it a page-turner – like you 
couldn’t stop reading! Other ways to say a book held your attention are to call 
it riveting or compelling and to say, “I couldn’t put it down.” 
You can also comment on the way the book made you feel: 
• A book that awoke your curiosity and interest is intriguing 
• A book that is happy and not too serious is lighthearted 
• A book that deeply emotionally affects you (often in a bittersweet way) 
is poignant 
• A book that is delightful and charming is enchanting 
• A book that is deep (not superficial) is profound 
 
 
How about the writing itself? One word you’ll often see in book reviews is 
prose – that simply means normal writing, not poetry. If the author’s prose is 
vivid, it means it was colorful and lively. Prose that is lyrical means it is 
beautiful to read or listen to. Writing that is succinct is clear and direct, with 
few extra words. If you don’t want to be specific, you can simply say the book 
was masterfully written.

Finally, a few words used for complimenting non-fiction books are: 
• insightful: had a lot of interesting ideas and perspectives 
• comprehensive: covering the topic completely, not missing anything 
• nuanced: expressing subtle differences and details 
• an in-depth analysis: a thorough, well-done study and explanation 
 
Here are some words for commenting on the book’s shortcomings – meaning 
flaws, or places where it wasn’t as good as it could have been. 
If the book had a lot of jargon, it had lots of technical terms that were not easy 
to understand. Describing writing as wordy means it had too many 
unnecessary words, and describing writing as flowery means it had an 
exaggerated number of “beautiful” words.  
Sometimes the author repeats the same ideas or phrases too often – in this 
case, you can describe the writing as repetitive. If the writing is boring and 
lacks variety, then it is monotonous or tedious.  
 
If the plot of a book was complicated and hard to follow, you can say it was 
disjointed. Sometimes, the end of a book is difficult to believe based on what 
happened earlier in the story – in this case, the ending was implausible.

When criticizing a non-fiction book, you can say that the author was biased – 
meaning his/her personal opinions interfere with the presentation of the 
facts. If the author has left out important points, then he has made some 
omissions. And if the author’s points are very general, not specific and not 
clearly expressed, then they are vague. 
Whenever you write a book or movie review, be careful to avoid spoilers – 
telling people who haven’t read the book yet about the surprise events or the 
ending of the story!
