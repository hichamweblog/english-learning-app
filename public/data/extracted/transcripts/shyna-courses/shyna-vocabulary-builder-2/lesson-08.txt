Lesson 8: Socializing 
Specific Types of Social Events 
There are many different kinds of social events with specific names in English. 
Here are a few of the most common. 
When people move into a new house, they can have a housewarming party – 
an informal gathering in which the hosts (the people organizing the party) 
present their new home, and the guests (the people attending the party) give 
gifts of household items. 
When someone is moving away or leaving for a long trip like a semester or 
year abroad, their friends often organize a going-away party or send-off to 
say goodbye and wish them a good trip. 
Before getting married, some men have a bachelor party with their male 
friends to celebrate their “last day of freedom” as a single person. The female 
equivalent is a bachelorette party. Sometimes these parties are held at a 
strip club or bar, and other times they are more innocent. 
 
The word “shower” is used to describe a party whose primary purpose is to 
give gifts. The two most common types are a bridal shower, in which gifts are 
given to a woman who is going to get married, and a baby shower, in which 
gifts for the baby are given to a pregnant woman. These events are usually 
organized by the woman’s sisters or friends.

A general word for an informal social gathering is a “get-together” – that’s a 
casual event without an event schedule, and you don’t need to dress up.  
One type of get-together which is common in the U.S. is a potluck, in which 
each guest brings a dish to share with everyone. 
 
A potluck 
A banquet and a ball are more formal events. Food is served at a banquet, 
and there is dancing at a ball.

A cocktail party is usually held in the early evening, and drinks and light 
refreshments are served. 
 
Some social events are held for a specific purpose – such as networking, 
making social connections for business or career opportunities. A fundraiser 
is an event whose purpose is to gather donations for a cause, charity, or non-
profit organization. 
Finally, a reunion is a gathering of group members who have been separated 
for some time – in the U.S., there are college reunions and high school 
reunions to re-encounter classmates after many years. 
You can read about a few more different types of parties and gatherings here: 
http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party 
One thing you might see on party invitations is RSVP – that means you need to 
tell the host if you plan to attend the party or not.

Describing Social Habits 
Someone who is friendly to others can be described as warm, cordial, or 
welcoming. The opposite – someone who is unfriendly – is cold or 
standoffish. 
 
 
 
A person who loves social situations and gets energy from being with people 
is an extrovert – but a more common way to describe this type of person is to 
say they are outgoing. 
Someone who is outgoing is often a social butterfly – someone who has many 
different friends and goes from person to person and conversation to 
conversation like a butterfly visits different flowers. 
 
One idiom to describe someone who is the center of attention and makes 
parties fun and exciting is to say they are “the life of the party” – and a

“party animal” is someone who likes to go out to parties and clubs as often as 
possible. 
 
The opposite of an extrovert is an introvert – someone who prefers to spend 
time alone or with only a few close friends. More common words to describe 
this type of person are shy and reserved.  
 
Sometimes (though not always) introverts are socially awkward, meaning 
they are uncomfortable and a little bit strange in social interactions. A person 
who is an extreme introvert and who actively avoids the company of others 
can be called a loner. 
When someone who used to be shy begins to be more sociable and outgoing, 
we say the person has come out of his/her shell – making reference to the 
way a turtle pokes its head out of its shell to interact with the world.

Two types of person that no one wants to be are a wet blanket and party 
pooper. A “wet blanket” is someone whose attitude lessens the enjoyment of 
other people around them, or who discourages joy. A “party pooper” is 
someone whose words or actions ruin the fun or excitement for everyone else.
