Lesson 22: Word Roots 
A “root” is the basic linguistic unit of a word, after the prefixes and suffixes are 
removed. Consider the word unhappiness. 
un  
+  
happy     +  ness 
(prefix) 
   (root) 
(suffix) 
Today’s lesson will focus on roots, and the next two lessons will discuss 
common prefixes and suffixes. 
A root may be an entire word, as in the example of happy / unhappiness. 
However, it can also be a word part – as in the example of vis, which means 
“see”: 
• Vision = your ability to see 
• Visual = relating to the sense of sight 
• Invisible = something that cannot be seen 
Many of the word roots in English come from Latin and Greek. Knowing the 
most common roots can give you a clue as to the meaning of new words when 
you come across them. 
Here’s an example. Based on these three words, what do you think the root 
phon means? 
• telephone 
• microphone 
• phonetic 
The root phon means “sound” – a telephone transmits sound, a microphone 
amplifies sound, and “phonetic” refers to the sounds of speech. So when you 
see the word cacophony, you can recognize the root phon and know that it 
has something to do with sound – and maybe guess the rest from the context.

A cacophony is a sound that is harsh and discordant (not pleasant or 
melodious); for example, if there’s a big traffic jam and all the cars are honking 
their horns repeatedly, that’s a cacophony.

means. Then I’ll reveal the answer and teach you a new word that includes 
this root.

ANSWERS 
ARCH = RULER 
• monarch = king or queen 
• hierarchy = order of power 
• archenemy = primary enemy 
The word anarchy refers to an absence of rule or of control, especially a 
political philosophy with no rulers or governments. 
BENE = GOOD 
• benefit = a good result 
• benevolent = showing goodwill 
• benediction = a blessing 
The word benefactor means someone who gives aid – especially financial aid. 
Ex) The donation was made by an anonymous benefactor. 
CHRON = TIME 


 
 
- EspressoEnglish.net 
• chronological = in the order of time 
• chronicle = a record of a time period 
• synchronize = put into the same time/rhythm 
An anachronism is something that seems to exist outside its proper time. Ex) 
In a world of tablet computers and tiny MP3 players, a cassette tape is an 
anachronism. 
CRED = BELIEF 
• incredible = amazing, difficult to believe 
• discredit = show to be false, make people not believe in it 
• credibility = ability to be believed/trusted 
The word credentials means evidence or qualifications that show your 
authority or show your ability to be trusted – that people can believe in you. 
Ex) He was hired because he had excellent credentials for the position. 
DICT = SAY/SPEAK 
• contradict = say something opposite 
• dictate = say or read aloud, or give orders 
• predict = say what will happen in the future 
The final statement of judgment in a legal case is called the verdict. This word 
can be used for any final conclusion or opinion, not only in official legal 
situations. 
JUR/JUS = LAW 
• jury = the group of people who hear the evidence and make decisions 
about a case in a court of law 
• justice = related to fairness and conformity to the law 
• unjust = not fair according to the rules/laws 
When someone lies or presents false evidence in court, that is called perjury – 
it’s a crime. 


 
 
- EspressoEnglish.net 
LOG = STUDY/THOUGHT 
• logic = organized and rational thought/reasoning 
• psychology = the study of the mind 
• analogy = similarity between two concepts 
This is why so many branches of study end in –ology (biology, geology, 
technology)! Here’s a new word with LOG – logistics, meaning the details, 
thought, planning, and organization of the aspects of a complex operation. 
LUM/LUC = LIGHT 
• illuminate = make something light/bright 
• luminous = bright, gives light 
• translucent = a substance that light passes through 
The word lucid can mean clear and easily understood. It is also used for 
somebody who is mentally OK and rational. 
MAL = BAD 
• malnourished = receiving bad nutrition 
• malfunction = not function correctly 
• malevolent = with bad intention 
The word malign means to make bad/evil statements about something. For 
example, a political candidate who can’t refute his opponent’s ideas in logical 
debate may resort to maligning his opponent in the media. 
PATH = FEELING 
• sympathize = feel compassion 
• pathetic = provoking the feeling of pity or scorn 
• empathy = identification with another’s feelings and motives 
Apathy is a condition of having no strong feelings and not caring much. 
People can become apathetic when depressed, when unmotivated, or when 
they simply don’t have much energy. 


 
 
- EspressoEnglish.net 
PORT = CARRY 
• transportation = something that carries you to another place 
• export = carry something out of a country 
• airport = a place where things are carried by air 
If something is portable, that means it can be carried with you. A piano is not 
portable, but a flute is. 
SCRIB/SCRIP = WRITE 
• manuscript = a written book or document 
• inscribe = to mark or engrave words on/in a surface 
• scripture = writings considered holy/religious 
When you write fast and often sloppily (not neatly or clearly, making it 
difficult to read) then you are scribbling. 
VAC = EMPTY 
• vacuum = pulls things by sucking air into an empty space 
• evacuate = get out of an area 
• vacancy = an open/empty place in a hotel 
If someone makes a vacuous comment or has a vacuous expression on their 
face, it means one that is empty of feeling or meaning. 
VOC/VOK = VOICE 
• vocalize = say something with your voice 
• advocate = use your voice to support or promote a cause/idea 
• provoke = bring about action/feeling (“call forth”) 
The word revoke means to “call back,” to remove or cancel something. If you 
drive drunk, your driver’s license will be revoked.
