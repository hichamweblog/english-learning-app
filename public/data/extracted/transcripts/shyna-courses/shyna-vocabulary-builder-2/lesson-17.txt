Lesson 17: Describing the World 
Climate 
Climate is different from weather - the word climate describes the general 
conditions of temperature and precipitation (how much rain/snow overall), 
whereas weather describes the temporary conditions which change every 
day. 
An arid climate is one that is very dry, where there is not enough water for 
much vegetation to grow - typically only cacti or small shrubs. 
 
 
A tropical climate is hot and humid (meaning there's a lot of moisture in the 
air). Some regions with tropical climates are prone to monsoons - seasons of 
very heavy rain. 
 
A temperate climate has moderate temperatures, which often vary by season, 
and mild weather, and an arctic/polar climate is extremely cold.

Vegetation 
The vegetation varies based on the climate. Tropical climates have jungles or 
rain forests, with large trees and great biodiversity. The tops of the trees 
form a canopy, a thick layer that blocks most sunlight. 
 
In more temperate climates, we call an area of dense trees a forest or woods. 
Two types of trees are evergreen trees - trees that keep their leaves during 
all four seasons - and deciduous trees, which completely lose their foliage 
(leaves) during the winter or dry season, then grow it back in the springtime. 
 
 
Evergreen and deciduous trees

A savanna is a more open area, where the trees are scattered. A scrubland 
has mostly bushes and grasses rather than trees, and a prairie has virtually 
no trees, mostly grasses. 
 
 
savanna, scrubland, and prairie 
As you might imagine from the name, a wetland is an area where the ground 
is saturated with water. Finally, there is tundra – an area where very little 
vegetation grows due to cold climate or high altitude.

Geographical Features 
You already know the basics like mountains, rivers, and valleys – so let’s go a 
little further into specific geographical features. 
A group or chain of islands is called an archipelago. The country of Greece 
has an archipelago. When there’s a part of land that’s surrounded on three 
sides by water but still connected to the mainland, that’s a peninsula. This is 
the Arabian peninsula in the Middle East. 
 
Greek archipelago and Arabian peninsula 
The opposite – a section of the ocean that is partly surrounded by land, so that 
the waters become calmer – is called a bay. A large bay is called a gulf – like 
the Gulf of Mexico. 
 


 
 
- EspressoEnglish.net 
In deserts and near beaches, we have sand dunes – hills or mountains of sand 
that can shift with the wind. In the middle of a desert, there can be an isolated 
area of vegetation called an oasis. These often form around springs – sources 
of water coming up from the ground. 
 
 
sand dunes and oasis 
Let’s look at some more mountainous vocabulary. A plateau is a raised area 
with a flat top. It can be small or very large, covering an entire region. When 
you have a rock face that is vertical or near vertical, this is a cliff. Finally, areas 
of flat or gently rolling terrain are called plains.

Using the Land 
Two major ways to use the land are agriculture and mining. 
Plants that are grown for food – rice, corn, fruit, vegetables, etc. are called 
crops. Farmers plant seeds and then harvest (collect) the crops when they 
are ready. 
 
harvesting crops 
It may be necessary to fertilize the soil – add natural or artificial substances 
to provide more nutrients and help the crops grow better. In areas where 
there is little rainfall, irrigation is needed to artificially apply water to the 
soil. To eliminate insects which can eat and damage the crops, pesticides can 
be used to kill them or keep them away from the plants. 
Mining is extracting valuable materials from 
the earth. Drills and explosives are used to 
dig down and create the mines 
underground.  
Mining seeks to find both precious stones 
like diamonds, sapphires, and rubies as well 
as ore – a type of rock that contains 
minerals including metals like iron or

copper. The ore must then be processed to remove and purify the useful 
metal. 
Stone that is used for construction and building materials is usually taken not 
from a mine, but instead from the surface of the earth, from a quarry.
