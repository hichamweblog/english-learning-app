Lesson 18: Newspaper English 
If you’ve ever read the news in English, you may have noticed that there are 
some changes in the vocabulary and grammar of newspaper English – 
especially in the headlines, the titles of the articles.  
Because space is at a premium, articles, prepositions, and auxiliary verbs are 
often omitted from headlines: 
• Man Killed in Accident 
A man was killed in an accident 
• Expert Claims Cell Phones Dangerous 
An expert claims that cell phones are dangerous 
• President Caught in Scandal 
The President was caught in a scandal 
To add action and urgency, verbs are often in the present, even when the 
event was in the past: 
• Local Woman Wins $10M in Lottery 
A local woman has won $10M in the lottery 
• President Visits Asia 
The president visited Asia recently 
For events in the future, headlines often use to + base form of the verb 
instead of “will” or “going to”: 
• President to Visit Asia 
The president will visit Asia in the future 
• Apple to Reveal New iPhone on Friday 
Apple will reveal the new iPhone on Friday 
Finally, headlines use strong, simple words rather than longer or vaguer 
expressions. For example:

There are also some words you already know, but which may be used in new 
ways. For example: 
• back (v.) = support 
Senator Backs Proposed Immigration Reform 
• blow (n.) = disappointment, setback 
Injury a Blow to Athlete’s Olympic Dreams 
• curb (v.) = restrain, limit 
Government to Curb Spending 
• hit (n./v.) = badly affect (OR reach) 
Snowstorm to Hit Northeast 
Temperature Hits Record Low 
• press for (v.) = insist on, demand 
Residents Press for Better Local Schools 


 
 
- EspressoEnglish.net 
• slam (v.) = criticize severely 
Singer Slams Rival’s New Album 
• spark (v.) = cause, produce  
Indie Movie Sparks Debate on Gender Roles 
In headlines, you might also see some completely new or less common words: 
• clash (n./v.) = argument, conflict, fight 
Police Clash with Protesters 
• coup (n.) = revolution, forced change in government 
Angry Citizens Calling for Coup 
• go-ahead (n.) = approval, permission 
Mayor Gives Go-Ahead to Expand Highway 
• hail (v.) = celebrate, acclaim 
World Leaders Hail New Treaty 
• loom (v.) = when something threatening is approaching 
Prospect of Economic Crash Looms 
• mob (n.) = mafia or uncontrolled crowd 
Mob Vandalizes Downtown Stores 
• ordeal (n.) = unpleasant experience 
Kidnapped Child Traumatized by Ordeal  
• soar (v.) = increase dramatically 
Unemployment Soars in July 
• woe (n.) = misfortune, problems 
Public Transportation Woes Anger Residents
