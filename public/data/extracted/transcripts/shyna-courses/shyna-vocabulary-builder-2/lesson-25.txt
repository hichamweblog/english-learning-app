Lesson 25: Suffixes – Part 2 
In this lesson, you’ll learn some patterns among the great number of noun 
suffixes in English. 
Noun Suffixes for People 
Some suffixes referring specifically to people are –ician, -ist, -ee, and  
–ant/-ent. 
-ician refers to “one who practices” a certain activity, usually a profession: 
• musician, technician, politician 
• beautician – someone skilled in giving beauty/cosmetic treatments 
• pediatrician – a doctor specializing in treating children 
The -ist suffix is much more common and can be used for professions, beliefs, 
and activities: 
Professions with –ist: 
• artist, dentist, geologist 
• ophthalmologist/optometrist – doctors specializing in eyes, vision, 
and glasses and contact lenses 
• therapist / physical therapist – professional specializing in treatment 
of emotional or physical problems, helping you get back to normal 
-ist for people with certain beliefs: 
• racist, feminist, pacifist 
• nihilist – extreme skeptic that denies all existence and knowledge 
• activist – someone who acts in support of a certain cause/idea 
-ist for people who do certain activities: 
• guitarist, soloist 
• rapist – performs forced sexual relations on another person

• The police detain a person, who is called the detainee 
• Someone who has had a limb amputated is an amputee 
Medical Suffixes 
Three noun suffixes often used in medicine are –ectomy, –itis, and –phobia. 
 -ectomy refers to the surgical removal of a body part, and the most common 
ones are: 
• hysterectomy: removal of the uterus 
• mastectomy: removal of the breasts 
• appendectomy: removal of the appendix, which is attached to your 
intestine 
• vasectomy: removal of part of the male reproductive system, making 
him unable to have any more children 
-itis refers to inflammation. The words most commonly used in daily life are: 
• arthritis: inflammation of the joints, a typical ailment of elderly people 
• bronchitis: serious respiratory infection 
• gingivitis: inflammation of the gums 
• laryngitis: the medical word for “a sore throat”

• tendonitis: inflammation of a tendon, often caused by repetitive 
motions 
-phobia is more psychological – it means “fear of.” There are hundreds of 
technical terms for various phobias, but some of the most common are: 
• claustrophobia: fear of closed/small spaces 
• homophobia: fear and dislike of homosexuals 
• xenophobia: fear and dislike of strangers/foreigners 
The adjective forms end with –phobic (claustrophobic, homophobic, 
xenophobic).
