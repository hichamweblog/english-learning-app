Lesson 6: Work & Employment 
Daily Work Routines 
Someone who works regular business hours has a nine-to-five job. Other 
people – such as bus drivers, emergency room doctors, waiters, and security 
personnel – work shifts, meaning they work for a certain period and then are 
replaced by another person who takes over the work.  
 
Example of shifts 
Some employers also offer flex-time (a flexible schedule in which the 
employee can decide when to work). 
If you need to take a day off due to health reasons, that’s called taking a sick 
day. And pregnant women can take maternity leave – time off to have a baby. 
If you work outside your home, then you begin the day with your commute – 
this word refers specifically to the trip between your home and your place of 
employment. It can be used as either a noun or a verb. 
Thanks to technology, nowadays some people can telecommute or work 
remotely – meaning they work from home or another place using the phone 
and internet.

A profession which involves physical work is called manual labor. Farmers, 
mechanics, and construction workers perform manual labor.  
 
On the other hand, if you work primarily at a desk then you have a desk job. 
Some people with desk jobs work in small compartments called cubicles, and 
others have their own private rooms, called offices. 
 
cubicles 
People & Hierarchy 
The people you work with are called your colleagues or co-workers. 
Hopefully you have a good rapport with your colleagues – that means a good 
relationship in which you trust, respect, and like each other. (The “T” in 
rapport is silent). 


 
 
- EspressoEnglish.net 
Let’s talk about the company hierarchy – the order of power and 
responsibility. The person directly above you, who is responsible for 
observing your work, is your boss or supervisor – and the people below you 
are your subordinates. 
Some bosses have the annoying habit of micromanaging their subordinates. 
That means monitoring and interfering with every single detail of the 
subordinate’s work, without letting them work independently. 
Many companies do an annual performance review of their staff – an 
evaluation of each employee’s work and results every year. These 
performance reviews are often the basis for giving promotions (increase in 
power/responsibility) and raises (increase in salary). 
Employment Conditions 
Many countries have a minimum wage – the minimum salary that companies 
are required to pay employees. Sometimes, in order to avoid paying the 
minimum wage, companies hire people to work “under the table” – that 
expression means the person is not legally/officially employed. 
Unfortunately, there’s still a lot of inequality in the job market, such as 
discrimination (different treatment of people) on the basis of skin color. In 
some professions, there is a “glass ceiling” – meaning an invisible barrier that 
prevents women and/or minorities from advancing to high positions. 
If the employees feel they are overworked (required to work too much) and 
underpaid (not paid enough), they might go on strike. “Going on strike” 
means they stop working until the company agrees to give them better 
working conditions. In many countries, each profession has an organization 
called a union which supports workers’ rights and negotiates with the 
company leadership during a strike.

workers on strike 
To avoid a strike, employers can provide incentives for good performance and 
make sure that morale – the general feeling/spirit of the group – stays high. 
Some companies offer their staff nice perks (that’s an informal word for 
“benefits”) such as a car or cell phone paid for by the company. 
A job is not only a way to earn a living (get money for daily life), but it can 
also be rewarding (meaning it gives satisfaction). If you have a job that you 
love or you have wanted to do for a long time, and/or which has great 
benefits, that’s called a dream job.
