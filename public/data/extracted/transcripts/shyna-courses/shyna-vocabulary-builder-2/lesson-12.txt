Lesson 12: Cradle to Grave 
Pregnancy & Birth 
The very first moment of pregnancy is conception – when the man’s sperm 
fertilizes the woman’s egg.  
 
The baby then begins growing in the mother’s uterus, which is more 
commonly called her womb – connected to the mother through the umbilical 
cord. 
 
When a couple is having difficulty conceiving, that is called infertility. There 
are a few different treatments, including fertility-enhancing drugs and IVF – in 
vitro fertilization – where the sperm and egg are combined outside the 
woman’s body and then implanted in her uterus. 
A pregnant woman may experience morning sickness and cravings – intense 
desires for particular foods. The nine months of a normal pregnancy are 
divided into three periods of three months each, called trimesters. One of the 
exams commonly performed during pregnancy is an ultrasound.

If a baby is born much earlier than the due date (the date expected), then it is 
premature (premature babies are sometimes called “preemies”).  
 
Any significant problems during the pregnancy are referred to as 
complications, and if the baby dies in the middle of the pregnancy, this is a 
miscarriage – although in everyday English it’s more common to say the 
woman “lost the baby/pregnancy.” In some countries, a woman can have an 
abortion – choose to terminate the pregnancy and not let the baby live. 
The work of giving birth is called labor, and during labor the woman will 
experience strong contractions. To relieve the pain, she can have an epidural 
– that’s an injection in her back. There are two methods of delivering a baby – 
a vaginal birth through the vagina, and a C-section in which the mother’s 
stomach is cut open and the baby is removed.

After the birth, the mother can breastfeed the baby or use formula, which is 
a manufactured mixture of milk and nutrients.

And the place where a newborn baby sleeps is called a cradle or bassinet. 
 
Death 
Let’s jump to the other end of the life cycle – death. After a person dies, 
his/her body is usually called a “body” if being referred to by people who 
know and love the person. The word corpse also means a dead body, but it’s 
more distant, less personal. 
If a corpse is found in the woods, for example, and no one knows how the 
person died, the police will do an autopsy – an investigation to find the cause 
of death. A place where dead bodies are kept and preserved until they are 
identified and arrangements can be made is called a morgue or mortuary.  


 
 
- EspressoEnglish.net 
 
What’s done with the body after a person dies? One option is being buried 
inside a coffin/casket, in a cemetery with a tombstone or headstone to 
mark the spot. The place where someone is buried is called a grave. 
 
 
coffin/casket 
 
 
 
tombstones/headstones 
An alternative is to have the body cremated (the body burned in a very hot 
fire) and have the ashes (the remains after cremation) either scattered 
outdoors or kept by the family in an urn. 
 
 


 
 
- EspressoEnglish.net 
A ceremony to mark someone’s death is called a funeral. At a funeral, a close 
friend or family member may give a eulogy – a speech praising the person 
who has died and sharing some happy memories. An obituary is often 
published in the local newspaper, giving a brief overview of the life of the 
deceased. 
Another funeral tradition in the U.S. is a wake or viewing, which is a time 
when family and friends can look at the body on display in the coffin and pay 
their respects. A memorial service may also be held – this is a ceremony to 
remember the person’s life, but the coffin/casket with the body is not present. 
When dealing with death, it’s important to be sensitive to the feelings of 
people who are grieving or mourning (feeling extremely sad because of a 
loss). A gentler way to say “died” is “passed away,” for example: “I heard your 
grandfather passed away last week. I’m so sorry for your loss.” 
This last phrase is common in spoken English; in written English we can use 
more formal phrases like “My heartfelt condolences to you and your family” 
and “I’d like to express my deepest sympathies for your loss.” (More phrases 
here: http://www.quickcondolence.com/)
