Lesson 29: Metaphors 
A metaphor is an expression that uses a quality of one thing (usually a more 
physical or concrete thing) to represent a less concrete thing or idea. Put more 
simply - metaphors create a "mental picture" that helps illustrate a concept. 
For example, we can say someone is experiencing a roller coaster of 
emotions. This expression uses a physical object - a roller coaster - to 
illustrate the idea that emotions are going up and down very quickly and 
unpredictably. 
 
A lot of phrasal verbs are used metaphorically. Compare these two sentences: 
• The tanks shot down two of the 
enemy aircraft. 
• My boss shot down every single 
one of my suggestions. 
To shoot down an idea or suggestion 
means to reject or discredit it, destroying it in the same way you would shoot 
down an airplane during a war.

Metaphors for People 
• She’s an angel. = She’s very good and kind, or she helped/saved you in a 
bad situation. 
 
• He’s a pig. = He is rude, gross, or greedy. 
 
• She’s a chicken. = She’s a coward, has no courage. 
 
• I’m a night owl. = I like to stay up late; I have lots of energy at night. 


 
 
- EspressoEnglish.net 
 
• My boss is an ogre. = My boss is cruel, monstrous, and demanding. 
 
• Turned out I was just a pawn in his game. = He used and manipulated 
me to gain some greater advantage for himself. 
 
A “pawn” is this piece in a chess game. 
• He has a heart of gold. = He has a kind and generous character. 
• She has a heart of stone. = She has no emotion, no compassion. 
• He was a late bloomer. = He developed his best skills/abilities later 
than “normal.”

When a flower “blooms,” it opens 
• You’re on fire! = You’re especially filled with inspiration and energy. 
 
• You’re on a roll! = You’re having a period of one success after another. 
 
 
Metaphors for Situations 
A dry spell is a long period without success – alluding to a lack of rain making 
it difficult to grow crops. Someone who hasn’t been in a romantic relationship 
for the past three years is going through a dry spell. 


 
 
- EspressoEnglish.net 
If there’s a tidal wave of something, it means there’s a LOT of it coming in. 
Can be good or bad – a tidal wave of donations for a child with cancer, or a 
tidal wave of violent crime. 
 
The literal meaning of the word minefield is an area where there are 
explosives buried underground – but the word is used metaphorically to mean 
a situation that is full of potential dangers. 
By contrast, something that is smooth sailing is easy with no problems 
during the process, in the same way that a ship sailing through smooth (calm) 
waters will have a tranquil voyage. 
 
When sailing, you do not want your boat to hit any rocks in the water – and in 
the English language the concept of “rocks” is often used for difficulty: 


 
 
- EspressoEnglish.net 
• A marriage that is on the rocks is 
having major problems. 
• A rocky relationship is full of 
problems, and a rocky start to 
something means it didn’t start very 
successfully. 
• If you are between a rock and a hard place, it means you have 
unpleasant options and no good way to avoid them. If you have a great 
job in a city you love, but your parents want you to move back to your 
hometown, you are between a rock and a hard place because you will 
either have to give up the job or anger your parents. 
In mythology, a silver bullet is the only thing that can kill a vampire or 
werewolf. In English, the expressions silver bullet and magic bullet refer to a 
quick and instant solution to a problem.  
It’s usually used in the negative form to say there’s no easy solution: There’s 
no magic bullet against cancer; the disease has many different forms and 
each one requires specialized treatment. 
We have three very vivid metaphors to describe a process that begins and 
then keeps going: 
• A domino effect / chain reaction is when one event leads to a 
continuing series of other, similar events. Ex) The collapse of the 
country’s government caused a chain reaction of revolutions 
throughout the entire region. 
 


 
 
- EspressoEnglish.net 
• A snowball effect is when something very small begins a process that 
becomes much larger with time – like a snowball getting bigger as it 
rolls down the side of a mountain. Ex) Being bullied as a child can begin 
a snowball effect of mounting insecurity and self-esteem issues that 
can result in professional and relational problems as an adult. 
 
When you are relieved that you don’t have to worry about something or be 
responsible for it any more, you can say it’s a weight off my shoulders. If you 
are very busy at work and your colleague offers to do part of the project 
originally assigned to you, you could say “Thank you so much. That’s a huge 
weight off my shoulders.” 
Finally, most people like listening to music – so when you say a piece of news 
is “music to my ears,” it means it makes you happy to hear it. If you’re hoping 
to go to the beach and someone tells you it’ll be sunny over the weekend, you 
could say “That’s music to my ears!”
