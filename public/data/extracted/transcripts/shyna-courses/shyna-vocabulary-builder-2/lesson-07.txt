Lesson 7: Issues in Education 
Let’s begin by reviewing the different levels and types of education. 
• Primary education refers to the first 5-7 years of schooling. This 
includes elementary school and middle school. 
 
• Secondary education is what we call high school in the U.S. – it is the 
school attended during adolescence. 
 
• Higher education refers to college and university. This includes 
undergraduate studies (typically four years) and graduate studies 
(Master’s and Doctorate programs). 
Parents can choose to send their children to a public school, which is run by 
the government, or a private school, which is run by a company or 
institution.

One alternative form of education is homeschooling – where the parents 
teach their children at home. And special education is for children with 
disabilities and special needs. 
Classroom Management 
Teachers must deal with a number of difficult issues in the classroom. 
Students may cheat – get the answers in a dishonest way – in order to get 
good grades. Examples of cheating include looking at another student’s test 
and using a cell phone to find answers during an exam. 
Another form of cheating is plagiarism, when a student copies someone else’s 
written work and claims it as his own. (“Plagiarism” is the noun, and 
“plagiarize” is the verb). 
Here are three common punishments given to students for bad behavior in the 
U.S.: 
• If a student gets detention, it means he or she must stay at school after 
the regular hours. 
• If a student is suspended, it means he or she is not permitted to enter 
the school for a few days or weeks. 
• If a student is expelled, it means he or she is officially and permanently 
removed from the school. 
It’s not easy to be a teacher – but being a student also has its own share of 
difficulties. Students face peer pressure – influence from friends and 
classmates to act or think in a certain way. 
The popular kids at school can form a clique – a 
small and exclusive group of friends that often 
views itself as superior. The unpopular kids may be 
bullied – suffering abuse or threats. Bullying can be 
physical, verbal, emotional, or online (called “cyber 
bullying”).

Curriculum & Evaluation Issues 
One controversy in education today is regarding the use of standardized 
testing – tests that are given to all students in a region or country. 
Some people say this is essential for evaluating students and comparing 
school rankings (positions in a list of best to worst) – and others claim the 
use of such tests encourages students to focus on memorization and not 
develop critical thinking skills. 
Another debate involves whether or not fine arts 
(music, theater, dance, painting, etc.) should be 
included in the core curriculum – the essential 
and required set of courses – or whether they 
should be electives (optional courses) or 
extracurricular activities (done outside regular 
school hours). 
Finally, a current concern in the U.S. is regarding 
the incredibly high tuition (the price you pay for 
attending a private school) at many colleges and universities. Students who 
are unable to get scholarships (financial help for paying for studies) often 
take out large loans in order to attend elite schools (those which are 
considered superior) – then graduate with thousands of dollars in debt and 
are unable to find high-paying jobs in their areas. 
This is causing some students to choose different options like 
entrepreneurship (starting your own business) or entering a trade (manual 
work for which you need specialized skills/training, such as being a carpenter 
or mechanic).
