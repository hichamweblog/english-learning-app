Lesson 34: Prepositions after Nouns 
Today we’ll be studying the prepositions that typically come after certain nouns. 
Let’s start with a quiz. I’m going to show you three sentences, and you’ll have to 
choose ONE preposition from the options that can fit in ALL three sentences. Some 
of the prepositions are used more than once, and others are not used. 
Preposition options: at / by / for / from / in / of / on / to / with 
Remember, it’s ONE preposition for ALL THREE sentences! 
1. Treatment helped her overcome her addiction ____ painkillers. 
The hurricane caused a lot of damage ____ our house. 
I'm impressed by her dedication ____ the cause. 
2. There's a great need ____ well-trained teachers. 
I have enormous respect ____ my father. 
The city is overcrowded; there's no room ____ new residents. 
3. She demonstrates little awareness ____ how her actions might affect others. 
He's trying to kick the habit ____ biting his nails. 
The cause ____ the miscommunication turned out to be a typo in an e-mail. 
4. He has a complicated relationship ____ his mother. 
His dealings ____ the underworld brought down his political career. 
My conversation ____ the consultant made me reconsider a few things. 
5. There's been a decrease ____ unemployment lately. 
The delay ____ notifying customers was inexcusable. 
The rise ____ the number of college graduates is very encouraging. 
6. That company has a reputation ____ mistreating its interns. 
We must understand the reasons ____ the problem before trying to fix it. 
Wow, you really have a talent ____ creative work. 
7. It was a long time ago, so my memory ____ the event could be wrong. 
His fear ____ rejection prevents him from approaching girls. 
You have a solid grasp ____ the basic concepts. 


8. I'm writing in reference ____ the ad I saw in the paper. 
This represents a threat ____ national security. 
The library provides access ____ archives of scientific journals. 
 
Special Cases 
In many cases, the noun is followed by the same preposition as its related verb form 
or adjective form: 
• She’s interested in science. 
(interested = adjective) 
• Her interest in science led her to become a biologist. 
(interest = noun) 
• How did he react to the news? 
(react = verb) 
• I was surprised by his reaction to the news. 
(reaction = noun) 
However, there are some cases where the verb form does not need a preposition 
after it, but the noun form does. For example, in yesterday’s lesson you learned not 
to use the word “about” after “discuss.” Yet when we use the noun form, 
“discussion,” then we do use “about”: 
• We discussed politics for an hour. 
(verb) 
• We had an hour-long discussion about politics. 
(noun) 
Here are a few more examples: 
• The government has banned smoking in public places. 
There’s now a ban on smoking in public places. 
• He admires Michael Jordan. 
His admiration for Michael Jordan inspired him to play basketball. 
• You’ve really improved your writing. 
I’ve seen a great improvement in your writing. 


• His religion influences his actions. 
He has had a major influence on his community. 
• The talk show host interviewed the novelist. 
That interview with the novelist was fascinating. 
• You can’t control other people. 
I had no control over the situation. 
• That village lacks access to clean water. 
The lack of access to clean water causes disease. 
Finally, we have a couple words that are followed by different prepositions 
depending on whether they are nouns or adjectives: 
• I’m very fond of chocolate. 
• I’ve gained 10 pounds thanks to my fondness for chocolate! 
• He’s proud of his work. 
• He takes pride in his work. 
 
Infinitive or ING after Nouns 
In Lessons 11 and 12, we studied whether to use the infinitive or ING form after 
certain verbs – now let’s consider nouns. See if you can guess the correct form in 
these ten sentences: 
1. The soldier was punished for his failure of following / to follow orders. 
2. We made several attempts of contacting / to contact you. 
3. I was frustrated by my inability of hiding / to hide my emotions. 
4. When you invest in stocks, there's always the risk of losing / to lose your 
money. 
5. You'll need to ask for permission of taking / to take a day off. 
6. She's paralyzed by her fear of making / to make the wrong decision. 
7. We’re exploring the possibility of forming / to form a partnership. 
8. He's not very experienced, but he demonstrates a strong willingness of 
learning / to learn. 
9. The probability of being / to be struck by lightning is very small. 


10. I'm intrigued by the prospect of running / to run my own business. 
Nouns often followed by of + ING include:  
• habit, effect, fear, likelihood, possibility, probability, prospect, and risk 
Nouns often followed by the infinitive include:  
• ability/inability, attempt, decision, desire, determination, failure, 
permission, proposal, and willingness/unwillingness
