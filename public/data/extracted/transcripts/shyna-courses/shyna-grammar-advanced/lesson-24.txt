Lesson 24:  
Reflexive and Emphatic Pronouns 
In today’s lesson, you’ll learn how to use words like myself, herself, themselves, 
etc. These words can be used as reflexive pronouns or emphatic pronouns.  
Reflexive Pronouns 
We use reflexive pronouns when the subject and object of the action are the 
same: 
• He saw me. 
He = subject / me = object 
• I saw me myself in the mirror. 
I am both the subject and the object 
• Mary told George not to worry. 
Mary = subject / George = object 
• Mary told her herself not to worry. 
Mary is both the subject and the object. 
• They bought their kids some toys. 
They = subject / their kids = object 
• They bought themselves some new clothes. 
They purchased the clothes for their own use

Differently from some other languages, we don't usually use -self with the verbs 
wash, shave, or dress: 
• I washed my face / my hands. / I took a shower. 
I washed myself. 
• He shaves himself every morning. 
• She got dressed. 
She dressed herself. 
Sometimes we do say “dress oneself” when talking about a child who is learning this 
basic skill, or someone who has difficulty: 
• After his injury, he wasn’t able to dress himself without help. 
• My daughter managed to dress herself this morning. 
Here are some verbs that are often used with reflexive pronouns: 
• amuse yourself  
= provide your own entertainment 
• enjoy yourself  
= have a good time 
• content yourself with  
= be satisfied with something, even if it is not exactly what you wanted or 
expected. Ex) I wanted to sell my bike for $200, but I had to content myself with 
$150. 
• behave yourself  
= behave well 


• cut/hurt/kill yourself 
• introduce yourself 
= present yourself to another person 
• teach yourself  
= when you are your own teacher 
• tell yourself / talk to yourself / think to yourself 
If you tell yourself something, it can be speaking aloud or simply thinking it 
If you talk to yourself, it means you are speaking aloud  
If you think to yourself, it means you have thoughts without sharing them with 
anyone 
• help yourself 
If you tell someone “help yourself” to some food for example, you are telling 
them to take as much as they want.  
• get yourself / buy yourself  
To “get yourself” something is to get something for yourself, and to “buy 
yourself” something is to buy something for your own use. 
• find yourself = suddenly realize you are in a certain situation. Ex) I spent more 
and more and one day I found myself with nothing in my bank account. 
• see yourself = your own opinion of yourself.  
Ex) He always sees himself as the victim. 
 
Here are some examples of “self” words being used with other prepositions: 
• You shouldn't just follow others' opinions, you should think for yourself. 
• We took one magazine for our son and another for ourselves. 
• I wrapped the blanket around myself. 
• The politicians were talking amongst themselves. 
• He’s so arrogant; he’s always talking about himself. 
• She doesn’t care about anybody but herself. 
Do not use the “self” words when somebody brings/takes something with them: 
• It’s pretty dark. I’ll take a flashlight with myself me. 
• Margaret is coming over tonight; I’m not sure if she’s bringing her kids with 
herself her.

Emphatic Pronouns 
These “self” words can also be used to emphasize the importance of that specific 
person doing the action, for example: 
• The President himself gave me the award. 
(it was the President, and not another person) 
• My daughter baked these cookies herself! 
• Give me the letter - I'll deliver it myself. 
• The teachers themselves are great, but the school administrators are 
terrible. (specifying the teachers and distinguishing them from the 
administrators) 
When we use a “self” pronoun for emphasis, we often place it either at the end of the 
phrase (more common), or immediately after the subject: 
• The President gave me the award himself. 
• The President himself gave me the award. 
If we include the word “by” before the “self” word, that changes the meaning – it 
means alone or without any help: 
• I walked home by myself. 
• He's shy. He's just standing there in the corner all by himself. 
• You’re not strong enough to move the refrigerator by yourself. 
 


Put it into practice!

• When was the last time you spent time by yourself?

Summary 
• We use reflexive pronouns when the subject and object of the action are the 
same person. Ex) I saw myself in the mirror. 
• We don't usually use -self words with wash, shave, or dress, or with 
bring/take something with (someone). 
• These “self” words can also be used to emphasize the importance of that 
specific person doing the action. Ex) The President himself gave me the 
award. 
• If we include the word “by” before the “self” word, that changes the meaning – 
it means alone or without any help. Ex) I walked home by myself.
