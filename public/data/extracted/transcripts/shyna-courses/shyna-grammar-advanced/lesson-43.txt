Lesson 43: Reported Speech (Part 2) 
In yesterday’s lesson, you learned that in reported speech, the main verb 
“backshifts” – changes to a different tense further in the past. Today you’ll learn 
some exceptions to that rule, as well as a few other changes that may need to be 
made when reporting speech. 
No Backshift 
There are three cases in which you don’t need to backshift in reported speech. 
1) When talking about a general truth: 
• Teacher: “The earth revolves around the sun.” 
The teacher explained that the earth revolves around the sun. 
• Kevin: “The capital of the U.S. is Washington D.C.” 
Kevin said that the capital of the U.S. is Washington D.C. 
• Martha: “William Shakespeare died in the year 1616.” 
Martha pointed out that Shakespeare died in the year 1616. 
2) When talking about a situation that still continues to the present: 
In this case, the backshift is optional: 
• Linda: “I work in a bank.” 
Linda said she works in a bank. 
Linda said she worked in a bank. 
If Linda continues to work in a bank in the present, we can say either “works” or 
“worked” in the reported speech – both are correct. 
Here’s another example: 
• Mary: “My brother is taking English classes twice a week.” 
• Mary said her brother is / was taking English classes twice a week. 
If her brother continues to take English classes in the present, we can use “is” or 
“was” in the reported speech – both are correct. 
Now, if Mary’s brother has stopped attending English classes in the time since she 
made that statement, then we must backshift and add a phrase indicating that the 
situation existed only at the time that Mary made the statement in the past:  


• At the time, Mary said her brother was taking English classes twice a week.  
 
3) When reporting future statements that are still in the future: 
When we report on future statements, we must consider whether the event has now 
already passed, or whether it is still in the future. If it is still in the future, then the 
backshift is optional, but it is probably more common to just keep the verb the same:

he gave it to me yesterday. 
 
 
Meredith: “I’m going on a date tonight.” 
 
If the date is still in the future: 
Meredith said she’s / she was going on 
a date tonight. 
If the date is now in the past: 
Meredith said she was going on a date 
that night. 
 
 
Changing Place & Time 
Sometimes we need to change other words in the reported speech as well, 
depending on place and time differences from the original statement. For example, 
let’s say Sally lives in Toronto, and her brother is visiting her: 
Direct 
Reported 
Sally: “My brother is here.” 
If we are currently in Toronto, we can 
say:  Sally said her brother is here. 
If we are NOT currently in Toronto, we 
must say: Sally said her brother is there. 
Sally said her brother is in Toronto. 


If we make the reported statement in the same place as the original statement, then 
there is no need to change “here” to “there” – but if we are in a different place, then 
we need to use “there” or else say the name of the place specifically: “in Toronto.” 
Here’s another example: 
Direct 
Reported 
Pedro (in a restaurant):  
“They have great salads here.” 
If we are in the same restaurant:  
Pedro said they have great salads here. 
If we are NOT in that restaurant:  
Pedro said they have great salads there. 
Pedro said they have great salads at the 
Olive Garden. 
 
A similar thing happens with “this” and “these,” because they are words used for 
something immediately visible or present with the speaker at that time. When 
reporting the speech, we need to specify the object: 
Direct 
Reported 
Sandra (looking at a dress in a store):  
“I love this dress!” 
Sandra said she loved the blue dress 
she saw in H&M. 
Jim (at the market):  
“These bananas are not yet ripe.” 
Jim said the bananas at the market 
were not yet ripe. 
 
Changes are also needed when the original statement contains a word related to 
time. For example, imagine that on Tuesday, Karen says, “We went to the zoo 
yesterday.” At that time, “yesterday” was Monday. 
However, if you’re speaking on Thursday, you can’t say “Karen said they’d gone to 
the zoo yesterday” – because on Thursday, “yesterday” = Wednesday.  
So we change “yesterday” to “the day before” or “the previous day.” Alternatively, 
we can specify the day: “Karen said they’d gone to the zoo on Monday.”

Other words to use besides “said” 
Finally, remember that there are many other words you can use instead of “said” 
when reporting speech, such as mentioned, pointed out, told (someone), replied, 
responded, declared, stated, explained, added, and others. 
 
Summary 
• When talking about a general truth, no backshift is necessary: The teacher 
explained that the earth revolves around the sun. 
• When talking about situations that still exist, the backshift is optional, but it is 
often not used: Linda said she works/worked in a bank. 


• If the situation does NOT still exist, we must backshift and add a time phrase 
to make it clear that the situation was in the past, but not now. 
• When reporting future speech, the backshift is not necessary if the event is 
still in the future: He said he’ll lend me the money by Friday. 
• We may also need to modify place and time words like here, this, these, now, 
today, tonight, last night, ago, etc. 
• There are many synonyms for the word "said," which you can use when 
reporting speech.

Writing Task 
Describe a speech, an interview, or a scene from a movie that you remember well. 
Use reported speech to tell what each person said. Try to use at least a few of the 
alternatives to the word “said.” 
If you can’t remember a particular speech/interview/scene well enough, then look 
for a new one! You can go to YouTube and search for an interview with a well-
known person (example: “Steve Jobs interview”) – watch the video, then describe 
the person’s words in your writing task using reported speech.  
Ex) “When I hire somebody really senior, competence is the ante. They have to 
be really smart. But the real issue for me is, are they going to fall in love with 
Apple?” 
Steve Jobs said that the people he hired had to be really smart. He also 
considered whether or not they would fall in love with Apple.
